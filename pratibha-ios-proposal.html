Before proposing anything new, it's worth being clear about what already exists — because the content and the engine are the hard part, and they're both done.

The scholarship is genuinely uncommon — present-tense translations, commentary that names the counterintuitive move, structural (not thematic) cross-tradition resonances. On the web it reads like a reference site. On a phone, held daily, it can become something closer to a practice.

The one decision everything hangs on

Stop shipping six tabs. Ship one rhythm.

The web app is organized around content operations — browse, random, chat, learn, journal. That's a librarian's mental model. A phone people keep needs a practitioner's: a short daily loop they return to, with depth underneath when they want it.

iOS — a daily loop

Arrive→ Sit→ Study→ Reflect

Four tabs — Today · Library · Paths · Ask. Journal isn't a destination; it's woven into every passage and every path gate. The app opens where you left off.

Random & Daily collapse into "Today"

"Random" is a feature, not a place. It becomes a gesture inside Today — pull to draw a fresh passage. Daily, the path you're mid-way through, and your last reflection all live on one arrival screen.

Journal stops being lonely

An empty journal tab is a guilt machine. Instead, reflection is prompted in context — at the close of a passage, at each path gate — and those entries surface back in Today as "return to this."

The visual thesis

Liquid Glass is Pratibhā

Pratibhā means the flash of recognition — the light by which consciousness knows itself. iOS 26's Liquid Glass is a material where light refracts through translucent layers. These are the same metaphor. We don't decorate the OS aesthetic; we let it carry the meaning. The palette is drawn from the actual pigments of the manuscript tradition — gold leaf, vermillion sindoor, lapis ultramarine, lamp-black ink.

The durable technical model

Reading is offline. Only the conversation needs a server.

The correct split isn't "wrap the web app." Reading is the heart of this app and should be instant, offline, and private. The corpus is tiny; the intelligence is heavy. So we cleave along that line.

On device · ships in the binary
  • All 563 passages — the canonical YAML is only a few MB. Bundle it. Library, Today, and every reading works with no network, no latency, on a plane.
  • Journal & progress — private by default, in SwiftData; optional iCloud sync so it follows the person, not a server.
  • The three guided paths — the teaching text is authored content; it ships too.
  • Full-text + on-device semantic search — the embedding bundle can drive local vector search for Library, so even discovery is offline.
Server · your existing FastAPI stays
  • Ask / RAG chat — retrieval over pgvector + LLM generation. This genuinely needs the backend and a key; keep it exactly as built.
  • Comparative "Two Voices" — already implemented server-side; the client just renders it well.
  • Corpus updates — new passages sync down; the app degrades gracefully to its bundled copy when offline.

The alternative — a thin client that fetches everything, or a WKWebView shell — is the cheap path and I'd advise against leading with it. It makes reading depend on a server that only Ask actually needs, and no amount of styling makes a webview feel like iOS 26. The hybrid above costs a little more up front and is the version that ages well.

Keeping the lights on, honestly

A quiet subscription, matched to the subject

A contemplative app shouldn't use dark patterns — it would contradict its own content. But the LLM calls cost real money, so the model should be clean and fair.

Free, forever

The full 563-passage library, daily passage, reading every layer, and a private journal. The scholarship is a gift; keep it open.

Pratibhā+ · subscription

Unlimited Ask and Two Voices, all guided paths, and iCloud journal sync. The paid tier is the intelligence, which is what actually costs.

The rhythm, not the guilt

A gentle "sit" streak and one considered daily notification — never manipulative loops. Retention through genuine return, per the contemplative frame.

Where I need your call

Four open decisions

Everything above is my curated default. These are the forks where your intent should steer, not mine.

01Name & positioning

Keep the scholarly "Pratibhā," or lead with a warmer consumer name and let Pratibhā be the wordmark? The former signals depth; the latter widens the top of the funnel.

My lean: keep Pratibhā — the name is the concept.

02Audience

Serious practitioners & students (depth-first), or curious newcomers (guidance-first)? It changes how much the app teaches before it shows.

My lean: newcomer-friendly surface, scholar-grade underneath.

03Architecture spend

The offline-first hybrid I recommend, or a faster thin-client v1 to validate demand before investing in on-device search?

My lean: hybrid — it's the version that lasts.

04Scope of v1

Ship Phase 1 (the Reader) to the App Store on its own as a statement, or hold until Ask is wired in Phase 2?

My lean: ship the Reader first. It's already a complete, lovely thing.

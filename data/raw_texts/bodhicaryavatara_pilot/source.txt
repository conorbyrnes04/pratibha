--- Chapter VIII — The Perfect Contemplation ---

BCA_08_SOLITUDE (Barnett, ch. VIII, opening):
"When thus vigour has been nurtured, it is well to fix the thought in concentred effort;
the man of wandering mind lies between the fangs of the Passions. It cannot wander if body
and thought be in solitude; so it is well to forsake the world and put away vain
imaginations. ... Passion is overcome only by him who has won through stillness of spirit
the perfect vision. Knowing this, I must first seek for stillness; it comes through the
contentment that is regardless of the world."

BCA_08_TREES (Barnett, ch. VIII):
"Trees are not disdainful, and ask for no toilsome wooing; fain would I consort with those
sweet companions! Fain would I dwell in some deserted sanctuary, beneath a tree or in caves,
that I might walk without heed, looking never behind! Fain would I abide in nature's own
spacious and lordless lands, a homeless wanderer free of will ... Alone man is born, alone
he dies; no other has a share in his sorrows."

BCA_08_EQUAL (Barnett, ch. VIII — equalizing self and other):
"First he will diligently foster the thought that his fellow-creatures are the same as
himself. 'All have the same sorrows, the same joys as I, and I must guard them like myself.'
... 'I must destroy the pain of another as though it were my own, because it is a pain; I
must show kindness to others, for they are creatures as I am myself.'"

BCA_08_EXCHANGE (Barnett, ch. VIII — exchange of self and other):
"By constant use the idea of an 'I' attaches itself to foreign drops of seed and blood,
although the thing exists not. Then why should I not conceive my fellow's body as my own
self? ... I will cease to live as self, and will take as my self my fellow-creatures. We
love our hands and other limbs, as members of the body; then why not love other living
beings, as members of the universe?"

BCA_08_BODY (Barnett, ch. VIII — non-clinging to the body):
"The body is a motionless thing stirred by something without, and ending in ashes, a
loathsome frame of foulness; why do I cling to it? ... It feels no love when I guard it, no
hate when vultures devour it; then why do I love it? ... So I have surrendered my body
indifferently for the weal of the world; it is but as an instrument of work that I still
bear it."

--- Chapter IX — The Perfect Knowledge ---

BCA_09_TWOTRUTHS (Barnett, ch. IX, opening):
"All this equipment the Sage has ordained for the sake of wisdom; so he that seeks to still
sorrow must get him wisdom. We deem that there are two verities, the Veiled Truth and the
Transcendent Reality. The Reality is beyond the range of the understanding; the
understanding is called Veiled Truth."

BCA_09_DREAM (Barnett, ch. IX — emptiness of persons):
"Since then the forms of being are empty, what can be gained, and what lost? who can be
honoured or despised, and by whom? Whence should come joy or sorrow? What is sweet, what
bitter? ... If thou considerest the world of living things, who shall die therein? who
shall be born, who is born? who is a kinsman and who a friend, and to whom? Life's courses,
if thou considerest them, are like dreams and as the plantain's branches."

BCA_09_COMPASSION (Barnett, ch. IX — union of emptiness and compassion):
"Would that my fellow-creatures should understand that all is as the void! ... Then when
will the day come when I may bring peace to them that are tortured in the fire of sorrow by
my ministrations of sweetness ... and when I may reverently declare to the souls who
imagine a real world that all is void, and righteousness is gathered by looking beyond the
Veiled Truth?"

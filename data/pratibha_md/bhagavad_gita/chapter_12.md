## Which Yoga Knows Best?
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 12.1–12.3

Arjuna said: Those devotees who, ever joined, worship You, and those who worship the imperishable unmanifest — which of them are the best knowers of yoga?

The Blessed Lord said: Those who fix the mind on Me, ever joined, worship Me, endowed with supreme faith — they are held by Me to be the most joined.

But those who worship the imperishable, the indefinable, the unmanifest, the all-pervading, unthinkable, the immovable, the constant—

---

### Devanagari
अर्जुन उवाचएवं सततयुक्ता ये भक्तास्त्वां पर्युपासते।येचाप्यक्षरमव्यक्तं तेषां के योगवित्तमाः

श्री भगवानुवाचमय्यावेश्य मनो ये मां नित्ययुक्ता उपासते।श्रद्धया परयोपेतास्ते मे युक्ततमा मताः

ये त्वक्षरमनिर्देश्यमव्यक्तं पर्युपासते।सर्वत्रगमचिन्त्यं च कूटस्थमचलं ध्रुवम्

### IAST
arjuna uvācaevaṃ satatayuktā ye bhaktāstvāṃ paryupāsate|yecāpyakṣaramavyaktaṃ teṣāṃ ke yogavittamāḥ

śrī bhagavānuvācamayyāveśya mano ye māṃ nityayuktā upāsate|śraddhayā parayopetāste me yuktatamā matāḥ

ye tvakṣaramanirdeśyamavyaktaṃ paryupāsate|sarvatragamacintyaṃ ca kūṭasthamacalaṃ dhruvam

### Pratibha Translation
Arjuna said: Those devotees ever joined who worship You, and those who worship the imperishable unmanifest [akṣara avyakta] — which of them are the best knowers of yoga?

The Blessed Lord said: Those who enter the mind into Me, ever joined, worship Me, endowed with the highest faith [śraddhā] — they are held by Me to be the most yoked.

But those who worship the imperishable, indefinable, unmanifest, all-pervading, unthinkable, the peak-still [kūṭastha], unmoving, firm—

### Pratibha Commentary
Arjuna's question is comparative without being cynical: personal devotion versus unmanifest Absolute — who knows yoga best? Kṛṣṇa's contested answer ranks without annihilating the second path: those who fix mind on Me with supreme faith are yuktatama — most joined. The unmanifest worshippers are not dismissed; they are introduced with a "but" that will continue into difficulty and eventual arrival. Existentially this refuses both anti-intellectual bhakti-chauvinism and cool contempt for devotion. The criterion of "best" is not aesthetic preference; it is degree of joining under faith. Faith here is not soft belief but the supreme trust that allows mind to enter the Lord. The chapter will soon admit the unmanifest path reaches the same Me — at greater cost for the embodied. Ranking is pedagogical mercy: start where joining is most possible. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**yuktatama (युक्ततम)** — most yoked/joined -> Kṛṣṇa's evaluation of personal devotees with supreme faith -> "best yogi" as status contest misses joining-intensity.

**akṣara avyakta (अक्षरमव्यक्त)** — imperishable unmanifest -> the alternative worship-object in Arjuna's question -> "formless God" is approximate but loses technical pair with personal worship.

**śraddhā (श्रद्धा)** — faith/trust -> supreme faith qualifying the most joined -> "belief" as opinion is too thin.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Cloud of Unknowing vs. affective devotion debates:** Both traditions argue relative excellence of apophatic vs personal love paths.
*Divergence:* Medieval Christian framing differs; Gītā ranks inside one Lord's first-person pedagogy.

**Plotinus' ascent vs theurgic devotion:** Structural tension between formless One and personal cult.
*Divergence:* Plotinus subordinates cult; Gītā elevates personal joining as yuktatama while still validating unmanifest worship.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Ask honestly which path you actually practice when tired: personal address, or abstract unmanifest. Practice that path today with supreme faith rather than hybrid vagueness.

## Harder Path, Same Arrival — Softened by Offering
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 12.4–12.6

— restraining the multitude of senses, equal-minded everywhere, rejoicing in the welfare of all beings — they also reach Me.

The difficulty is greater for those whose minds are attached to the unmanifest; for the unmanifest goal is reached with suffering by the embodied.

But those who renounce all actions in Me, intent on Me, meditating on Me with undivided yoga, worship Me—

---

### Devanagari
संनियम्येन्द्रियग्रामं सर्वत्र समबुद्धयः।ते प्राप्नुवन्ति मामेव सर्वभूतहिते रताः

क्लेशोऽधिकतरस्तेषामव्यक्तासक्तचेतसाम्।

अव्यक्ता हि गतिर्दुःखं देहवद्भिरवाप्यते

ये तु सर्वाणि कर्माणि मयि संन्यस्य मत्पराः।अनन्येनैव योगेन मां ध्यायन्त उपासते

### IAST
saṃniyamyendriyagrāmaṃ sarvatra samabuddhayaḥ|te prāpnuvanti māmeva sarvabhūtahite ratāḥ

kleśo'dhikatarasteṣāmavyaktāsaktacetasām|

avyaktā hi gatirduḥkhaṃ dehavadbhiravāpyate

ye tu sarvāṇi karmāṇi mayi saṃnyasya matparāḥ|ananyenaiva yogena māṃ dhyāyanta upāsate

### Pratibha Translation
Having restrained the sense-host, equal-minded everywhere, delighting in the good of all beings — they also reach Me alone.

Greater is the trouble for those whose awareness clings to the unmanifest; for the unmanifest path is reached painfully by the embodied.

But those who renounce all actions in Me, intent on Me as supreme, meditating on Me with undivided yoga, worship—

### Pratibha Commentary
The unmanifest path is affirmed: sense-restraint, sameness everywhere, dedication to all beings' welfare — and they reach Me. The contested pastoral claim follows: for the embodied, avyakta-attachment brings greater kleśa. Embodiment makes formless goal hard; this is anthropology, not insult. Then the alternative is named: renounce actions in Me, be mat-para, meditate with ananya yoga. Existentially this legitimizes needing form, relation, and offering without calling it inferior metaphysics. If you are embodied and find pure unmanifest practice grinding you down, the text predicted that — and offered a path of action-renunciation into the Lord. Difficulty is not always a sign of depth; sometimes it is a sign of wrong tool for this body. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**kleśa (क्लेश)** — affliction/trouble -> greater for unmanifest-attached embodied minds -> "stress" understates spiritual-structural difficulty.

**ananya yoga (अनन्य योग)** — undivided yoga -> meditation-worship of Kṛṣṇa after renouncing actions in Him -> "exclusive focus" is close but misses yoga as joining.

**sarva-bhūta-hita (सर्वभूतहित)** — welfare of all beings -> mark of unmanifest worshippers who still reach Me -> "humanitarianism" secularizes a yogic criterion.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Madhyamaka emptiness practice vs deity yoga:** Both traditions debate formless vs form methods.
*Divergence:* Buddhist emptiness is not the Gītā's avyakta-as-path-to-personal-Me.

**Jewish aniconism vs temple presence:** Structural tension of imageless vs present God.
*Divergence:* Biblical law-frame differs; Gītā allows both paths with cost-accounting for the embodied.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
If your practice feels like harsh grinding toward the formless, try one session of offering today's actions into the Lord with undivided attention — compare the kleśa honestly.

## I Lift You — Fix Mind and Intellect
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 12.7–12.9

For them I am the swift uplifter from the ocean of death-saṃsāra, O Pārtha, for those whose awareness has entered Me.

Fix the mind on Me alone; enter the intellect into Me; you will dwell in Me alone hereafter — no doubt.

If you cannot steadily fix awareness on Me, then seek to reach Me by practice-yoga, O Winner of Wealth.

---

### Devanagari
तेषामहं समुद्धर्ता मृत्युसंसारसागरात्।भवामि नचिरात्पार्थ मय्यावेशितचेतसाम्

मय्येव मन आधत्स्व मयि बुद्धिं निवेशय।निवसिष्यसि मय्येव अत ऊर्ध्वं न संशयः

अथ चित्तं समाधातुं न शक्नोषि मयि स्थिरम्।अभ्यासयोगेन ततो मामिच्छाप्तुं धनञ्जय

### IAST
teṣāmahaṃ samuddhartā mṛtyusaṃsārasāgarāt|bhavāmi nacirātpārtha mayyāveśitacetasām

mayyeva mana ādhatsva mayi buddhiṃ niveśaya|nivasiṣyasi mayyeva ata ūrdhvaṃ na saṃśayaḥ

atha cittaṃ samādhātuṃ na śaknoṣi mayi sthiram|abhyāsayogena tato māmicchāptuṃ dhanañjaya

### Pratibha Translation
For them I become the uplifter [samuddhartṛ] from the death-saṃsāra ocean before long, O Pārtha — for those whose awareness is entered into Me.

Fix the mind on Me alone; settle the intellect in Me; thereafter you will dwell in Me alone — of this there is no doubt.

If you are unable to fix awareness steadily on Me, then seek to obtain Me by practice-yoga [abhyāsa-yoga], O Dhanañjaya.

### Pratibha Commentary
Grace speaks as rescue: for minds entered into Him, Kṛṣṇa becomes samuddhartṛ from the death-ocean — and not after endless delay (nacirāt). The contested command is cognitive and total: mind on Me, intellect in Me, then dwelling in Me without doubt. Yet immediately a concession ladder begins: if you cannot fix steadily, use abhyāsa-yoga. Existentially this is anti-perfectionist without being anti-aspirational. The ideal is clear; the next step if you fail the ideal is also clear. Rescue is promised to the entered-mind; practice is prescribed to the unable. Do not use the promise to skip practice, or the practice-ladder to forget the promise. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**samuddhartṛ (समुद्धर्तृ)** — uplifter/rescuer -> Kṛṣṇa's role for those whose mind has entered Him -> "helper" is too weak for ocean-rescue from death-saṃsāra.

**abhyāsa-yoga (अभ्यासयोग)** — yoga of repetition/practice -> fallback when steady fixing fails -> "try harder" misses structured yogic repetition.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Bodhisattva vow to rescue beings from the ocean of saṃsāra:** Shared rescue-ocean imagery.
*Divergence:* Bodhisattva agency is compassion-vow; here the Lord Himself is the uplifter for entered minds.

**Psalm language of God drawing from deep waters:** Shared rescue grammar.
*Divergence:* Biblical lament/praise frame differs from Gītā's mind-intellect placement yoga.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Today, if steady fixation fails, do not quit — switch to abhyāsa: ten short returns of mind to the Lord across the day, timed to ordinary transitions.

## The Ladder: Practice, My Work, Relinquishing Fruit
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 12.10–12.12

If you are unable even in practice, be intent on My work; doing actions for My sake, you will also attain perfection.

If even this you are unable to do, then taking refuge in My yoga, relinquish the fruits of all actions, self-controlled.

Better is knowledge than practice; than knowledge, meditation excels; than meditation, relinquishing action-fruits — from relinquishing, peace follows at once.

---

### Devanagari
अभ्यासेऽप्यसमर्थोऽसि मत्कर्मपरमो भव।मदर्थमपि कर्माणि कुर्वन् सिद्धिमवाप्स्यसि

अथैतदप्यशक्तोऽसि कर्तुं मद्योगमाश्रितः।सर्वकर्मफलत्यागं ततः कुरु यतात्मवान्

श्रेयो हि ज्ञानमभ्यासाज्ज्ञानाद्ध्यानं विशिष्यते।ध्यानात्कर्मफलत्यागस्त्यागाच्छान्तिरनन्तरम्

### IAST
abhyāse'pyasamartho'si matkarmaparamo bhava|madarthamapi karmāṇi kurvan siddhimavāpsyasi

athaitadapyaśakto'si kartuṃ madyogamāśritaḥ|sarvakarmaphalatyāgaṃ tataḥ kuru yatātmavān

śreyo hi jñānamabhyāsājjñānāddhyānaṃ viśiṣyate|dhyānātkarmaphalatyāgastyāgācchāntiranantaram

### Pratibha Translation
If you are incapable even of practice, become one who takes My action as supreme; doing actions for My sake, you will attain success.

If even this you are unable to do, then taking refuge in My yoga, relinquish the fruit of all actions, with self restrained.

Knowledge is better than practice; meditation excels over knowledge; relinquishing the fruit of actions excels over meditation — from relinquishing, peace [śānti] comes immediately.

### Pratibha Commentary
The concession ladder continues downward with astonishing pastoral realism: if practice fails, do My work; if that fails, relinquish fruits under My yoga. Then a revaluation: jñāna better than abhyāsa; dhyāna better than jñāna; karma-phala-tyāga better than dhyāna — and from tyāga, śānti at once. The contested move is that the "lowest" concession (fruit-relinquishing) is simultaneously ranked as immediately peace-giving — not as spiritual failure. Existentially this breaks shame-spirals: inability to meditate is not exile from the path. Relinquishing fruit while acting under refuge is not second-class; it is a door that opens onto peace now. The ladder is mercy; the ranking is clarity about what actually yields śānti. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**karma-phala-tyāga (कर्मफलत्याग)** — relinquishing fruits of actions -> the rung that brings immediate peace -> "not caring about results" as apathy misses disciplined relinquishing under yoga.

**śānti (शान्ति)** — peace -> immediate fruit of tyāga -> "calm feeling" understates the soteriological quietude meant here.

**mat-karma (मत्कर्म)** — My action / action for Me -> the work-rung when practice fails -> "religious busywork" misses dedicated action-as-worship.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Ignatian finding God in action:** Both allow action-paths when contemplation falters.
*Divergence:* Jesuit frame is Christocentric; Gītā's ladder explicitly ranks tyāga above dhyāna for immediate peace.

**Stoic reserve clause:** Structural kinship with fruit-relinquishing.
*Divergence:* Stoicism centers prohairesis; Gītā centers refuge in the Lord's yoga.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Do one necessary task today while practicing only this: relinquish the fruit before you start. Notice whether peace appears sooner than when you chase a meditative high.

## Without Hate, Friend to All
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 12.13

Not hating any being, friendly and compassionate, free of "mine" and of I-making, even in pain and pleasure, patient—

---

### Devanagari
अद्वेष्टा सर्वभूतानां मैत्रः करुण एव च।निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः समदुःखसुखः क्षमी

### IAST
adveṣṭā sarvabhūtānāṃ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca|nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ samaduḥkhasukhaḥ kṣamī

### Pratibha Translation
Unhating toward all beings, friendly [maitra] and compassionate [karuṇa], free of possessiveness and of ego-making [nirahaṅkāra], equal in pain and pleasure, forbearing [kṣamī]—

### Pratibha Commentary
The beloved devotee is first described by social-affective marks: non-hatred, friendliness, compassion — then by interior renunciations: no mama, no ahaṅkāra — then by affective equality and forbearance. The contested claim is that devotion's authenticity is readable in how you treat beings, not only in how you feel toward God. Existentially this prevents private mysticism that remains harsh to neighbors. Non-hatred is not niceness; it is the refusal to let aversion define relation. Friendliness and compassion are positive shapes, not mere absence of harm. Equality in pain and pleasure blocks the devotee from making comfort the god. Patience (kṣamī) is the time-virtue that lets the others land. This verse begins a portrait; live even one clause fully and the portrait ceases to be literature. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**adveṣṭṛ (अद्वेष्टृ)** — non-hater -> first mark of the beloved devotee -> "tolerant" is weaker than absence of enmity toward all beings.

**nirahaṅkāra (निरहङ्कार)** — without I-making -> interior mark paired with nirmama -> "humble" as personality trait misses the technical stoppage of the I-maker.

**kṣamī (क्षमी)** — patient/forbearing -> endurance-virtue in the devotee list -> "patient" as waiting calmly understates forgiving forbearance.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Metta/karuṇā brahmavihāras:** Shared friendliness and compassion as liberated affect.
*Divergence:* Buddhist brahmavihāras are immeasurables without Kṛṣṇa-bhakti frame; Gītā embeds them in beloved-devotee criteria.

**1 Corinthians 13 (love's marks):** Structural list of relational virtues as authenticity-test.
*Divergence:* Pauline love is Christ-formed; Gītā's list is the Lord's "dear to Me" criteria.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Pick one being you subtly hate or resent. For today, practice only adveṣṭā: no rehearsing of the case against them. Add one concrete friendly or compassionate act if possible.

## Content, Steady, Mind Offered — Dear to Me

Always content, the yogin, self-controlled, of firm resolve, with mind and intellect offered to Me — that devotee of Mine is dear to Me.

---

### Devanagari

Firm resolve without restraint becomes fanaticism; restraint without resolve becomes limp. Existentially, "Am I dear?" is the wrong anxious question; "Are these marks growing?" is the right one. ### Key Terms
**santuṣṭa (सन्तुष्ट)** — content/satisfied -> continual contentment as devotee-mark -> "happy" misses settled sufficiency.

**dṛḍha-niścaya (दृढनिश्चय)** — firm resolve/certainty -> decisiveness of the dear devotee -> "stubborn" is the shadow; the text means non-vacillating orientation.

**priya (प्रिय)** — dear/beloved -> the Lord's evaluative love for the devotee -> "liked" is too casual for this theological endearment.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Sufi riḍā (contentment with God):** Shared contentment-as-love.
*Divergence:* Sufi idioms of fanā differ; Gītā keeps yogin identity and firm niścaya explicit.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Practice one hour of santuṣṭa: do not renegotiate your life situation in the mind. If resolve wavers, renew the offering of mind and intellect rather than feeding the waver.

## From Whom the World Does Not Shrink

### Key Terms
**udvega (उद्वेग)** — agitation/tremor of mind -> one of the freedoms of the dear devotee -> "stress" is too vague for this sudden disturbance.

**amarṣa (अमर्ष)** — impatience/indignation -> reactive intolerance freed in the dear one -> "anger" is broader; amarṣa is the inability to bear others' rise or slight.

## Independent, Even, Beyond Pairings
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 12.16–12.18

Dependent on nothing, pure, able, disengaged, untroubled, renouncing all undertakings — that devotee of Mine is dear to Me.

Who neither rejoices nor hates, neither grieves nor desires, renouncing pure and impure — that one full of devotion is dear to Me.

Equal to enemy and friend, and to honor and dishonor; equal in cold and heat, pleasure and pain; free from attachment—

---

### Devanagari
अनपेक्षः शुचिर्दक्ष उदासीनो गतव्यथः।सर्वारम्भपरित्यागी यो मद्भक्तः स मे प्रियः

यो न हृष्यति न द्वेष्टि न शोचति न काङ्क्षति।शुभाशुभपरित्यागी भक्ितमान्यः स मे प्रियः

समः शत्रौ च मित्रे च तथा मानापमानयोः।शीतोष्णसुखदुःखेषु समः सङ्गविवर्जितः

### IAST
anapekṣaḥ śucirdakṣa udāsīno gatavyathaḥ|sarvārambhaparityāgī yo madbhaktaḥ sa me priyaḥ

yo na hṛṣyati na dveṣṭi na śocati na kāṅkṣati|śubhāśubhaparityāgī bhakitamānyaḥ sa me priyaḥ

samaḥ śatrau ca mitre ca tathā mānāpamānayoḥ|śītoṣṇasukhaduḥkheṣu samaḥ saṅgavivarjitaḥ

### Pratibha Translation
Without expectation [anapekṣa], pure, skillful, unconcerned, free of distress, renouncing all initiatory undertakings — that devotee of Mine is dear to Me.

Who does not exult or hate, does not grieve or long, renouncing pleasant and unpleasant — that devotee is dear to Me.

The same to foe and friend, and in honor and disgrace; the same in cold and heat, pleasure and pain; free from clinging—

### Pratibha Commentary
The portrait intensifies into radical non-dependence and pair-equality. Anapekṣa (not leaning on outcomes), purity, competence (dakṣa), udāsīna disengagement, freedom from distress, and renunciation of ego-started projects (sarvārambha) define dearness. Then affective non-reactivity: no thrill, hate, grief, or craving — including renouncing the pure/impure pairing as possession. Then social and sensory equality: enemy/friend, honor/dishonor, cold/heat, pleasure/pain, without saṅga. The contested claim is that devotion matures into a stillness that still acts skillfully (dakṣa) without being owned by beginnings and dualities. Existentially this is not numbness; it is non-possession. Keep competence; drop the craving that makes competence a hostage. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**anapekṣa (अनपेक्ष)** — without looking toward / expectation -> independence of the dear devotee -> "detached" can sound cold; the term is non-leaning.

**sarvārambha-parityāga (सर्वारम्भपरित्याग)** — renouncing all undertakings -> dropping ego-initiated projects as identity -> "laziness" is the misreading; skillful dakṣa remains.

**saṅga (सङ्ग)** — clinging/attachment -> what is abandoned amid equalities -> "relationship" must not be confused with saṅga.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Daoist non-contention and wuwei:** Shared non-forcing and non-clinging.
*Divergence:* Wuwei is Dao-process; Gītā's equality is dearness-criteria under bhakti.

**Epictetus on preferred indifferents:** Shared equality toward externals.
*Divergence:* Stoic hegemonikon vs Gītā's mad-bhakta identity.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Renounce one ego-started undertaking today (a project begun mainly to prove yourself). Keep doing necessary duties with dakṣa — watch the difference in saṅga.

## Equal to Praise and Blame — This Deathless Dharma
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 12.19–12.20

To whom blame and praise are equal, silent, content with anything, without home, firm in mind, full of devotion — that person is dear to Me.

Those who honor this deathless dharma as taught, trusting, intent on Me as supreme — those devotees are exceedingly dear to Me.

---

### Devanagari
तुल्यनिन्दास्तुतिर्मौनी सन्तुष्टो येनकेनचित्।अनिकेतः स्थिरमतिर्भक्ितमान्मे प्रियो नरः

ये तु धर्म्यामृतमिदं यथोक्तं पर्युपासते।श्रद्दधाना मत्परमा भक्तास्तेऽतीव मे प्रियाः

### IAST
tulyanindāstutirmaunī santuṣṭo yenakenacit|aniketaḥ sthiramatirbhakitamānme priyo naraḥ

ye tu dharmyāmṛtamidaṃ yathoktaṃ paryupāsate|śraddadhānā matparamā bhaktāste'tīva me priyāḥ

### Pratibha Translation
The same in blame and praise, silent [maunin], content with whatever comes, without fixed nest [aniketa], firm-minded, full of devotion — that person is dear to Me.

Those who resort to this deathless dharma [dharmya amṛta] as declared, having faith, holding Me as supreme — those devotees are exceedingly [atīva] dear to Me.

### Pratibha Commentary
The portrait closes with social silence toward reputation: tulyanindāstuti — equal in blame and praise — maunin, content with anything, aniketa (unhoused in clinging), firm mind, devotion. Then the meta-verse: those who take up this very teaching as deathless dharma, with faith, Me-supreme, are atīva priya — exceedingly dear. The contested climax is that practicing the portrait is itself the amṛta-dharma, and faith in it intensifies dearness. Existentially the chapter refuses to leave you with a beautiful list and no mandate: this is to be honored as taught. Homelessness here is primarily interior non-nesting, not necessarily literal wandering — though it judges every nest that has become absolute. Exceedingly dear is not a new elite; it is the warmth of those who actually live the deathless ethic. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**aniketa (अनिकेत)** — without home/nest -> non-clinging dwelling of the dear devotee -> "homeless" as social status alone misses interior non-nesting.

**dharmya amṛta (धर्म्यामृत)** — deathless dharma / nectar of dharma -> the teaching of this chapter as immortal ethic -> "religious duty" flattens the amṛta claim.

**atīva priya (अतीव प्रिय)** — exceedingly dear -> intensified endearment for faithful practitioners of this dharma -> "favorites" as favoritism misreads recognition of lived faith.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Franciscan / mendicant aniketa ideals:** Shared non-nesting as spiritual mark.
*Divergence:* Christian poverty vows differ from Gītā's dear-devotee portrait under Kṛṣṇa-bhakti.

**Buddhist aniketa as homeless renunciant mark:** Shared term-history in Indic renunciation.
*Divergence:* Buddhist arahant path vs Gītā's bhakti dearness and faith in this dharma as amṛta.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Practice one day of tulyanindāstuti: when praise or blame arrives, do not build a nest in either. End the day by re-reading 12.20 as a vow, not a compliment.

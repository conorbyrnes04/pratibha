## From Hearing to Wanting Vision
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 11.1–11.3

Arjuna said: By the supreme secret concerning the self, spoken in Your grace, this delusion of mine has gone. The arising and passing of beings I have heard from You in detail, O Lotus-eyed, and also Your imperishable majesty. Just as You have described Yourself, O Supreme Lord, I wish to see Your lordly form, O Highest Person.

---

### Devanagari
अर्जुन उवाच

मदनुग्रहाय परमं गुह्यमध्यात्मसंज्ञितम्।

यत्त्वयोक्तं वचस्तेन मोहोऽयं विगतो मम

भवाप्ययौ हि भूतानां श्रुतौ विस्तरशो मया।

त्वत्तः कमलपत्राक्ष माहात्म्यमपि चाव्ययम्

एवमेतद्यथात्थ त्वमात्मानं परमेश्वर।

द्रष्टुमिच्छामि ते रूपमैश्वरं पुरुषोत्तम

### IAST
arjuna uvāca

madanugrahāya paramaṃ guhyamadhyātmasaṃjñitam|

yattvayoktaṃ vacastena moho'yaṃ vigato mama

bhavāpyayau hi bhūtānāṃ śrutau vistaraśo mayā|

tvattaḥ kamalapatrākṣa māhātmyamapi cāvyayam

evametadyathāttha tvamātmānaṃ parameśvara|

draṣṭumicchāmi te rūpamaiśvaraṃ puruṣottama

### Pratibha Translation
Arjuna said: By the supreme secret concerning the self, spoken in Your grace, this delusion of mine has gone. The arising and passing of beings I have heard from You in detail, O Lotus-eyed, and also Your imperishable majesty. Just as You have described Yourself, O Supreme Lord, I wish to see Your lordly form, O Highest Person.

### Pratibha Commentary
Hearing has done real work: moha is gone. Yet Arjuna asks to see — not from disbelief but from the logic of grace-received secret. The contested move is that right hearing awakens desire for darshana of aishvara rupa. Existentially this validates hunger for vision without scorning the prior verbal teaching. Delusion's removal is not the end; it is clearance for a more dangerous seeing. Wanting to see the Lord as the Lord has described Himself is fidelity, not curiosity-as-entertainment. Not through Vedas, austerity, gift, or sacrifice can I be seen thus as you have seen Me. But by undivided devotion alone can I be known thus, seen in truth, and entered into, O Scorcher of Foes.

### Pratibha Commentary
Highest vision's general door is devotion not demanding Arjuna's optics. The contested move in this stretch of the vision is that form hard to see even for gods; not by veda austerity gift sacrifice; by undivided devotion known seen entered. Existentially the line forces a choice about how you relate to overwhelm, agency, and devotion after seeing. Do not aestheticize the theophany; let it revise what you think action, fear, and love are for. The chapter's pedagogy moves from granted seeing to ethical-devotional walking; this unit is one step on that arc. The teaching's force is practical: what it claims about reality must reshape attention and choice, or it remains ornamental. If rehearsal does not change in ordinary time, the claim will not hold when pressure rises.

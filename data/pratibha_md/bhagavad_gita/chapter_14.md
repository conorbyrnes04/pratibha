# Bhagavad Gītā — Chapter 14 (Guṇa-Traya Vibhāga Yoga)
**Corpus entry:** Bhagavad Gītā / Mahābhārata
**Units:** 12 (one per brief CHUNK)

## The Highest Knowledge Returns to Kinship
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 14.1–14.3

The Blessed Lord said: I will again declare the supreme knowledge, the best of knowings, knowing which all the sages have gone from here to the highest perfection. Those who take refuge in this knowledge and come to share My nature are not born even at the creation, nor do they tremble at the dissolution. My womb is the great Brahman; in it I place the seed. From that, O Bhārata, comes the birth of all beings.

---

### Devanagari
श्री भगवानुवाचपरं भूयः प्रवक्ष्यामि ज्ञानानां ज्ञानमुत्तमम्।यज्ज्ञात्वा मुनयः सर्वे परां सिद्धिमितो गताः
इदं ज्ञानमुपाश्रित्य मम साधर्म्यमागताः।सर्गेऽपि नोपजायन्ते प्रलये न व्यथन्ति च
मम योनिर्महद्ब्रह्म तस्मिन् गर्भं दधाम्यहम्।संभवः सर्वभूतानां ततो भवति भारत

### IAST
śrī bhagavānuvācaparaṃ bhūyaḥ pravakṣyāmi jñānānāṃ jñānamuttamam|yajjñātvā munayaḥ sarve parāṃ siddhimito gatāḥ
idaṃ jñānamupāśritya mama sādharmyamāgatāḥ|sarge'pi nopajāyante pralaye na vyathanti ca
mama yonirmahadbrahma tasmin garbhaṃ dadhāmyaham|saṃbhavaḥ sarvabhūtānāṃ tato bhavati bhārata

### Pratibha Translation
The Blessed Lord said: I will again declare the supreme knowledge, the best of knowings, knowing which all the sages have gone from here to the highest perfection. Those who take refuge in this knowledge and come to share My nature are not born even at the creation, nor do they tremble at the dissolution. My womb is the great Brahman; in it I place the seed. From that, O Bhārata, comes the birth of all beings.

### Pratibha Commentary
Krishna reopens teaching not with novelty but with intensification: this is jñānānāṃ jñānam uttamam—the highest among knowledges. The contested claim arrives immediately in its fruit: those who take refuge in this knowledge attain sādharmya, kinship of nature with the Lord, and stand beyond birth at creation and anguish at dissolution. Cosmology then turns parental: mahad-brahman is the womb, Krishna the seed-giver. The universe is not an accident of matter; it is a fertile field quickened by divine impregnation. In the Gītā’s arc, chapter 14 does not abandon earlier yoga; it names the qualitative weather that makes yoga necessary. Existentially, the verse relocates spiritual ambition from accumulating insights to entering a shared nature that does not panic when worlds end. Knowledge here is shelter (upāśritya), not ornament. The sage’s “perfection” is not technical mastery but passage into a mode of being that creation and destruction cannot commandeer. When you fear endings, the teaching asks whether you are clinging to a private timeline or resting in kinship with the seed-giver of all wombs.

### Key Terms
**jñānam uttamam (ज्ञानमुत्तमम्)** — highest knowledge -> knowledge whose fruit is divine kinship -> 'best philosophy' misses soteriological kinship.
**sādharmya (साधर्म्य)** — sameness of dharma/nature -> participation in the Lord's mode of being -> 'similarity' is too weak for ontological sharing.
**mahad-brahman (महद्ब्रह्म)** — great Brahman as womb -> prakṛti as cosmic matrix -> 'matter' erases sacred fertility.
**garbhaṃ dadhāmi (गर्भं दधामि)** — I place the seed/embryo -> divine seeding of the womb of nature -> 'create' is too generic.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Chāndogya Upaniṣad III.19 / cosmic egg motifs]:** Cosmic genesis through a fertile matrix resonates with womb-and-seed cosmology.
*Divergence:* Upaniṣadic eggs often remain impersonal; here Krishna is explicitly the seed-placing father.
**[Genesis 1 / impregnating Word traditions]:** World-birth through divine initiative parallels seed placed in a receptive matrix.
*Divergence:* Biblical creation is typically ex nihilo command; the Gītā uses sexual-cosmic metaphor inside guṇa psychology to come.
**[Dao De Jing 1 / 6]:** The gateway of the mysterious female as root of heaven and earth echoes womb-language.
*Divergence:* Daoist mystery resists a personal seed-giver; Krishna claims paternal agency within Brahman-womb.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
When anxiety about beginnings or endings arises, silently rest in kinship: 'Not my private timeline—shared nature.' Hold that for ten breaths.

## Father of Forms in Every Womb
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 14.4

Whatever forms are born in any womb, O son of Kuntī, the great Brahman is their womb, and I am the father who gives the seed.

---

### Devanagari
सर्वयोनिषु कौन्तेय मूर्तयः सम्भवन्ति याः।तासां ब्रह्म महद्योनिरहं बीजप्रदः पिता

### IAST
sarvayoniṣu kaunteya mūrtayaḥ sambhavanti yāḥ|tāsāṃ brahma mahadyonirahaṃ bījapradaḥ pitā

### Pratibha Translation
Whatever forms are born in any womb, O son of Kuntī, the great Brahman is their womb, and I am the father who gives the seed.

### Pratibha Commentary
The previous cosmology becomes universal law: every form, in every womb, has mahad-brahman as matrix and Krishna as seed-father. The contested move is to sacralize embodiment without romanticizing it. Birth is neither shame nor mere biology; it is the meeting of cosmic womb and divine seed. This undercuts both materialist reduction and world-rejecting spirituality. The verse also quietly universalizes dignity: no womb is outside this parentage, which means contempt for any form is a theological mistake. In classical commentary, this line anchors the Lord’s immanence inside prakṛti’s productivity rather than against it. Existentially, looking at any living form becomes a recognition scene: matrix and father are already at work. Ethics follows: cruelty toward bodies is hostility toward the Lord’s generative act. Wonder follows too: the ordinary face before you is not “just biology,” but a seeded form of the great womb. The parental metaphor also disciplines pride: if every form is womb-born and seed-given, spiritual status cannot be a private achievement trophy.

### Key Terms
**yoni (योनि)** — womb / source -> matrix of manifestation -> 'origin' loses embodied specificity.
**mūrti (मूर्ति)** — form / embodiment -> concrete appearance born of womb -> 'image' can sound merely representational.
**bīja-prada pitā (बीजप्रदः पिता)** — seed-giving father -> Krishna as impregnating principle -> 'creator god' flattens the parental metaphor.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad V–VI]:** God as source of beings resonates with father-of-seed language.
*Divergence:* Śvetāśvatara leans Rudra-theistic; the Gītā prepares a guṇa analysis of bondage within this parentage.
**[Plato, Timaeus]:** The demiurge and receptacle parallel seed and womb functions.
*Divergence:* Timaeus is craft-cosmology; Krishna’s parentage is intimate and immediately existential.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Look at one living being today and silently say: 'Great Brahman is the womb; the Lord is the seed-father.' Notice what hardness softens.

## The Guṇas Bind the Embodied
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 14.5

Sattva, rajas, and tamas—the guṇas born of nature—bind the imperishable embodied one to the body, O mighty-armed.

---

### Devanagari
सत्त्वं रजस्तम इति गुणाः प्रकृतिसंभवाः।निबध्नन्ति महाबाहो देहे देहिनमव्ययम्

### IAST
sattvaṃ rajastama iti guṇāḥ prakṛtisaṃbhavāḥ|nibadhnanti mahābāho dehe dehinamavyayam

### Pratibha Translation
Sattva, rajas, and tamas—the guṇas born of nature—bind the imperishable embodied one to the body, O mighty-armed.

### Pratibha Commentary
Here the chapter’s engine starts: the three guṇas, born of prakṛti, bind the imperishable embodied one (dehin) to the body. The contested claim is that what is avyaya—undecaying—can still be bound. Bondage is not damage to the Self’s essence; it is constraint of the embodied through qualitative modes. Sattva, rajas, and tamas are not moral teams first; they are ontological straps. This is why moralism alone fails: you can “be good” in a sattvic way and remain tightly bound. The Gītā’s psychology is cosmological; your moods are not merely private weather but expressions of prakṛti’s strands. Existentially, this explains why insight alone does not free: the straps are affective-behavioral climates, not mere opinions. The dehin remains imperishable while living as if perishable under guṇa-bondage. Freedom begins when binding is correctly named as binding, not as identity. Naming the straps correctly is already a sattvic act—so long as one does not cling to the naming as a new identity.

### Key Terms
**guṇa (गुण)** — strand / quality -> constitutive mode of prakṛti that binds -> 'quality' as adjective misses binding force.
**nibadhnanti (निबध्नन्ति)** — they bind -> active fastening of the embodied to body -> 'influence' softens captivity.
**dehin (देहिन्)** — embodied one -> the one possessing a body, imperishable yet bindable -> 'soul' can float free of embodiment.
**avyaya (अव्यय)** — imperishable -> undecaying subject of bondage -> 'eternal soul' can sound immune in the wrong way.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Sāṃkhya Kārikā 12–14]:** Tripartite guṇa theory is the direct structural ancestor.
*Divergence:* Classical Sāṃkhya aims at puruṣa’s isolation; the Gītā will teach transcendence into divine being through non-attachment and bhakti.
**[Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics on hexeis]:** Enduring dispositions shaping action parallel guṇa climates.
*Divergence:* Aristotelian ethics seeks virtuous mean; guṇas are cosmological binders before they are character styles.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
For one day, when you feel 'stuck,' ask: 'Which climate is binding me—clarity-attachment, restless drive, or dull concealment?' Name it without fixing it yet.

## How Each Guṇa Binds
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 14.6–14.8

Among them, sattva, being stainless, is luminous and healthy; it binds through attachment to joy and attachment to knowledge, O sinless one. Know rajas to be of the nature of passion, arising from thirst and attachment; it binds the embodied one through attachment to action, O son of Kuntī. Know tamas, born of ignorance, as the deluder of all embodied beings; it binds through negligence, indolence, and sleep, O Bhārata.

---

### Devanagari
तत्र सत्त्वं निर्मलत्वात्प्रकाशकमनामयम्।सुखसङ्गेन बध्नाति ज्ञानसङ्गेन चानघ
रजो रागात्मकं विद्धि तृष्णासङ्गसमुद्भवम्।तन्निबध्नाति कौन्तेय कर्मसङ्गेन देहिनम्
तमस्त्वज्ञानजं विद्धि मोहनं सर्वदेहिनाम्।प्रमादालस्यनिद्राभिस्तन्निबध्नाति भारत

### IAST
tatra sattvaṃ nirmalatvātprakāśakamanāmayam|sukhasaṅgena badhnāti jñānasaṅgena cānagha
rajo rāgātmakaṃ viddhi tṛṣṇāsaṅgasamudbhavam|tannibadhnāti kaunteya karmasaṅgena dehinam
tamastvajñānajaṃ viddhi mohanaṃ sarvadehinām|pramādālasyanidrābhistannibadhnāti bhārata

### Pratibha Translation
Among them, sattva, being stainless, is luminous and healthy; it binds through attachment to joy and attachment to knowledge, O sinless one. Know rajas to be of the nature of passion, arising from thirst and attachment; it binds the embodied one through attachment to action, O son of Kuntī. Know tamas, born of ignorance, as the deluder of all embodied beings; it binds through negligence, indolence, and sleep, O Bhārata.

### Pratibha Commentary
Each guṇa binds through what it makes lovable. Sattva binds by sukha-saṅga and jñāna-saṅga—attachment to joy and to knowing. The contested move is ruthless: even clarity and happiness are straps when clutched. Rajas binds through karma-saṅga, the addiction to doing born of thirst. Tamas binds through pramāda, ālasya, and nidrā—negligence, laziness, sleep—as forms of delusion. Spiritual culture often flees tamas and rajas into sattva, then worships luminous captivity as arrival. The Gītā refuses that bargain. In the sequence of the teaching, this triad prepares the later claim that transcendence is beyond all three, not an upgrade to the nicest strand. Existentially, watch what you defend most eloquently: if it is your calm, your insight, or your busy usefulness, you may be polishing a bind. Freedom is not upgrading your guṇa; it is seeing the binding mechanism in all three. The practical test is simple and severe: what happens when your favorite clarity is interrupted? If rage or despair follows, sattva was a strap.

### Key Terms
**sattva (सत्त्व)** — luminosity / purity-strand -> binds via joy-and-knowledge attachment -> 'goodness' moralizes a binder.
**rajas (रजस्)** — passion-strand -> binds via action-attachment -> 'energy' misses thirst-born compulsion.
**tamas (तमस्)** — darkness-strand -> binds via negligence, indolence, sleep -> 'laziness' shrinks cosmological delusion.
**saṅga (सङ्ग)** — attachment / adherence -> the actual strap within each guṇa -> 'liking' understates bondage.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Patañjali, YS II.3–9 (kleśas)]:** Afflictions that bind through attachment and ignorance parallel guṇa-binding.
*Divergence:* Kleśas are psychological; guṇas are also cosmic strands structuring rebirth.
**[Evagrius, eight thoughts]:** Passions that capture the monk resonate with rajas-tamas mechanisms; vainglory with sattvic binding.
*Divergence:* Evagrius aims at pure prayer; Krishna aims at guṇātīta freedom into divine being.
**[Zhuangzi on usefulness of useless]:** Suspicion of achievement-drive resonates with seeing rajas-binding.
*Divergence:* Zhuangzi dissolves fixed success metrics; the Gītā keeps a clear hierarchy toward transcendence of all three.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Catch one sattvic bind today (pride in insight or clinging to a calm mood). Soften the clutch while keeping the clarity.

## Dominance, Rotation, and the Signs of Sattva
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 14.9–14.11

Sattva causes attachment to joy, rajas to action, O Bhārata; but tamas, veiling knowledge, causes attachment to negligence. Sattva arises by overpowering rajas and tamas; rajas by overpowering sattva and tamas; likewise tamas by overpowering sattva and rajas. When light dawns through all the gates of this body, and knowledge as well—then one should know that sattva has increased.

---

### Devanagari
सत्त्वं सुखे सञ्जयति रजः कर्मणि भारत।ज्ञानमावृत्य तु तमः प्रमादे सञ्जयत्युत
रजस्तमश्चाभिभूय सत्त्वं भवति भारत।रजः सत्त्वं तमश्चैव तमः सत्त्वं रजस्तथा
सर्वद्वारेषु देहेऽस्मिन्प्रकाश उपजायते।ज्ञानं यदा तदा विद्याद्विवृद्धं सत्त्वमित्युत

### IAST
sattvaṃ sukhe sañjayati rajaḥ karmaṇi bhārata|jñānamāvṛtya tu tamaḥ pramāde sañjayatyuta
rajastamaścābhibhūya sattvaṃ bhavati bhārata|rajaḥ sattvaṃ tamaścaiva tamaḥ sattvaṃ rajastathā
sarvadvāreṣu dehe'sminprakāśa upajāyate|jñānaṃ yadā tadā vidyādvivṛddhaṃ sattvamityuta

### Pratibha Translation
Sattva causes attachment to joy, rajas to action, O Bhārata; but tamas, veiling knowledge, causes attachment to negligence. Sattva arises by overpowering rajas and tamas; rajas by overpowering sattva and tamas; likewise tamas by overpowering sattva and rajas. When light dawns through all the gates of this body, and knowledge as well—then one should know that sattva has increased.

### Pratibha Commentary
Guṇas are dynamic, not fixed personality tattoos. Each prevails by overpowering the others. Sattva attaches to joy, rajas to action, tamas—by veiling knowledge—to negligence. The diagnostic for rising sattva is concrete: prakāśa through all gates of the body, and jñāna dawning. The contested claim is that the body itself telegraphs ontology: luminous sense-doors are not merely good mood; they are evidence of sattva’s increase. This makes contemplative life empirical without becoming medicalized. You learn to read gates, not to obsess over labels. Existentially, this trains honest weather-reading without fatalism: climates rotate, and recognition is already a form of freedom’s beginning. When light increases, gratitude is appropriate; clutching the light as identity returns you to sattvic bondage named in the previous verses. Rotation also means compassion: the person lost in tamas today may stand in sattva tomorrow; contempt freezes a moving climate into a false essence. This is not ornamental theology; it is a demand that lived climate, ethical stance, and metaphysical claim stay braided in one practice of seeing.

### Key Terms
**sañjayati (सञ्जयति)** — causes to cling / attaches -> guṇa as binder to a domain -> 'leads to' is too mild.
**abhibhūya (अभिभूय)** — having overpowered -> rotational dominance among guṇas -> 'balance' wrongly implies stable equilibrium.
**sarva-dvāreṣu (सर्वद्वारेषु)** — in all gates -> sense-doors of the body as sites of light -> 'everywhere' misses embodied gates.
**prakāśa (प्रकाश)** — light / illumination -> sign of increased sattva -> 'happiness' conflates mood with luminosity.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Abhidharma / vipassanā mind-state diagnosis]:** Reading present qualities through signs parallels guṇa diagnostics.
*Divergence:* Buddhist reading aims at impermanence and non-self; here signs point to guṇa dominance and eventual transcendence.
**[Galenic temperaments]:** Humor climates shaping behavior are a loose Western cousin.
*Divergence:* Humors are medical; guṇas are soteriological binders tied to rebirth and liberation.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Three times today, scan the 'gates' of the body. If light and knowing are present, note 'sattva rising' without clinging to it.

## Signs of Rajas and Tamas, and Death in Sattva
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 14.12–14.14

Greed, activity, undertaking of actions, unrest, and longing arise when rajas increases, O best of Bharatas. Darkness, inactivity, negligence, and delusion arise when tamas increases, O joy of the Kurus. When the embodied one dies while sattva is dominant, then he attains the stainless worlds of those who know the highest.

---

### Devanagari
लोभः प्रवृत्तिरारम्भः कर्मणामशमः स्पृहा।रजस्येतानि जायन्ते विवृद्धे भरतर्षभ
अप्रकाशोऽप्रवृत्तिश्च प्रमादो मोह एव च।तमस्येतानि जायन्ते विवृद्धे कुरुनन्दन
यदा सत्त्वे प्रवृद्धे तु प्रलयं याति देहभृत्।तदोत्तमविदां लोकानमलान्प्रतिपद्यते

### IAST
lobhaḥ pravṛttirārambhaḥ karmaṇāmaśamaḥ spṛhā|rajasyetāni jāyante vivṛddhe bharatarṣabha
aprakāśo'pravṛttiśca pramādo moha eva ca|tamasyetāni jāyante vivṛddhe kurunandana
yadā sattve pravṛddhe tu pralayaṃ yāti dehabhṛt|tadottamavidāṃ lokānamalānpratipadyate

### Pratibha Translation
Greed, activity, undertaking of actions, unrest, and longing arise when rajas increases, O best of Bharatas. Darkness, inactivity, negligence, and delusion arise when tamas increases, O joy of the Kurus. When the embodied one dies while sattva is dominant, then he attains the stainless worlds of those who know the highest.

### Pratibha Commentary
Behavioral signatures clarify the climates: rajas shows as greed, initiation-fever, unrest, craving; tamas as darkness, non-activity, negligence, delusion. Then the chapter raises the stakes to death: dying in sattva yields stainless worlds of the highest knowers. The contested move is to treat the dying climate as decisive without reducing life to deathbed panic. One’s cultivated predominance at the end expresses a trajectory already rehearsed in ordinary days. Classical readers hear continuity between living practice and dying fruition; modern readers can hear a demand for honesty about the climate you feed. Existentially, this is not superstition; it is continuity: the weather you rehearse becomes the world you inherit. The teaching does not ask for perfection at the last minute; it asks for long apprenticeship in luminosity without making luminosity your prison. Therefore daily rehearsal matters more than emergency spirituality: the dying mind continues the groove already cut by living preference. This is not ornamental theology; it is a demand that lived climate, ethical stance, and metaphysical claim stay braided in one practice of seeing.

### Key Terms
**lobha (लोभ)** — greed -> rajasic signature -> 'ambition' can romanticize the same bind.
**aprakāśa (अप्रकाश)** — non-light / darkness -> tamasic signature -> 'confusion' is narrower than ontological darkening.
**pralaya (प्रलय)** — dissolution / death here as body's end -> transition keyed to guṇa dominance -> 'death' alone misses climate-continuity.
**amalān lokān (अमलान् लोकान्)** — stainless worlds -> sattvic postmortem fruition -> 'heaven' may import alien geography.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Tibetan bardos / dying awareness teachings]:** State at death shaping next arising is structurally homologous.
*Divergence:* Bardo teachings detail transitional visions; the Gītā compresses into guṇa predominance and world-attainment.
**[Plato, Phaedo]:** Philosophical life preparing a good death resonates with dying in sattva.
*Divergence:* Phaedo seeks pure soul freed from body; Krishna keeps guṇa cosmology and multiple postmortem tracks.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Ask once today: 'If this climate solidified, what world would it imply?' Let the answer sober your next indulgence of rajas or tamas.

## Fruits of the Guṇas
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 14.15–14.17

Dying in rajas, one is born among those attached to action; likewise, dying in tamas, one is born in the wombs of the deluded. They say the fruit of good action is sattvic and stainless; the fruit of rajas is pain; the fruit of tamas is ignorance. From sattva, knowledge is born; from rajas, greed; from tamas arise negligence and delusion, and ignorance as well.

---

### Devanagari
रजसि प्रलयं गत्वा कर्मसङ्गिषु जायते।तथा प्रलीनस्तमसि मूढयोनिषु जायते
कर्मणः सुकृतस्याहुः सात्त्विकं निर्मलं फलम्।रजसस्तु फलं दुःखमज्ञानं तमसः फलम्
सत्त्वात्सञ्जायते ज्ञानं रजसो लोभ एव च।प्रमादमोहौ तमसो भवतोऽज्ञानमेव च

### IAST
rajasi pralayaṃ gatvā karmasaṅgiṣu jāyate|tathā pralīnastamasi mūḍhayoniṣu jāyate
karmaṇaḥ sukṛtasyāhuḥ sāttvikaṃ nirmalaṃ phalam|rajasastu phalaṃ duḥkhamajñānaṃ tamasaḥ phalam
sattvātsañjāyate jñānaṃ rajaso lobha eva ca|pramādamohau tamaso bhavato'jñānameva ca

### Pratibha Translation
Dying in rajas, one is born among those attached to action; likewise, dying in tamas, one is born in the wombs of the deluded. They say the fruit of good action is sattvic and stainless; the fruit of rajas is pain; the fruit of tamas is ignorance. From sattva, knowledge is born; from rajas, greed; from tamas arise negligence and delusion, and ignorance as well.

### Pratibha Commentary
Postmortem destinations and present fruits are braided. Rajas leads to birth among karma-saṅgins; tamas into deluded wombs. Action’s fruit: sattvic stainlessness, rajasic pain, tamasic ignorance. Causally inverted and reinforced: sattva births knowledge, rajas greed, tamas negligence-delusion-ignorance. The contested claim is moral cosmology without cheap moralism—pain is not random punishment but rajas’s own yield. Ignorance is not an insult; it is tamas ripening. This loop explains why “trying harder” in a rajasic key often deepens pain, and why tamasic comfort deepens fog. Existentially, you can taste fruits now: the aftertaste of your actions already announces their guṇa. Liberation pedagogy uses that aftertaste as teacher before death makes the climate harder to revise. Watch especially the fruit that flatters you—stainlessness can intoxicate—and the fruit that shames you; both are teachers if not clutched. This is not ornamental theology; it is a demand that lived climate, ethical stance, and metaphysical claim stay braided in one practice of seeing.

### Key Terms
**karmasaṅgi (कर्मसङ्गि)** — one attached to action -> rajasic rebirth niche -> 'workaholic' shrinks the ontological claim.
**mūḍha-yoni (मूढयोनि)** — deluded womb -> tamasic birth matrix -> 'bad reincarnation' as cartoon misses epistemic darkness.
**phala (फल)** — fruit -> intrinsic yield of guṇa-shaped action -> 'reward/punishment' externalizes what is ripening.
**ajñāna (अज्ञान)** — ignorance -> tamas’s fruit and offspring -> 'not knowing facts' understates delusive closure.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Karma theory in Manu / Dharmaśāstra]:** Action-fruit continuity resonates, but here fruit is guṇa-coded.
*Divergence:* Dharmaśāstra often maps social duty; the Gītā maps psychological-cosmological ripening toward liberation.
**[Stoic doctrine of consequences in character]:** Actions shape the agent who then inherits their world.
*Divergence:* Stoicism centers assent and virtue; guṇa theory centers qualitative binders and rebirth.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

## Rising, Standing, Falling—and Seeing Beyond the Doer
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 14.18–14.20

Those established in sattva go upward; the rajasic remain in the middle; those established in the lowest guṇa-activity go downward. When the seer sees no doer other than the guṇas, and knows what is beyond the guṇas, he attains My being. Having transcended these three guṇas that arise from the body, the embodied one, freed from birth, death, age, and pain, tastes immortality.

---

### Devanagari

गुणानेतानतीत्य त्रीन्देही देहसमुद्भवान्।जन्ममृत्युजरादुःखैर्विमुक्तोऽमृतमश्नुते

### IAST

### Pratibha Translation
Those established in sattva go upward; the rajasic remain in the middle; those established in the lowest guṇa-activity go downward. When the seer sees no doer other than the guṇas, and knows what is beyond the guṇas, he attains My being. ### Pratibha Commentary
Trajectory language becomes vertical: sattva upward, rajas middle, tamas downward. Then the decisive philosophical cut: the seer who sees no doer apart from the guṇas, and knows what is beyond them, attains madbhāva—Krishna’s being. Transcending the three yields immortality beyond birth-death-age-pain. The contested move is non-doership without nihilism: guṇas act; the seer knows the beyond. This is not permission for irresponsibility; it is the end of the fantasy that a separate ego authors the strands. In the Gītā’s theistic frame, seeing beyond the guṇas is not blank neutrality; it opens into divine being. Existentially, freedom is not becoming a better guṇa-bundle; it is seeing agency correctly and tasting the deathless while life continues. Madbhāva keeps the teaching from collapsing into blank witnessing: beyond the guṇas is not nowhere; it is kinship with the Lord. This is not ornamental theology; it is a demand that lived climate, ethical stance, and metaphysical claim stay braided in one practice of seeing.

**ūrdhvam / madhye / adhaḥ (ऊर्ध्वम् / मध्ये / अधः)** — up / middle / down -> guṇa trajectories -> 'social mobility' parody misses cosmic psychology.

**guṇātīta (गुणातीत)** — beyond the guṇas -> transcendence of the three binders -> 'above good and evil' can sound antinomian.
**amṛta (अमृत)** — immortality / deathless nectar -> fruit of transcending guṇas -> 'afterlife' postpones present tasting.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Gītā 3.27–28]:** Guṇas act among guṇas; the knower is not entangled—direct continuity.
*Divergence:* Chapter 3 emphasizes karma-yoga; chapter 14 thematizes transcendence into divine being.
**[Plotinus on rising above lower soul motions]:** Ascent beyond turbulent psychic modes resonates.
*Divergence:* Plotinus ascends toward the One; Krishna names attainment as madbhāva, personal-divine kinship.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
In one action today, watch the guṇas doing. Silently: 'No doer other than the strands—and there is the beyond.' Rest one breath in the beyond.

## Marks of One Who Has Gone Beyond
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 14.21–14.23

Arjuna said: By what marks is the one who has gone beyond these three guṇas known, O Lord? How does he conduct himself, and how does he transcend these three guṇas? The Blessed Lord said: Light, activity, and delusion alike—he does not hate them when they arise, O son of Pāṇḍu, nor longs for them when they cease. ### Devanagari

### IAST

### Pratibha Translation
Arjuna said: By what marks is the one who has gone beyond these three guṇas known, O Lord? How does he conduct himself, and how does he transcend these three guṇas? ### Pratibha Commentary
Arjuna asks for liṅgas—marks—and ācāra—conduct—of the guṇātīta. Krishna answers with non-reactivity across all three climates: he neither hates prakāśa, pravṛtti, and moha when they arise nor craves them when they cease. The contested image is udāsīnavad āsīnaḥ—seated as if indifferent—not numb, but unthrown. Knowing 'guṇā vartante'—the guṇas operate—he does not waver. This is the practical face of the previous metaphysics: if guṇas are the doers, hatred of a climate is still entanglement, and craving for a climate is still bondage. Existentially, transcendence looks like weather literacy without weather worship or weather war. The mark is not a costume of calm; it is unmoved intimacy with rising and ceasing alike. If you still need a particular mood to feel spiritual, you have not yet sat 'as if indifferent'; you have only switched allegiances among the strands. This is not ornamental theology; it is a demand that lived climate, ethical stance, and metaphysical claim stay braided in one practice of seeing.

### Key Terms
**liṅga (लिङ्ग)** — mark / sign -> recognizable traits of the transcender -> 'symptom' medicalizes liberation.
**udāsīna (उदासीन)** — seated as if aside / indifferent -> poised non-entanglement -> 'apathetic' is the failure mode of the translation.
**neṅgate (नेङ्गते)** — does not stir / waver -> stability amid guṇa rotation -> 'rigid' misreads as stiffness.
**guṇā vartante (गुणा वर्तन्ते)** — the guṇas operate -> mantra of non-doership -> 'stuff happens' is cynical counterfeit.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Patañjali, YS I.16 / para-vairāgya]:** Dispassion even toward the highest sattvic attainments resonates.
*Divergence:* Patañjali aims at aloneness of the seer; Krishna’s transcender is en route to unswerving bhakti (14.26).
**[Stoic phantasia and non-assent]:** Not being dragged by appearances parallels non-hate/non-craving toward guṇa-states.
*Divergence:* Stoicism trains rational assent; here the key cognition is 'guṇas alone operate.'

### Practice (Abhyasa)
When a mood arises or fades, practice the double refusal: do not hate its arrival; do not clutch its departure. Whisper: 'the guṇas operate.'

## I Am the Ground of Brahman
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 14.27

For I am the foundation of Brahman, of the deathless and the imperishable, of everlasting dharma, and of absolute bliss.

---

### Devanagari
ब्रह्मणो हि प्रतिष्ठाऽहममृतस्याव्ययस्य च।शाश्वतस्य च धर्मस्य सुखस्यैकान्तिकस्य च

### IAST
brahmaṇo hi pratiṣṭhā'hamamṛtasyāvyayasya ca|śāśvatasya ca dharmasya sukhasyaikāntikasya ca

### Pratibha Translation
For I am the foundation of Brahman, of the deathless and the imperishable, of everlasting dharma, and of absolute bliss.

### Pratibha Commentary
The chapter’s final thunder: Krishna is the pratiṣṭhā—foundation—of Brahman, of the deathless and the imperishable, of everlasting dharma, and of aikāntika sukha—final, unalloyed joy. The contested claim is theistic grounding of the Absolute: Brahman is not bypassing Krishna; Krishna is Brahman’s ground. This blocks an escape hatch into impersonalism that abandons love, and blocks a piety that never tastes the deathless. It also reinterprets sattvic joy: guṇa-sukha was a bind; aikāntika sukha is grounded in the Lord. Existentially, the highest knowledge ends not in a diagram of strands but in a person who is the ground of the Absolute, of dharma, and of joy that does not trade on the guṇas. Refuge becomes metaphysical realism, not sentiment. To say Krishna is Brahman’s foundation is to refuse choosing between love and the Absolute; the Absolute is loved as ground. This is not ornamental theology; it is a demand that lived climate, ethical stance, and metaphysical claim stay braided in one practice of seeing.

### Key Terms
**pratiṣṭhā (प्रतिष्ठा)** — foundation / resting-place -> Krishna as ground of Brahman -> 'support' is too architectural-external.
**amṛta / avyaya (अमृत / अव्यय)** — deathless / imperishable -> grounded in Krishna -> 'eternity' can stay abstract.
**śāśvata dharma (शाश्वत धर्म)** — everlasting dharma -> grounded in the Lord -> 'religion' domesticates the claim.
**aikāntika sukha (ऐकान्तिक सुख)** — absolute / exclusive joy -> final bliss beyond guṇa-sukha -> 'happiness' is far too thin.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Gītā 7.7 / 10.8]:** Krishna as source and origin of all resonates with pratiṣṭhā of Brahman.
*Divergence:* Those chapters emphasize manifestation and vibhūti; 14.27 explicitly grounds Brahman, dharma, and absolute joy.
**[Rāmānuja’s viśiṣṭādvaita reading]:** The personal Lord as ground of Brahman is theologically decisive here.
*Divergence:* Non-dual readings stress identity; the verse’s 'I am the foundation' preserves a theistic asymmetry productive for devotion.
**[Pseudo-Dionysius on God as cause of being and good]:** Ground of deathless reality and joy resonates.
*Divergence:* Dionysius proceeds by ascent and negation; Krishna speaks as the foundation in first person to a friend in war.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Sit quietly and contemplate: 'The ground of the deathless and of joy is not a mood—it is the Lord.' Rest the mind there without argument.

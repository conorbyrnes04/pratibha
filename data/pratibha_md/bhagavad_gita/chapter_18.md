## Renunciation and Relinquishment Are Not the Same
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 18.1–18.3

Arjuna said: I wish to know the truth of renunciation [saṃnyāsa], O mighty-armed, and of relinquishment [tyāga], O Hṛṣīkeśa, severally, O slayer of Keśin. The Blessed Lord said: Sages know the laying aside of desire-driven actions as renunciation; the clear-sighted declare the relinquishment of the fruits of all actions to be relinquishment. Some thoughtful ones say action should be abandoned as fault-like; others say acts of sacrifice, giving, and austerity should not be abandoned.

---

सर्वकर्मफलत्यागं प्राहुस्त्यागं विचक्षणाः

### Pratibha Commentary
The finale opens by refusing synonym collapse. Saṃnyāsa and tyāga are adjacent but not identical: one is nyāsa of kāmya-karmas; the other is phala-tyāga of all actions. The contested move is already ethical-metaphysical: freedom is not primarily joblessness. Then Krishna stages a debate—some abandon action as doṣa; others insist yajña-dāna-tapas must remain. Existentially, many moderns call burnout “renunciation” and call bargaining “engagement.” The Gītā will adjudicate with guṇa precision. ### Key Terms
**saṃnyāsa (संन्यास)** — laying down / renunciation -> specifically of desire-driven acts in this definition -> 'monkhood' over-narrows.
**tyāga (त्याग)** — relinquishment -> fruit-relinquishment of actions -> 'giving up' is too vague.
**kāmya-karma (काम्यकर्म)** — desire-motivated ritual/action -> what saṃnyāsa drops -> 'hobbies' trivializes.
**phala-tyāga (फलत्याग)** — relinquishing fruit -> tyāga’s core -> 'not caring about outcomes' can become negligence parody.

## Do Not Abandon the Purifying Acts
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 18.4–18.6

Hear My settled conclusion on relinquishment, O best of Bharatas; for relinquishment is declared to be of three kinds, O tiger among men. Acts of sacrifice, giving, and austerity are not to be abandoned; they must indeed be done. Sacrifice, giving, and austerity purify the wise. But even these actions should be done abandoning attachment and fruits—this is My definite and highest view, O Pārtha.

## Three Relinquishments: Dark, Passionate, Luminous
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 18.7–18.9

Renunciation of prescribed action is not proper; abandoning it out of delusion is declared tamasic. The one who abandons action merely as bodily suffering, from fear of pain—doing rajasic relinquishment—does not obtain the fruit of relinquishment. When prescribed action is done simply because it must be done, O Arjuna, abandoning attachment and fruit—that relinquishment is held to be sattvic.

## The True Relinquisher Cannot Drop All Action
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 18.10–18.12

For embodied beings cannot entirely abandon actions; but the one who relinquishes the fruit of action is called a relinquisher. The threefold fruit of action—undesired, desired, and mixed—accrues after death to those who do not relinquish, but never to renouncers.

## The Lone Agent Is a Bad View
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 18.16–18.18

## Abandon All Dharmas: Take Refuge in Me Alone
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 18.66

---

### Devanagari

### IAST

### Pratibha Translation
Abandoning all dharmas, take refuge in Me alone. I will liberate you from all evils; do not grieve.

### Pratibha Commentary
This is the Gītā’s razor. Sarva-dharmān parityajya māmekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja—having abandoned all dharmas, go to Me alone as refuge. Then the vow: ahaṃ tvā sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi—I will liberate you from all evils/sins; mā śucaḥ—do not grieve. The contested move is not antinomian vandalism of ethics; it is the end of salvation-by-portfolio. “All dharmas” includes the righteous projects by which the ego still seeks to secure itself—varṇa-duty as self-justification, renunciation as self-justification, even “Gītā practice” as self-justification. The verse does not license cruelty; it dethrones rival refuges. After eighteen chapters of discrimination, guṇas, svadharma, and tapas, Krishna says the last act is not another technique but exclusive śaraṇa. Theologically, agency shifts: mokṣa is promised in the first person by the Lord. Pastorally, the battlefield grief that opened the Gītā is addressed at the root: do not grieve—not because the field is unreal only, but because refuge holds. Existentially, find what you are still clutching as co-savior beside the Lord—your correctness, your wound, your role, your metaphysics—and let the verse pry it open. The fear that abandoning all dharmas means becoming lawless is itself often a dharma-idol’s voice. True refuge produces a life; it does not need a side-altar of self-rescue. When this verse is heard only as slogan, it becomes cheap. When heard after 18.63–65’s freedom, love, and vow, it becomes the end of bargaining.

तच्च संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य रूपमत्यद्भुतं हरेः।

विस्मयो मे महान् राजन् हृष्यामि च पुनः पुनः
यत्र योगेश्वरः कृष्णो यत्र पार्थो धनुर्धरः।

तत्र श्रीर्विजयो भूतिर्ध्रुवा नीतिर्मतिर्मम

### IAST
rājansaṃsmṛtya saṃsmṛtya saṃvādamimamadbhutam|keśavārjunayoḥ puṇyaṃ hṛṣyāmi ca muhurmuhuḥ
tacca saṃsmṛtya saṃsmṛtya rūpamatyadbhutaṃ hareḥ|

vismayo me mahān rājan hṛṣyāmi ca punaḥ punaḥ
yatra yogeśvaraḥ kṛṣṇo yatra pārtho dhanurdharaḥ|

tatra śrīrvijayo bhūtirdhruvā nītirmatirmama

### Pratibha Translation
O King, remembering again and again this wondrous and holy dialogue of Keśava and Arjuna, I rejoice again and again. And remembering again and again that most wondrous form of Hari, great is my amazement, O King, and I rejoice again and again. Wherever there is Kṛṣṇa, Lord of yoga, and wherever there is Pārtha, the archer—there, I hold, are fortune, victory, thriving, and lasting policy.

### Pratibha Commentary
Sañjaya’s coda is liturgical memory: saṃsmṛtya saṃsmṛtya—remembering again and again—the dialogue and the cosmic form, he rejoices. Then the final maxim: where Yogesvara Krishna and archer Arjuna are, there are śrī, vijaya, bhūti, and dhruvā nīti. The contested claim is not sports-team theology; it is that victory’s ecology is the conjunction of the Lord of yoga and the devoted doer of the word. Existentially, seek that conjunction, not isolated technique or isolated aggression. The Gītā ends not with withdrawal from the field but with a criterion for where thriving truly stands. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics. ### Key Terms
**saṃsmṛtya saṃsmṛtya (संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य)** — remembering again and again -> joy’s engine -> 'nostalgia' understates living recollection.
**yogeśvaraḥ kṛṣṇaḥ (योगेश्वरः कृष्णः)** — Krishna, Lord of yoga -> one pole of victory’s presence -> 'god on our side' is the corrupt hearing.
**pārtho dhanurdharaḥ (पार्थो धनुर्धरः)** — Pārtha the bow-bearer -> devoted actor pole -> 'heroic ego' is the corrupt hearing.
**dhruvā nīti (ध्रुवा नीति)** — lasting right policy/conduct -> fruit of that conjunction -> 'winning strategy' can be amoral counterfeit.
**śrīr vijayo bhūtiḥ (श्रीर्विजयो भूतिः)** — fortune, victory, thriving -> signs of the conjunction -> 'success gospel' is the danger.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Gītā 11 remembered]:** Sañjaya’s amaze at the form ties back to theophany.
*Divergence:* The ending pairs form-memory with dialogue-memory as twin joys.
**[Exodus / 'God with us' victory motifs]:** Divine presence as victory condition resonates.
*Divergence:* Biblical holy war frames differ; here presence is Krishna-plus-Arjuna specifically.
**[Mahābhārata’s dharma-war horizon]:** The verse places the Gītā back into the epic’s stakes.
*Divergence:* Sañjaya speaks to Dhṛtarāṣṭra: the theological criterion implicitly judges the Kaurava project.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Ask of your current struggle: 'Is the Lord of yoga present here—and am I present as one who will do the word?' If one pole is missing, restore it before seeking victory.

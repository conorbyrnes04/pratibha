## The Inverted Tree of Becoming
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.1

The Blessed Lord said: They speak of an eternal aśvattha tree with roots above and branches below, whose leaves are the Vedic hymns. Whoever knows it is a knower of the Veda.

---

### Devanagari
श्री भगवानुवाचऊर्ध्वमूलमधःशाखमश्वत्थं प्राहुरव्ययम्।छन्दांसि यस्य पर्णानि यस्तं वेद स वेदवित्

### IAST
śrī bhagavānuvācaūrdhvamūlamadhaḥśākhamaśvatthaṃ prāhuravyayam|chandāṃsi yasya parṇāni yastaṃ veda sa vedavit

### Pratibha Translation
The Blessed Lord said: They speak of an eternal aśvattha tree with roots above and branches below, whose leaves are the Vedic hymns. Whoever knows it is a knower of the Veda.

### Pratibha Commentary
The cosmos is imaged as an inverted sacred fig: roots above, branches below, leaves as Vedic chants. The contested move is epistemological and cosmological at once. Ordinary perception roots the world in the visible below; Krishna roots it in the unseen above. To know this tree is to be vedavit—not a technician of ritual sound, but one who understands what the hymns themselves are leaves of. The aśvattha is avyaya, enduring as saṃsāric process, yet knowable as a structure that can later be cut. Existentially, the verse asks whether you live as if meaning rises from sensory branches, or as if the nourishing root is above. The battlefield’s confusion is a canopy problem: too many leaves, forgotten root. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine.

### Key Terms
**aśvattha (अश्वत्थ)** — sacred fig / 'that which does not stand tomorrow' -> saṃsāra-tree -> 'world-tree' loses the name’s instability hint.
**ūrdhva-mūla (ऊर्ध्वमूल)** — root above -> transcendent sourceward orientation -> 'upside-down' as quirky image misses metaphysics.
**chandāṃsi (छन्दांसि)** — meters/Vedic hymns as leaves -> revelation as foliage of the tree -> 'poetry' understates liturgical cosmology.
**vedavit (वेदवित्)** — knower of Veda -> one who knows this tree -> 'scripture scholar' can miss the ontological knowing.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Kaṭha Upaniṣad II.3.1]:** The inverted aśvattha is near-direct source homology.
*Divergence:* Kaṭha emphasizes Brahman beyond; the Gītā will weaponize non-attachment as the axe.
**[Plato, cave allegory]:** Reversing the apparent source of reality resonates structurally.
*Divergence:* Plato ascends to forms; Krishna maps a cuttable tree of becoming.
**[Yggdrasil / world-tree myths]:** Cosmic tree as world-structure is cross-cultural.
*Divergence:* Those myths often stabilize cosmos; here the tree is to be known in order to be felled.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Visualize your worries as leaves. Ask once: 'Where is the root—below in events, or above?' Rest attention rootward for three minutes.

## Branches Everywhere, Axe of Non-Attachment
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.2–15.4

Its branches spread below and above, nourished by the guṇas, with sense-objects as twigs; and below, the roots extend into the world of humans, binding through action. Its form is not perceived here as such—no end, no beginning, no foundation. Having cut this deeply rooted aśvattha with the strong axe of non-attachment, one should then seek that goal from which, having gone, one does not return again, saying: 'I take refuge in that primal person from whom the ancient current streamed forth.'

---

### Devanagari
अधश्चोर्ध्वं प्रसृतास्तस्य शाखा     गुणप्रवृद्धा विषयप्रवालाः।अधश्च मूलान्यनुसन्ततानि     कर्मानुबन्धीनि मनुष्यलोके
न रूपमस्येह तथोपलभ्यते    नान्तो न चादिर्न च संप्रतिष्ठा।अश्वत्थमेनं सुविरूढमूल    मसङ्गशस्त्रेण दृढेन छित्त्वा
ततः पदं तत्परिमार्गितव्यम्

यस्मिन्गता न निवर्तन्ति भूयः।

तमेव चाद्यं पुरुषं प्रपद्ये

यतः प्रवृत्तिः प्रसृता पुराणी

### IAST
adhaścordhvaṃ prasṛtāstasya śākhā     guṇapravṛddhā viṣayapravālāḥ|adhaśca mūlānyanusantatāni     karmānubandhīni manuṣyaloke
na rūpamasyeha tathopalabhyate    nānto na cādirna ca saṃpratiṣṭhā|aśvatthamenaṃ suvirūḍhamūla    masaṅgaśastreṇa dṛḍhena chittvā
tataḥ padaṃ tatparimārgitavyam

yasmingatā na nivartanti bhūyaḥ|

tameva cādyaṃ puruṣaṃ prapadye

yataḥ pravṛttiḥ prasṛtā purāṇī

### Pratibha Translation
Its branches spread below and above, nourished by the guṇas, with sense-objects as twigs; and below, the roots extend into the world of humans, binding through action. Its form is not perceived here as such—no end, no beginning, no foundation. Having cut this deeply rooted aśvattha with the strong axe of non-attachment, one should then seek that goal from which, having gone, one does not return again, saying: 'I take refuge in that primal person from whom the ancient current streamed forth.'

### Pratibha Commentary
The tree thickens: guṇa-fed branches, sense-object shoots, and downward roots binding humans through karma. Yet its form cannot be grasped as a finished object—no end, beginning, or foundation available to ordinary seeing. The contested instruction is violent and precise: cut it with asaṅga-śastra, the axe of non-attachment, then seek the irreversible goal and take refuge in the primal person. Knowledge of the tree is not botanical tourism; it is preparatory to cutting and refuge. Existentially, non-attachment is not mood; it is a blade. Without refuge after the cut, one merely becomes homeless among falling leaves. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**asaṅga-śastra (असङ्गशस्त्र)** — weapon/axe of non-attachment -> cutting tool against the world-tree -> 'detachment' as vibe misses the blade.
**karmānubandhīni (कर्मानुबन्धीनि)** — binding through action -> downward roots in human world -> 'consequences' softens bondage.
**pada (पद)** — goal/standing-place -> that from which there is no return -> 'state' is too vague.
**prapadye (प्रपद्ये)** — I take refuge -> first-person surrender to the primal person -> 'believe in' is thinner than refuge.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Gītā 15.1 continuity with Kaṭha]:** Same tree, now explicitly cuttable by non-attachment.
*Divergence:* The Gītā adds karma-roots and explicit śaraṇa language.
**[John of the Cross, detachment]:** Cutting creaturely cling for God resonates.
*Divergence:* John’s nights are interior purification; Krishna’s axe follows a cosmological tree map.
**[Nagarjuna / pruning false establishments]:** Undoing falsely founded structures loosely parallels cutting the tree.
*Divergence:* Madhyamaka empties inherent existence; here one seeks a positive irreversible pada.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Name one clinging that feeds your tree. Practice one concrete cut today (a refusal), then immediately take refuge in the primal person for one minute.

## The Undying Place Beyond Sun and Fire
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.5–15.6

Without pride and delusion, having conquered the defect of attachment, constantly abiding in the Self, with desires turned away, freed from dualities called pleasure and pain—the undeluded go to that imperishable goal. That abode of Mine the sun does not light, nor the moon, nor fire; having gone there, they do not return. That is My supreme home.

---

### Devanagari
निर्मानमोहा जितसङ्गदोषा    अध्यात्मनित्या विनिवृत्तकामाः।द्वन्द्वैर्विमुक्ताः सुखदुःखसंज्ञै    र्गच्छन्त्यमूढाः पदमव्ययं तत्
न तद्भासयते सूर्यो न शशाङ्को न पावकः।यद्गत्वा न निवर्तन्ते तद्धाम परमं मम

### IAST
nirmānamohā jitasaṅgadoṣā    adhyātmanityā vinivṛttakāmāḥ|dvandvairvimuktāḥ sukhaduḥkhasaṃjñai    rgacchantyamūḍhāḥ padamavyayaṃ tat
na tadbhāsayate sūryo na śaśāṅko na pāvakaḥ|yadgatvā na nivartante taddhāma paramaṃ mama

### Pratibha Translation
Without pride and delusion, having conquered the defect of attachment, constantly abiding in the Self, with desires turned away, freed from dualities called pleasure and pain—the undeluded go to that imperishable goal. That abode of Mine the sun does not light, nor the moon, nor fire; having gone there, they do not return. That is My supreme home.

### Pratibha Commentary
The path-qualifications are listed as a single compound life: free of pride-delusion, attachment conquered, Self-abiding, desires quieted, freed from pleasure-pain dualities. Then the goal is imaged as Krishna’s supreme dhāman, which sun, moon, and fire do not illuminate. The contested claim is that the highest abode is not another lit object in the cosmos; it is the lighting-source beyond cosmic lamps. Non-return (na nivartante) seals irreversibility. Existentially, if your 'spiritual goals' still need the sun of status and the fire of intensity, you are still navigating branches. The supreme home is dark only to worldly lamps because it is too intimate to be their object. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**nir-māna-moha (निर्मानमोह)** — without pride and delusion -> purification before the goal -> 'humility' alone misses moha’s cognitive fog.
**padam avyayam (पदमव्ययम्)** — imperishable goal -> non-returning standing-place -> 'heaven' may mis-locate it.
**dhāman (धामन्)** — abode / home / light-sphere -> Krishna’s supreme dwelling -> 'place' can sound spatial-external.
**na nivartante (न निवर्तन्ते)** — they do not return -> irreversible liberation -> 'stay forever' can sound touristic.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Śvetāśvatara / Kaṭha light verses]:** Where sun/moon/fire do not shine is classic Upaniṣadic homology.
*Divergence:* The Gītā personalizes the abode as mama dhāma.
**[Pseudo-Dionysius, divine darkness]:** Beyond cosmic lights resonates with unknowing luminosity.
*Divergence:* Dionysius stresses negation; Krishna also lists ethical-yogic qualifications for entry.
**[Gītā 8.21]:** Non-returning supreme abode continues earlier teaching.
*Divergence:* Chapter 15 embeds it inside the tree-cutting sequence.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Sit in a darkened room. Contemplation: 'No sun, moon, or fire lights That.' Rest as the need for dramatic illumination softens.

## My Own Portion Become Living
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.7

A portion of Mine alone becomes the eternal living being in the world of the living, and draws to itself the senses, with the mind as the sixth, that rest in nature.

---

### Devanagari
ममैवांशो जीवलोके जीवभूतः सनातनः।मनःषष्ठानीन्द्रियाणि प्रकृतिस्थानि कर्षति

### IAST
mamaivāṃśo jīvaloke jīvabhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ|manaḥṣaṣṭhānīndriyāṇi prakṛtisthāni karṣati

### Pratibha Translation
A portion of Mine alone becomes the eternal living being in the world of the living, and draws to itself the senses, with the mind as the sixth, that rest in nature.

### Pratibha Commentary
The jīva is not an alien spark kidnapped by matter; it is mamaiva aṃśa—Krishna’s own portion—become living in the living world, eternal (sanātana). This portion draws the senses and mind (the sixth) that are stationed in prakṛti. The contested move is simultaneous intimacy and tension: you are the Lord’s portion, and you are the one yanking prakṛti’s instruments into a private drama. Bondage is not that an aṃśa exists; it is how it appropriates mind-senses. Existentially, this blocks both self-contempt and self-deification. You are not trash, and you are not the whole; you are a portion that can either clutch instruments or remember source. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**aṃśa (अंश)** — portion / share -> jīva as the Lord’s portion -> 'piece of God' can become crude pantheism.
**jīva-bhūta (जीवभूत)** — become a living being -> portion as embodied life -> 'soul incarnate' may smuggle foreign anthropology.
**karṣati (कर्षति)** — draws / drags -> appropriation of mind-senses -> 'uses' softens the dragging violence.
**manaḥ-ṣaṣṭha (मनःषष्ठ)** — mind as sixth -> mind grouped with senses as drawn instruments -> 'mind and five senses' as list misses the dragging.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Gītā 7.5 / 13.22]:** Jīva amid prakṛti continues the field-person analysis.
*Divergence:* 15.7 makes the jīva explicitly the Lord’s portion.
**[Rāmānuja on aṃśa-śeṣa]:** The soul as mode/portion of the Lord is theistic homology.
*Divergence:* Advaita readings stress apparent limitation; the verse’s 'draws the senses' keeps psychological realism.
**[Paul, 'body of Christ' membership]:** Portion-of-the-whole anthropology loosely resonates.
*Divergence:* Pauline membership is ecclesial; here the portion is metaphysical and sense-dragging.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
When craving pulls hard, note: 'A portion is dragging instruments.' Soften the drag for five breaths and remember the whole.

## Wind Taking Scents: The Traveler of Bodies
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.8–15.10

When the lord acquires a body and when he departs, he carries these and goes, as wind carries scents from their seat. Presiding over hearing, sight, touch, taste, and smell, and over the mind, he enjoys the sense-objects. The deluded do not see him departing, staying, or enjoying, endowed with the guṇas; those with the eye of knowledge see.

---

### Devanagari

उत्क्रामन्तं स्थितं वापि भुञ्जानं वा गुणान्वितम्।विमूढा नानुपश्यन्ति पश्यन्ति ज्ञानचक्षुषः

### Pratibha Commentary
Transmigration is imaged with sensory precision: the īśvara of the body carries mind-senses as wind carries fragrance from a seat. Embodiment is not a hard reset; it is transport of subtle instruments. While staying, he enjoys objects through the senses and mind. The deluded do not see departing, staying, or enjoying; jñāna-cakṣus does. The contested claim is that what is most intimate—enjoyership and migration—is invisible to confusion and obvious to knowledge’s eye. Existentially, this trains seeing the traveler in living faces, including your own: not a corpse-to-be only, but a fragrance-bearing lord of a temporary seat. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**utkrāmati (उत्क्रामति)** — goes up/out / departs -> leaving the body with instruments -> 'dies' erases the carrying.
**vāyur gandhān iva (वायुर्गन्धानिव)** — like wind with scents -> migration analogy -> 'spirit rises' is vaguer than scent-transport.
**guṇānvita (गुणान्वित)** — endowed with guṇas -> enjoyer as guṇa-clothed -> 'having qualities' misses binding dress.
**jñāna-cakṣus (ज्ञानचक्षुस्)** — eye of knowledge -> seeing the traveler -> 'insight' can stay psychological.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad IV.4]:** Departure carrying knowledge/karma is homologous.
*Divergence:* The Gītā’s wind-scent image and guṇa-clothing sharpen the phenomenology.
**[Tibetan subtle-body migration imagery]:** Continuity of subtle faculties across deaths resonates.
*Divergence:* Bardo systems detail channels and visions; Krishna compresses to scent-wind and seeing/not-seeing.
**[Plato, Phaedrus chariot]:** Soul traveling with powers loosely parallels carrying senses-mind.
*Divergence:* Plato mythologizes wings and heaven; Krishna emphasizes delusion versus knowledge-eye in ordinary living.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
In meditation, watch a sensation arise and fade like scent in wind. Then notice the enjoyer-function without denying it or becoming it.

## Yogins See; the Unready Do Not
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.11–15.13

Striving yogins also see him established in the Self; but the unready, even though striving, do not see him, lacking awareness. Know that brilliance in the sun that lights the whole world, and that in the moon and in fire, to be Mine. Entering the earth, I support beings with strength, and having become soma, savor itself, I nourish all plants.

---

### Devanagari
यतन्तो योगिनश्चैनं पश्यन्त्यात्मन्यवस्थितम्।यतन्तोऽप्यकृतात्मानो नैनं पश्यन्त्यचेतसः
यदादित्यगतं तेजो जगद्भासयतेऽखिलम्।यच्चन्द्रमसि यच्चाग्नौ तत्तेजो विद्धि मामकम्
गामाविश्य च भूतानि धारयाम्यहमोजसा।पुष्णामि चौषधीः सर्वाः सोमो भूत्वा रसात्मकः

### IAST
yatanto yoginaścainaṃ paśyantyātmanyavasthitam|yatanto'pyakṛtātmāno nainaṃ paśyantyacetasaḥ
yadādityagataṃ tejo jagadbhāsayate'khilam|yaccandramasi yaccāgnau tattejo viddhi māmakam
gāmāviśya ca bhūtāni dhārayāmyahamojasā|puṣṇāmi cauṣadhīḥ sarvāḥ somo bhūtvā rasātmakaḥ

### Pratibha Translation
Striving yogins also see him established in the Self; but the unready, even though striving, do not see him, lacking awareness. Know that brilliance in the sun that lights the whole world, and that in the moon and in fire, to be Mine. Entering the earth, I support beings with strength, and having become soma, savor itself, I nourish all plants.

### Pratibha Commentary
Effort alone does not guarantee seeing. Yogins who strive see the Self-established one; those with unready selves (akṛtātmanaḥ), even striving, do not—because they are acetasaḥ, not truly aware. Then Krishna widens identity into cosmic energies: solar, lunar, and fiery brilliance are his; he supports beings by entering the earth; as rasa-natured soma he nourishes plants. The contested pairing is interior seeing and exterior cosmology: the same Lord missed by unreadiness is the brilliance and the soil-strength you already live from. Existentially, unreadiness is not lack of hours on the cushion; it is an unworked self that can perform effort while remaining opaque. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**akṛtātman (अकृतात्मन्)** — whose self is unready/unmade -> effort without maturation -> 'undisciplined' is narrower.
**tejas (तेजस्)** — brilliance / energy -> solar-lunar-fire splendor as the Lord’s -> 'light' can become merely visual.
**ojas (ओजस्)** — vital strength -> supporting power in the earth -> 'force' misses living vigor.
**soma / rasātmaka (सोम / रसात्मक)** — soma as savor-nature -> nourishing essence of plants -> 'moon-plant juice' only is too botanical-literal.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Īśā / Śvetāśvatara cosmic pervasion]:** Lord as energy in sun/fire resonates.
*Divergence:* Here unreadiness explains why pervasion is missed.
**[Gītā 7.8–9]:** Krishna as taste, light, life continues.
*Divergence:* 15.11–13 ties that cosmology to who can see the indwelling one.
**[Gregory of Nyssa on spiritual senses]:** Needing formed senses to perceive God parallels readiness.
*Divergence:* Gregory’s formation is Christic; here readiness is yogic self-work amid cosmic theism.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Ask honestly: 'Is my effort paired with readiness, or with opacity?' Do one act of self-work (sleep, apology, or restraint) that increases readiness.

## I Digest as the Fire in All
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.14

Having become vaiśvānara, seated in the bodies of living beings, joined with in-breath and out-breath, I digest the fourfold food.

---

### Devanagari
अहं वैश्वानरो भूत्वा प्राणिनां देहमाश्रितः।प्राणापानसमायुक्तः पचाम्यन्नं चतुर्विधम्

### IAST
ahaṃ vaiśvānaro bhūtvā prāṇināṃ dehamāśritaḥ|prāṇāpānasamāyuktaḥ pacāmyannaṃ caturvidham

### Pratibha Translation
Having become vaiśvānara, seated in the bodies of living beings, joined with in-breath and out-breath, I digest the fourfold food.

### Pratibha Commentary
The Lord is not only sun and soil; he is vaiśvānara, the digestive fire in every body, married to prāṇa and apāna, cooking the four kinds of food. The contested intimacy is extreme: transcendence is metabolically immanent. Spirituality that despises digestion despises a divine act. Classical readers hear Agni-Vaiśvānara theology; modern readers can hear a refusal of body-rejecting mysticism. Existentially, each meal is already a theophany of sorts—not sentimental gratitude theater, but recognition that the cooker of food is the same Lord the unreadiness misses. The battlefield body Arjuna fears is also a hearth of the Lord. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**vaiśvānara (वैश्वानर)** — universal fire / digestive fire -> Lord as metabolizer in all -> 'stomach acid' parodies the theology.
**prāṇa-apāna (प्राणापान)** — forward and downward breaths -> respiratory pair joined to digestion -> 'breathing' alone misses the pair’s metabolic yoga.
**pacāmi (पचामि)** — I cook/digest -> first-person divine digestion -> 'nature digests' erases the claim.
**caturvidha anna (चतुर्विध अन्न)** — fourfold food -> all edible modes -> 'meals' is too casual.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Bṛhadāraṇyaka / Chāndogya Vaiśvānara]:** Upaniṣadic cosmic digestive fire is direct homology.
*Divergence:* The Gītā personalizes: ahaṃ ... pacāmi.
**[Christian Eucharist metabolisms of divine life]:** God feeding and being intimate with bodies resonates loosely.
*Divergence:* Eucharist is sacramental memorial/presence; here digestion itself is the Lord’s ongoing act.
**[Daoist body-gods / alchemy]:** Divinity in visceral processes has cross-tradition echoes.
*Divergence:* Daoist maps are many-spirited; Krishna asserts one Lord as the fire.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Before eating once today, silently: 'You digest this as the fire in me.' Eat slowly enough to mean it.

## I Am Seated in Every Heart
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.15

And I am seated in the heart of all; from Me come memory, knowledge, and their loss. I alone am to be known by all the Vedas; I am the author of Vedānta and the knower of the Veda.

---

### Devanagari
सर्वस्य चाहं हृदि सन्निविष्टो    मत्तः स्मृतिर्ज्ञानमपोहनं च।वेदैश्च सर्वैरहमेव वेद्यो    वेदान्तकृद्वेदविदेव चाहम्

### IAST
sarvasya cāhaṃ hṛdi sanniviṣṭo    mattaḥ smṛtirjñānamapohanaṃ ca|vedaiśca sarvairahameva vedyo    vedāntakṛdvedavideva cāham

### Pratibha Translation
And I am seated in the heart of all; from Me come memory, knowledge, and their loss. I alone am to be known by all the Vedas; I am the author of Vedānta and the knower of the Veda.

### Pratibha Commentary
The center shifts to the heart: Krishna is seated in all hearts; from him come smṛti, jñāna, and apohana—memory, knowledge, and removal/loss. The contested completeness is stunning: not only illumination but forgetting issues from the same source. Then the Veda claim: he alone is the knowable of all Vedas, maker of Vedānta, and knower of Veda. Scripture circles back into the heart-dweller. Existentially, this reinterprets both insight and amnesia as not merely neural accidents. The question becomes relational: what in my knowing and forgetting is refuge, and what is flight from the heart’s occupant? In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**hṛdi sanniviṣṭa (हृदि सन्निविष्ट)** — seated firmly in the heart -> intimate indwelling -> 'in my feelings' psychologizes the heart.
**apohana (अपोहन)** — removal / loss / negation -> forgetting as from the Lord -> 'amnesia' as pathology only misses the theological net.
**vedya (वेद्य)** — to be known -> Krishna as Veda’s knowable -> 'topic of scripture' is too academic.
**vedānta-kṛt (वेदान्तकृत्)** — maker of Vedānta -> author of the Veda’s end/teaching -> 'Vedānta philosopher' invents a schoolroom.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Kaṭha / Śvetāśvatara heart-dweller]:** Thumb-sized Self in the heart is homologous.
*Divergence:* The Gītā adds memory/knowledge/loss and Veda-authorship.
**[Augustine on God as inner teacher]:** Illumination from within resonates.
*Divergence:* Augustine includes forgetting differently; here apohana is explicitly from the Lord.
**[Gītā 10.20]:** Self as indwelling one continues.
*Divergence:* 15.15 intensifies by claiming Veda’s knowability and Vedānta’s authorship.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
When you forget something important, pause before self-attack: 'From Him also is loss.' Then ask what the heart is teaching through the gap.

## The Highest Person Beyond Perishable and Imperishable
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.16–15.18

There are two persons in the world: the perishable and the imperishable. The perishable is all beings; the imperishable is called the unchanging. But there is another, the highest person, called the supreme Self, who, entering the three worlds, supports them as the undying Lord. Because I transcend the perishable and am higher even than the imperishable, I am celebrated in the world and in the Veda as the Person Supreme.

---

### Devanagari
द्वाविमौ पुरुषौ लोके क्षरश्चाक्षर एव च।क्षरः सर्वाणि भूतानि कूटस्थोऽक्षर उच्यते
उत्तमः पुरुषस्त्वन्यः परमात्मेत्युदाहृतः।यो लोकत्रयमाविश्य बिभर्त्यव्यय ईश्वरः
यस्मात्क्षरमतीतोऽहमक्षरादपि चोत्तमः।अतोऽस्मि लोके वेदे च प्रथितः पुरुषोत्तमः

### IAST
dvāvimau puruṣau loke kṣaraścākṣara eva ca|kṣaraḥ sarvāṇi bhūtāni kūṭastho'kṣara ucyate
uttamaḥ puruṣastvanyaḥ paramātmetyudāhṛtaḥ|yo lokatrayamāviśya bibhartyavyaya īśvaraḥ
yasmātkṣaramatīto'hamakṣarādapi cottamaḥ|ato'smi loke vede ca prathitaḥ puruṣottamaḥ

### Pratibha Translation
There are two persons in the world: the perishable and the imperishable. The perishable is all beings; the imperishable is called the unchanging. But there is another, the highest person, called the supreme Self, who, entering the three worlds, supports them as the undying Lord. Because I transcend the perishable and am higher even than the imperishable, I am celebrated in the world and in the Veda as the Person Supreme.

### Pratibha Commentary
A triad replaces a duality. Kṣara is all beings; akṣara is the kūṭastha, the unchanging. Beyond both is uttama puruṣa / Paramātman / Puruṣottama, who enters and supports the three worlds. Krishna’s claim is comparative and absolute: transcending perishable and higher than imperishable, he is celebrated as Puruṣottama. The contested move is against two reductions: reducing the divine to changing nature, or to a static impersonal absolute that cannot love or support. Existentially, refuge aims not at becoming a frozen unchanging principle, nor at surfing perishability forever, but at the highest person who holds both. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**kṣara / akṣara (क्षर / अक्षर)** — perishable / imperishable -> two persons of the world -> 'mortal/immortal' is too coarse.
**kūṭastha (कूटस्थ)** — standing at the peak/anvil-fixed -> unchanging person -> 'immutable soul' may miss the technical term.
**Puruṣottama (पुरुषोत्तम)** — Person Supreme -> beyond both kṣara and akṣara -> 'God' without the triad loses the claim’s precision.
**bibharti (बिभर्ति)** — supports / bears -> active sustaining of three worlds -> 'exists behind' is too passive.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Śvetāśvatara III–VI]:** Higher than the perishable/imperishable motif appears in Upaniṣadic theism.
*Divergence:* The Gītā crystallizes the title Puruṣottama.
**[Rāmānuja’s tattva-traya]:** God, souls, matter as structured reality resonates.
*Divergence:* Exact mappings differ; the verse’s hinge is Krishna’s transcendence of both persons.
**[Christian Trinity debates on personhood]:** Personal Absolute beyond impersonal abstraction resonates loosely.
*Divergence:* Trinitarian grammar differs; here the triad is kṣara/akṣara/Puruṣottama.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Contemplate three layers in experience: changing beings, witnessing stillness, and the Lord who supports both. Rest in the third for two minutes.

## Knowing Me as Puruṣottama Completes the Work
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 15.19–15.20

Whoever, undeluded, thus knows Me as the Person Supreme, knows all and worships Me with his whole being, O Bhārata. This most secret teaching has been declared by Me, O sinless one; knowing this, one becomes wise and has done what is to be done.

---

### Devanagari
यो मामेवमसम्मूढो जानाति पुरुषोत्तमम्।स सर्वविद्भजति मां सर्वभावेन भारत
इति गुह्यतमं शास्त्रमिदमुक्तं मयाऽनघ।एतद्बुद्ध्वा बुद्धिमान्स्यात्कृतकृत्यश्च भारत

### IAST
yo māmevamasammūḍho jānāti puruṣottamam|sa sarvavidbhajati māṃ sarvabhāvena bhārata
iti guhyatamaṃ śāstramidamuktaṃ mayā'nagha|etadbuddhvā buddhimānsyātkṛtakṛtyaśca bhārata

### Pratibha Translation
Whoever, undeluded, thus knows Me as the Person Supreme, knows all and worships Me with his whole being, O Bhārata. This most secret teaching has been declared by Me, O sinless one; knowing this, one becomes wise and has done what is to be done.

### Pratibha Commentary
Undeluded knowing of Krishna as Puruṣottama yields sarva-vid—knowing all—and whole-being worship (sarva-bhāvena). The most secret śāstra ends in a completion formula: becoming buddhimān and kṛta-kṛtya, one who has done what needed doing. The contested claim is that metaphysical recognition and total devotion are one act, and that human incompleteness ends there—not in endless self-improvement. Existentially, 'knowing all' is not trivia mastery; it is knowing the One who ranks above perishable and imperishable, and letting that knowledge become worship with every bhāva. The secret is not information; it is undeluded recognition that finishes the task. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**asammūḍha (असम्मूढ)** — undeluded -> clarity required for Puruṣottama-knowing -> 'smart' is irrelevant.
**sarva-vid (सर्वविद्)** — all-knower -> fruit of knowing Puruṣottama -> 'omniscient' can sound mythic-inflated.
**sarva-bhāvena (सर्वभावेन)** — with one’s whole being/mode -> total worship -> 'enthusiastically' is too thin.
**kṛta-kṛtya (कृतकृत्य)** — one who has done what is to be done -> completion of human task -> 'successful' is worldly counterfeit.
**guhyatama śāstra (गुह्यतम शास्त्र)** — most secret teaching -> climax disclosure -> 'esoteric doctrine' can become clubbish.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Gītā 18.50–55 / 18.65–66]:** Completion and whole-being devotion foreshadow the finale.
*Divergence:* Chapter 15’s secret is Puruṣottama; chapter 18’s is refuge beyond all dharmas.
**[Muṇḍaka’s para vidyā]:** Highest knowledge completing the aim of life resonates.
*Divergence:* Muṇḍaka aims at Brahman; here completion is knowing and worshipping Puruṣottama.
**[Ignatius, 'take, Lord, receive']:** Whole-being offering parallels sarva-bhāva worship.
*Divergence:* Ignatian offering is Christic-mission; here the hinge is undeluded Puruṣottama recognition.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
At day’s end ask: 'What remains undone if I have not worshipped with my whole being?' Offer the residue in one honest bow.

## Faith Has Three Climates
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 17.1–17.2

Arjuna said: Those who discard scriptural injunction yet sacrifice filled with faith—what is their standing, Kṛṣṇa? Sattva, or rajas, or tamas? The Blessed Lord said: Faith in embodied beings is threefold, born of their own nature: sattvic, rajasic, and also tamasic. Hear of it.

---

### Devanagari
अर्जुन उवाचये शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य यजन्ते श्रद्धयाऽन्विताः।तेषां निष्ठा तु का कृष्ण सत्त्वमाहो रजस्तमः
श्री भगवानुवाचत्रिविधा भवति श्रद्धा देहिनां सा स्वभावजा।सात्त्विकी राजसी चैव तामसी चेति तां श्रृणु

### IAST
arjuna uvācaye śāstravidhimutsṛjya yajante śraddhayā'nvitāḥ|teṣāṃ niṣṭhā tu kā kṛṣṇa sattvamāho rajastamaḥ
śrī bhagavānuvācatrividhā bhavati śraddhā dehināṃ sā svabhāvajā|sāttvikī rājasī caiva tāmasī ceti tāṃ śrṛṇu

### Pratibha Translation
Arjuna said: Those who discard scriptural injunction yet sacrifice filled with faith—what is their standing, Kṛṣṇa? Sattva, or rajas, or tamas? The Blessed Lord said: Faith in embodied beings is threefold, born of their own nature: sattvic, rajasic, and also tamasic. Hear of it.

### Pratibha Commentary
Arjuna’s question is pastorally exact: what of sincere people who abandon śāstra yet sacrifice with śraddhā? Krishna refuses a binary of “faith good / lawlessness bad.” Śraddhā itself is trividhā and svabhāva-jā—threefold, born of one’s nature. The contested move is to subject faith to guṇa analysis. Sincerity does not automatically sanctify; climate colors trust. Existentially, this protects against two errors: condemning all extra-canonical devotion, and romanticizing intensity as truth. The next verses will show faith’s object and style as diagnostic of its guṇa. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**śraddhā (श्रद्धा)** — faith / trustful placing-of-heart -> threefold, nature-born -> 'belief' as assent understates lived trust.
**niṣṭhā (निष्ठा)** — standing / orientation -> Arjuna’s question about their stance -> 'denomination' is too institutional.
**svabhāva-jā (स्वभावजा)** — born of own nature -> faith arising from constitution -> 'personality preference' shrinks ontology.
**śāstra-vidhiṃ utsṛjya (शास्त्रविधिमुत्सृज्य)** — discarding scriptural injunction -> the problem-case -> 'spiritual but not religious' is a modern echo.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Gītā 16.23–24]:** Śāstra as measure sets up Arjuna’s question about faithful discarders.
*Divergence:* 17 opens the guṇa-internal analysis of faith itself.
**[Luther / sola fide debates (contrast)]:** Faith’s primacy is affirmed yet here faith is guṇa-qualified.
*Divergence:* Christian debates differ; Krishna will not treat all śraddhā as equal.
**[William James on varieties of religious experience]:** Varieties of faith-temperament resonate loosely.
*Divergence:* James is psychological-pragmatic; the Gītā maps varieties onto sattva/rajas/tamas.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Name your current faith-object (what you actually trust). Ask which climate it breathes: clarity, restless gain, or dark compulsion.

## A Person Is Made of Faith
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 17.3

The faith of each is in accordance with their essence, O Bhārata. A person is made of faith; whatever one’s faith is, that one verily is.

---

### Devanagari
सत्त्वानुरूपा सर्वस्य श्रद्धा भवति भारत।श्रद्धामयोऽयं पुरुषो यो यच्छ्रद्धः स एव सः

### IAST
sattvānurūpā sarvasya śraddhā bhavati bhārata|śraddhāmayo'yaṃ puruṣo yo yacchraddhaḥ sa eva saḥ

### Pratibha Translation
The faith of each is in accordance with their essence, O Bhārata. A person is made of faith; whatever one’s faith is, that one verily is.

### Pratibha Commentary
This is among the Gītā’s most compressed anthropologies: śraddhāmayo’yaṃ puruṣaḥ—this person is made of faith. Yo yacchraddhaḥ sa eva saḥ—as is one’s faith, so is one. The contested claim collapses the gap between “having beliefs” and “being.” You do not merely hold śraddhā; you are shaped as it. Existentially, this makes faith-diagnosis identity-diagnosis without freezing people into types. If you want to know who you are becoming, inspect what you entrust yourself to when pressed. The verse also explains conversion’s depth: changing faith is changing the person. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**sattva (सत्त्व)** — here as essence/being -> faith according to one’s sattva -> 'mood' is far too thin.
**śraddhāmaya (श्रद्धामय)** — made of faith -> person as faith-constituted -> 'faithful type' misses constitution.
**sa eva saḥ (स एव सः)** — that one verily is -> identity with one’s faith -> 'you are what you believe' is a rough cousin.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Proverbs / 'as a man thinketh' traditions]:** Inner trust shaping the person resonates.
*Divergence:* Biblical lines vary; here faith is explicitly guṇa-correlated.

**[Tillich’s 'ultimate concern']:** Being shaped by what one ultimately trusts is homologous.
*Divergence:* Tillich’s ontology of concern differs; the Gītā keeps guṇa typology concrete.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Complete the sentence: 'I am becoming what I trust about ___.' Change one trust-practice today to match who you intend to be.

## Whom One Worships Reveals the Guṇa
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 17.4–17.6

The sattvic sacrifice to the gods; the rajasic to yakṣas and rākṣasas; others, the tamasic people, sacrifice to ghosts and hosts of spirits. ## Food Preference as Guṇa Mirror
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 17.7–17.9

Even food dear to all is of three kinds, as are sacrifice, austerity, and giving; hear this distinction among them. Foods that increase life, sattva, strength, health, joy, and cheer, that are savory, smooth, substantial, and agreeable—these are dear to the sattvic. Foods that are bitter, sour, salty, excessively hot, pungent, dry, and burning—dear to the rajasic—produce pain, grief, and disease.

## Tamasic Sacrifice and the Triple Austerity
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 17.13–17.15

Sacrifice without ordinance, without food offered, without mantras, without gifts, and without faith is declared tamasic. Worship of gods, of the twice-born, of teachers, and of the wise; purity, uprightness, continence, and non-harming are called austerity of the body. Speech that causes no agitation, that is true, pleasant, and beneficial, and also practice of sacred study—these are called austerity of speech.

## Mental Austerity and Its Guṇa-Colors
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 17.16–17.18

Serenity of mind, gentleness, silence, self-restraint, purification of disposition—these are called austerity of the mind. This threefold austerity practiced with highest faith by people joined to yoga, not craving fruit, is declared sattvic. Austerity practiced for esteem, honor, and reverence, and also with hypocrisy—said to be rajasic, unstable and impermanent.

---

### Devanagari

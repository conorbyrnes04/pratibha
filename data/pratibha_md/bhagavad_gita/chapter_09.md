## The Royal Secret That Liberates
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 9.1–9.3

The Blessed Lord said: To you who do not cavil I will declare this most secret knowledge joined with realization, knowing which you will be freed from what is inauspicious. This is royal knowledge, the royal secret, purifying, supreme, directly experienceable, aligned with dharma, easy to practice, and imperishable. People without faith in this dharma, O Scorcher of Foes, failing to reach Me, return on the path of death-saṃsāra.

### Pratibha Commentary
The most secret teaching is not withheld as elite puzzle; it is given to the non-caviling and joined to vijñāna so that knowing frees from aśubha. Calling it rājavidyā and rājaguhya crowns knowledge as sovereign while insisting it is purifying, directly ascertainable, dharmic, easy to practice, and imperishable. The contested move is that the royal secret is easy to practice yet refused by the faithless, who cycle in death-saṃsāra. Existentially this cuts two errors: treating liberation-knowledge as grim technicality, and treating ease as shallowness. Faith is the condition of arrival; without it, even hearing the secret returns you to the mortal loop. ## Pervading Yet Not Contained
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 9.4–9.6

By Me, in My unmanifest form, all this world is pervaded. All beings rest in Me; I do not rest in them. And yet beings do not rest in Me — behold My sovereign yoga! Supporting beings, I do not dwell in them; My Self is the source of beings. As the great wind resting in space goes everywhere, so all beings rest in Me — understand this.

## Cycles of Emission Without Binding
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 9.7–9.9

All beings enter My nature at a kalpa's end, O Son of Kuntī; at a kalpa's beginning I emit them again. Resting on My own nature, I emit again and again this whole helpless host of beings, under nature's control. And those actions do not bind Me, O Winner of Wealth; I sit as one unattached, like one seated as indifferent amid those actions.

## Nature Under Witness — Fools Scorn

## Become Mind-of-Me
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 9.34

Become one whose mind is on Me, My devotee, My sacrificer; bow to Me. Joining yourself thus, intent on Me as supreme, you will come to Me alone.

### Pratibha Translation
Become one whose mind is on Me, My devotee, My sacrificer; bow to Me. Joining yourself thus, intent on Me as supreme, you will come to Me alone.

### Pratibha Commentary
Chapter 9 closes as imperative cascade: mind on Me, My devotee, My sacrificer, bow to Me — then joining with Me as supreme aim guarantees coming to Me alone. The contested simplicity is that the royal secret culminates not in more cosmology but in a form of life aimed entirely at the Lord. Existentially this is the exam: not whether you can explain sovereign yoga, but whether mind, devotion, offering, and bow are becoming one orientation. The teaching's force is practical: what it claims about reality must reshape attention and choice, or it remains ornamental.

## Renouncer Is Not the Fireless Idle
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.1–6.3

The Blessed Lord said: Who does the action that must be done without depending on action’s fruit — that one is a renouncer and a yogin, not the one without fire and not the inactive. What they call renunciation, know that as yoga, Pāṇḍava; for no one becomes a yogin without renouncing intention. For the sage wishing to ascend to yoga, action is said to be the means; for the same one who has ascended to yoga, stillness is said to be the means.

---

### Devanagari

श्री भगवानुवाच

अनाश्रितः कर्मफलं कार्यं कर्म करोति यः।

स संन्यासी च योगी च न निरग्निर्न चाक्रियः

यं संन्यासमिति प्राहुर्योगं तं विद्धि पाण्डव।

न ह्यसंन्यस्तसङ्कल्पो योगी भवति कश्चन

आरुरुक्षोर्मुनेर्योगं कर्म कारणमुच्यते।

योगारूढस्य तस्यैव शमः कारणमुच्यते

### IAST

śrī bhagavānuvāca

anāśritaḥ karmaphalaṃ kāryaṃ karma karoti yaḥ|

sa saṃnyāsī ca yogī ca na niragnirna cākriyaḥ

yaṃ saṃnyāsamiti prāhuryogaṃ taṃ viddhi pāṇḍava|

na hyasaṃnyastasaṅkalpo yogī bhavati kaścana

ārurukṣormuneryogaṃ karma kāraṇamucyate|

yogārūḍhasya tasyaiva śamaḥ kāraṇamucyate

### Pratibha Translation

The Blessed Lord said: Who performs the action that must be done without depending on the fruit of action — that one is a renouncer and a yogin — not the fireless one, and not the inactive. What they call renunciation, know as yoga, Pāṇḍava; for no one becomes a yogin without having renounced intention [saṅkalpa]. For the sage wishing to climb to yoga, action is said to be the means; for that same one who has climbed to yoga, stillness [śama] is said to be the means.

### Pratibha Commentary

Chapter 6 opens by collapsing false images of holiness: neither niragni nor akrīya defines saṃnyāsa/yoga. The contested identity is positive — do kārya without āśraya on fruit — and then conceptual: saṃnyāsa is yoga because without renounced saṅkalpa no one is a yogin. Stage-theory follows: for the ārurukṣu, karma is the means; for the yogārūḍha, śama is the means. In pedagogical yoga this prevents beginners from aping the stillness of the ascended, and prevents the ascended from clinging to beginner activism as identity. Existentially, locate your stage honestly. If you still need climbing, act without fruit-dependence; if you have climbed, let stillness be means without contempt for those still acting. Costume without stage-fit is failure. Stage-fit saves practice from costume. Beginners aping stillness skip the karma that climbs; the ascended clinging to activism refuse the śama that is now their means. Saṅkalpa-renunciation is the common root that makes both stages yoga rather than career choices. Neither fireless inactivity nor fruit-dependent work earns the names renouncer and yogin. Locate your stage, then use the means that actually belong to it.

### Key Terms

**niragni (निरग्नि)** — without (sacrificial) fire -> false emblem of renunciation -> ritual absence mistaken for yoga

**saṅkalpa (सङ्कल्प)** — formative intention/resolve -> what must be renounced to be a yogin -> deeper than quitting tasks

**yogārūḍha (योगारूढ)** — one who has ascended to yoga -> stage where śama becomes the means -> marks a real transition of method

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**John of the Cross on active vs passive nights:** Structural homology in different means proper to different stages of ascent.
*Divergence:* Carmelite staging is christological-mystical; the Gītā stages karma then śama under saṅkalpa-renunciation.

**Confucian graded learning vs quiet sitting debates:** Structural homology in refusing premature stillness as moral excellence.
*Divergence:* Confucian framing is ritual-moral cultivation; here stillness is explicitly the means of the already yoga-ascended.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Decide whether you are climbing or ascended for today: if climbing, complete one necessary action without leaning on its fruit; if ascended, take one period of deliberate stillness without using it to judge actors.

## When Nothing Clings, Yoga Has Ascended
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.4

When one no longer clings to sense-objects or to actions, having renounced all intentions — then one is said to have ascended to yoga.

---

### Devanagari

यदा हि नेन्द्रियार्थेषु न कर्मस्वनुषज्जते।

सर्वसङ्कल्पसंन्यासी योगारूढस्तदोच्यते

### IAST

yadā hi nendriyārtheṣu na karmasvanuṣajjate|

sarvasaṅkalpasaṃnyāsī yogārūḍhastadocyate

### Pratibha Translation

For when one does not cling to sense-objects or to actions, renouncer of all intentions — then one is called yoga-ascended.

### Pratibha Commentary

The definition of yogārūḍha is negative-positive: non-clinging to indriyārtha and to karmas, plus total saṅkalpa-saṃnyāsa. The contested clarity is diagnostic — ascent is not measured by hours seated but by the end of anuṣaṅga (adhesive following) toward objects and deeds, and by the emptying of formative resolves. In yoga psychology this is liberation’s behavioral signature inside ordinary life. Existentially, inventory cling-points: which objects, which projects, which resolutions still own you? When those adhesives release, you may use the name yogārūḍha without fraud. Non-clinging to objects and actions plus total intention-renunciation is a public-private definition of ascent: visible adhesives and invisible resolves both release. Hours on a seat cannot override anuṣaṅga that still owns your projects. The name yogārūḍha is honest only when the cling-inventory is empty. Let that inventory, not your self-branding, decide whether stillness is yet your proper means. The contested force of the passage remains practical: it asks for a change in how you inhabit action, attention, and relation — not for ornamental agreement. Return to the line until the claim bites a live decision today.

### Key Terms

**anuṣajjate (अनुषज्जते)** — clings/follows adhesively -> the verb whose cessation marks ascent -> more precise than “likes”

**sarvasaṅkalpasaṃnyāsī (सर्वसङ्कल्पसंन्यासी)** — renouncer of all intentions -> comprehensive dropping of formative resolve -> the positive content of ascent

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Zen “no dwelling” (Diamond Sūtra echoes):** Structural homology in non-abiding amid objects and acts.
*Divergence:* Zen non-abiding is emptiness-flavored; here non-clinging is definitional of yogārūḍha within Gītā staging.

**Stoic release of hormē toward externals:** Structural homology in ending adhesive impulse toward things and outcomes.
*Divergence:* Stoicism retains rational assent structure; the Gītā centers total saṅkalpa-renunciation as yoga-ascent.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

List three current saṅkalpas; release one completely for twenty-four hours and watch whether sense-objects and tasks lose their adhesive pull.

## Lift the Self by the Self
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.5

One should lift the self by the Self, and not degrade the self; for the self alone is the self’s friend, and the self alone is the self’s enemy.

---

### Devanagari

उद्धरेदात्मनाऽऽत्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत्।

आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः

### IAST

uddharedātmanā''tmānaṃ nātmānamavasādayet|

ātmaiva hyātmano bandhurātmaiva ripurātmanaḥ

### Pratibha Translation

One should raise the self by the Self, and should not cause the self to sink; for the self alone is friend of the self, and the self alone is enemy of the self.

### Pratibha Commentary

Responsibility returns inward with shocking symmetry: uplift and degradation are both self-to-self relations. The contested claim refuses permanent outsourcing of salvation or blame — ātman alone is bandhu and ripu. In yogic ethics this is empowerment without narcissism: the same “self” that sabotages can be the instrument of raising, and the deeper Self is the lever. Existentially, notice the moment you treat yourself as helpless victim of yourself while still choosing the sinking move. The verse commands uddharet — lift — and forbids avasādayet — do not sink yourself. Friendship with yourself is an achievement of discipline, not a feeling. Self-as-friend and self-as-enemy refuse permanent outsourcing of either blame or rescue. The same agency that chooses sinking can choose lifting; the deeper Self is the lever of uplift. This is discipline’s empowerment, not pep talk. Notice the moment you rehearse helplessness while still selecting the degrading move. Friendship with yourself is built by the lift you actually perform.

### Key Terms

**uddharet (उद्धरेत्)** — should lift up -> imperative of self-raising -> agency located in self-Self relation

**avasādayet (अवसादयेत्)** — should sink/depress -> the forbidden self-sabotage -> names degradation as active

**bandhu / ripu (बन्धु / रिपु)** — friend / enemy -> twin roles the self plays toward itself -> moral psychology as interior alliance or war

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Epictetus on self-responsibility of prohairesis:** Structural homology in locating friend/enemy in one’s own governing choices.
*Divergence:* Stoic hegemonikon differs from Gītā’s ātman/Self double play toward yoga uplift.

**Hasidic “working on oneself”:** Structural homology in refusing to leave the inner enemy unconfronted.
*Divergence:* Hasidic work is God-relational within mitsvot; here the formula is terse Self-by-self uplift for yoga.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Catch one self-sinking move (rumination, spite, indulgence); interrupt it with one concrete self-raising act chosen as friendship, not as punishment.

## Conquered Self Is Friend; Unconquered Is Foe
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.6

The self is friend to that self by whom the self itself has been conquered; but for the unconquered self, the self would stand in enmity like an enemy.

---

### Devanagari

बन्धुरात्माऽऽत्मनस्तस्य येनात्मैवात्मना जितः।

अनात्मनस्तु शत्रुत्वे वर्तेतात्मैव शत्रुवत्

### IAST

bandhurātmā''tmanastasya yenātmaivātmanā jitaḥ|

anātmanastu śatrutve vartetātmaiva śatruvat

### Pratibha Translation

The self is a friend to the self of one by whom the self has been conquered by the self; but for the one without self-mastery, the self would operate in enmity — the self itself like an enemy.

### Pratibha Commentary

Friendship with oneself is conditional on jita — conquest of self by self. The contested severity is that without mastery (anātman here as unmastered person), the self behaves as enemy. This is not self-hatred theology; it is diagnostic of interior civil war when discipline fails. In yoga, “self” is both the field to be conquered and the conqueror — a reflexive martial ethics. Existentially, if your impulses systematically ambush your aims, you do not need a new enemy outside; you need conquest that turns the inner actor into bandhu. Until then, expect sabotage from the nearest agent: you. Conditional friendship means mastery transforms the inner relation: conquered self becomes bandhu; unmastered self operates like an enemy. Interior civil war is then not mysterious fate but the predictable climate of non-conquest. Yoga’s reflexive martial ethics — self conquering self — aims at alliance, not at self-hatred. If impulses ambush aims, the nearest enemy is already in the house. Conquer once today and feel the alliance begin.

### Key Terms

**jitātman (implied जित)** — conquered self -> condition for self-friendship -> mastery as relational transformation

**anātman (अनात्मन्)** — here: one lacking self-possession/mastery -> not metaphysical non-self doctrine in this verse’s use -> practical unmastered person

**śatruvat (शत्रुवत्)** — like an enemy -> simile for unmastered self’s operation -> makes interior conflict vivid

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Plato’s Republic (city-soul justice as inner rule):** Structural homology in friendship/peace requiring right rule within.
*Divergence:* Plato’s parts are logistikon/thumos/epithumia; the Gītā compresses to self-conquest by self for yoga.

**Freud’s structural conflict (popularly):** Structural homology in self opposed to itself.
*Divergence:* Psychoanalysis theorizes unconscious agencies; Krishna commands conquest as yogic ethical task.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Choose one recurrent self-enemy behavior; conquer it once today by a pre-decided counter-act, and note the shift from enmity toward friendship.

## Equal in Extremes, Equal in Gold and Clod
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.7–6.9

For the self-conquered, the peaceful, the supreme Self is collected in cold and heat, pleasure and pain, and likewise in honor and dishonor. The one satisfied in knowledge and realization, standing on the peak, senses conquered — that yogin is called integrated, to whom clod, stone, and gold are equal. Surpassing is the one of equal understanding toward well-wishers, friends, enemies, neutrals, mediators, the hateful, and kin — and toward the good and the evil as well.

---

### Devanagari

जितात्मनः प्रशान्तस्य परमात्मा समाहितः।

शीतोष्णसुखदुःखेषु तथा मानापमानयोः

ज्ञानविज्ञानतृप्तात्मा कूटस्थो विजितेन्द्रियः।

युक्त इत्युच्यते योगी समलोष्टाश्मकाञ्चनः

सुहृन्मित्रार्युदासीनमध्यस्थद्वेष्यबन्धुषु।

साधुष्वपि च पापेषु समबुद्धिर्विशिष्यते

### IAST

jitātmanaḥ praśāntasya paramātmā samāhitaḥ|

śītoṣṇasukhaduḥkheṣu tathā mānāpamānayoḥ

jñānavijñānatṛptātmā kūṭastho vijitendriyaḥ|

yukta ityucyate yogī samaloṣṭāśmakāñcanaḥ

suhṛnmitrāryudāsīnamadhyasthadveṣyabandhuṣu|

sādhuṣvapi ca pāpeṣu samabuddhirviśiṣyate

### Pratibha Translation

For the self-conquered, the peaceful, the supreme Self is gathered in cold-heat, pleasure-pain, and honor-dishonor. Satisfied in knowledge and realization, peak-steady, senses conquered — that yogin is called integrated, to whom clod, stone, and gold are the same. Distinguished is the one of equal understanding toward ally, friend, enemy, neutral, mediator, the hateful, and kin — and toward the good and the sinful alike.

### Pratibha Commentary

Paramātman is samāhita for the jita and praśānta across climatic, hedonic, and status pairs. Yukta is further marked by jñāna-vijñāna satisfaction, kūṭastha steadiness, conquered senses, and value-equality of loṣṭa-aśma-kāñcana. Then the social summit: samabuddhi across the full relational spectrum, including sādhu and pāpa. The contested claim is that integration is tested not in cave silence but in honor’s volatility and in human ambivalence. Existentially, gold vs clod and friend vs foe are the exams. If your buddhi still surges with status and faction, the supreme Self is not yet collected in you. Equality across climate, pleasure, status, substances, and social factions is the phenomenological proof that Paramātman is collected and the yogin yoked. Gold-versus-clod and friend-versus-foe are not optional advanced tests; they are where kūṭastha steadiness shows. Samabuddhi that includes the sinner prevents holiness from becoming factional anesthesia. If honor and hatred still swing your buddhi, integration remains incomplete. Let extremes examine you.

### Key Terms

**kūṭastha (कूटस्थ)** — standing on the peak/immovable -> steadiness beyond fluctuation -> mountain-peak metaphor for consciousness

**samaloṣṭāśmakāñcana (समलोष्टाश्मकाञ्चन)** — equal toward clod, stone, gold -> value-equality of substances -> dismantles greed’s ontology

**samabuddhi (समबुद्धि)** — equal understanding -> impartial cognition across persons -> ethical flower of yoga equality

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Marcus Aurelius on indifferent materials and difficult people:** Structural homology in equal mind across status goods and social types.
*Divergence:* Stoic cosmopolitanism differs from Gītā kūṭastha-paramātman collection language.

**Mengzi / Confucian impartial care debates:** Structural homology in extending regard beyond faction.
*Divergence:* Confucian graded love often remains; the Gītā’s samabuddhi explicitly includes enemy and sinner in equal understanding.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Hold equal mind for ten minutes toward a valued object and a trivial one, then toward a liked and disliked person, without forcing fake affection — only refusing buddhi’s wild upgrade/downgrade.

## Alone, on a Clean Seat, for Self-Purification
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.10–6.12

Let the yogin yoke the self continually, staying in solitude, alone, mind and self restrained, without longing, without grasping. Establishing for oneself a firm seat in a clean place — not too high, not too low — with cloth, skin, and kuśa layered. There, making the mind one-pointed, with thought and sense-activity restrained, sitting on the seat, one should practice yoga for purification of the self.

---

### Devanagari

Meditation here is infrastructural before it is mystical: solitude, aloneness, restrained citta-ātman, nirāśī, aparigraha; then a śuci place, stable āsana of measured height, layered seat; then ekāgra mind and yoked practice for ātma-viśuddhi. Infrastructure before mystique: solitude, non-grasping, clean mid-height seat, one-pointed mind, purification aim. Continual yoking matters more than occasional fireworks. If longing and clutter still set the room’s weather, insight claims will keep collapsing. Build conditions that make return possible. Dedicate sitting to ātma-viśuddhi so experience-collecting cannot hijack the session. The contested force of the passage remains practical: it asks for a change in how you inhabit action, attention, and relation — not for ornamental agreement. ### Key Terms

**ekāgra (एकाग्र)** — one-pointed -> mind made single-tipped -> technical condition of practice on the seat

**aparigraha (अपरिग्रह)** — non-grasping -> outer condition of the yogin’s solitude -> links meditation to non-possessiveness

**ātmaiviśuddhi (आत्मविशुद्धि)** — purification of the self -> purpose of yuñjīta yoga -> teleology of the seating instructions

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Desert monastic cell discipline:** Structural homology in solitude, measured place, and purification aim.
*Divergence:* Monastic cell is christological-ascetic; here the seat-layers and ekāgra mind serve yogic ātma-viśuddhi.

**Patañjali’s āsana-sthira-sukha lineage:** Structural homology in stable seating as base for interior work.
*Divergence:* Patañjali systematizes eight limbs; the Gītā compresses a practical vignette within dhyāna-yoga teaching.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Prepare a clean, mid-height seat; sit alone without phone for fifteen minutes, aim mind to one point, and dedicate the sitting solely to purification rather than to results.

## Body Aligned, Mind on Me
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.13–6.15

Holding body, head, and neck aligned, motionless and steady, gazing at the tip of one’s nose without looking around — with peaceful self, fear gone, established in the vow of brahmacarya, mind restrained, thought on me, integrated, let one sit devoted to me as highest. Yoking the self thus always, the yogin of controlled mind attains peace that is the supreme nirvāṇa, established in me.

---

Technique without devotion is incomplete here; devotion without bodily steadiness is also incomplete. Existentially, let the spine teach the mind, and let the mind’s content be the Lord rather than self-monitoring vanity. Continual yoking yields peace that is not mood but establishment. Aligned body and nose-tip gaze are not ends; they serve mac-citta and mat-para sitting that ripens into peace as abiding in the Lord. ### Key Terms

**maccitta (मच्चित्त)** — mind on me / thought formed of me -> theistic content of meditation -> specifies what ekāgratā is for

**matsaṃsthā (मत्संस्था)** — abiding/establishment in me -> the locus of supreme nirvāṇa-peace -> personalist seal on meditative attainment

**brahmacārivrata (ब्रह्मचारिव्रत)** — vow of brahmacarya -> disciplined sexual/energetic continence supporting sitting -> ethical base of dhyāna

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

*Divergence:* Sufi love-idiom and silsila differ; Gītā specifies nose-gaze and brahmacarya-vrata in epic yoga idiom.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Sit aligned and still; rest gaze toward the nose tip without strain; when mind wanders, return it as “on You,” and end by sensing peace as abiding rather than as achievement.

## Yoga Fails Extremes of Food and Sleep
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.16

Yoga is not for one who eats too much or who does not eat at all, nor for one who sleeps too much or who stays forever awake, Arjuna.

---

### Devanagari

### IAST

na cātisvapnaśīlasya jāgrato naiva cārjuna

### Pratibha Translation

Yoga is not for the overeater, nor for one who entirely refrains from eating, nor for one excessively given to sleep, nor for the one who stays awake without measure, Arjuna.

### Pratibha Commentary

Yoga is incompatible with extremity in the basic biological rhythms of eating and sleeping. The contested claim attacks both indulgence and showy deprivation: atyaśnata and ekāntam anaśnata alike miss yoga; so do oversleep and compulsive vigil. In classical discipline this is madhyama as physiological wisdom, not mediocrity. Existentially, check whether your “spiritual intensity” is actually dysregulated appetite or sleep. The path that ignores the organism’s measure will not yield the mind’s measure. Extremes of eating and sleeping sabotage yoga whether they look like indulgence or like heroic deprivation. The organism’s measure is part of the path’s intelligence. Dysregulated appetite or vigil often masquerades as spiritual intensity while making citta untrainable. Restore measure before blaming the teaching. Yoga is incompatible with a life that refuses biological sanity. The contested force of the passage remains practical: it asks for a change in how you inhabit action, attention, and relation — not for ornamental agreement. ### Key Terms

**atyaśnata (अत्यश्नत्)** — overeating one -> extremity that blocks yoga -> body excess as spiritual obstacle

**ekāntam anaśnataḥ (एकान्तमनश्नत्)** — entirely not eating -> opposite extremity also blocking -> austerity theater without yoga

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Aristotle’s mean in bodily habits:** Structural homology in virtue as measured middle against twin vices.
*Divergence:* Aristotelian ethics aims at eudaimonia in the polis; here measure is specifically condition for yoga.

**Buddha’s Middle Way after extreme asceticism:** Structural homology in rejecting starvation vigil as awakening’s method.
*Divergence:* Buddhist Middle Way is path to nibbāna; the Gītā frames measure as prerequisite of dhyāna-yoga.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

## Infinite Happiness Beyond the Senses
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.21–6.23

Keep yoking without theatrical hope or theatrical quit. Yoga as disentangling from pain-conjunction is also ultimate happiness known by buddhi beyond senses — positive and negative faces of one attainment. Unshakeability under heavy sorrow and refusal of greater-gain thinking are marks, not slogans. Practice then requires determination with undespairing mind, blocking both fragile enthusiasm and bitter quit. Keep yoking when sorrow is heavy. **duḥkhasaṃyogaviyoga (दुःखसंयोगवियोग)** — disjunction from pain-conjunction -> technical definition of yoga here -> yoga as unbinding from suffering’s grip

**anirviṇṇacetas (अनिर्विण्णचेतस्)** — undespairing mind -> affective condition of practice -> endurance without bitterness

**Boethius on happiness unshaken by fortune:** Structural homology in a good not surpassed and not overthrown by sorrow.

## Slowly, Slowly, Think Nothing
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 6.24–6.25

In meditation pedagogy this is gold: ending thought is the fruit of establishment, not the first forced act. Existentially, if you try to think nothing while desires still author the mind, you only breed underground saṅkalpa. Drop desire-seeds, herd the senses, settle slowly, then let thinking thin. Gradualism is doctrinal: slowly, slowly come to rest with firm-held buddhi after desires born of intention are abandoned and senses herded by mind. Thinking nothing is culmination after Self-establishment, not the first violent act. Forced blanking while saṅkalpa still authors the mind breeds underground wanting. Drop seeds, restrain the flock, settle gradually, then let thought thin. Firmness holds the process without haste.

### Key Terms

**śanaiḥ śanaiḥ (शनैः शनैः)** — slowly, slowly -> mandatory gradualism of resting -> against abrupt suppression fantasies

**dhṛtigṛhītā buddhi (धृतिगृहीता बुद्धि)** — understanding held by firmness -> the grip that allows gradual rest -> steadiness as method

**na kiñcid api cintayet (न किञ्चिदपि चिन्तयेत्)** — should not think anything at all -> culmination after Self-establishment -> not first technique

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

*Divergence:* Cloud is apophatic Christian; Gītā specifies saṅkalpa-desire abandonment and sense-herding first.

*Divergence:* Those systems have distinct view-practices; here gradual uparama is explicit scriptural method.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Drop one intention-born desire, restrain senses with mind for a few minutes, then settle “slowly, slowly” into Self-abiding until thoughts thin — without attacking thoughts as enemies.

### IAST

prayatnādyatamānastu yogī saṃśuddhakilbiṣaḥ|

anekajanmasaṃsiddhastato yāti parāṃ gatim

tapasvibhyo'dhiko yogī jñānibhyo'pi mato'dhikaḥ|

karmibhyaścādhiko yogī tasmādyogī bhavārjuna

yogināmapi sarveṣāṃ madgatenāntarātmanā|

śraddhāvānbhajate yo māṃ sa me yuktatamo mataḥ

### Pratibha Translation

But the yogin striving with effort, thoroughly cleansed of evil, perfected through many births, then goes to the highest goal. The yogin is higher than the ascetics, and thought higher even than the knowers; higher than the ritual-actors too is the yogin — therefore be a yogin, Arjuna. And of all yogins, the one who worships me with faith, with the inner self gone to me — that one is thought by me the most integrated.

### Pratibha Commentary

The chapter’s encouragement climaxes in long horizon: prayatna-striving, cleansed kilbiṣa, aneka-janma-saṃsiddhi, then parā gati. Ranking follows — yogin above tapasvin, jñānin, and karmin — hence the imperative: yogī bhava. Then the innermost rank: among yogins, yuktatama is the śraddhāvant who bhajati Krishna with antarātman gone to him (madgata). The contested hierarchy refuses both anti-yoga intellectualism and yoga without devotion. Existentially, take the long view of many births without postponing today’s effort, and let integration culminate as devoted inwardness, not as technique pride. The most yoked is the one whose inner self has gone to the Lord in faith-filled worship. Many-birth perfection joins today’s effortful cleansing: long horizon without postponement. Ranking the yogin above ascetic, knower, and ritual-actor culminates in the imperative to be a yogin — then ranks again among yogins by faithful worship with inner self gone to the Lord. Technique pride loses; devoted inwardness wins the title yuktatama. Take the long view and today’s yoking together. Integration’s summit is madgata faith, not display of limbs.

### Key Terms

**anekajanmasaṃsiddha (अनेकजन्मसंसिद्ध)** — perfected through many births -> long-horizon completion -> comfort and demand together

**yuktatama (युक्ततम)** — most integrated/yoked -> ranked by madgata antarātman devotion -> theistic summit of yoga chapter

**madgata (मद्गत)** — gone to me -> inner self’s destination in the Lord -> marks devotion as yoga’s highest integration

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Bhakti climax pattern (Gītā 9, 12, 18):** Structural homology in knowledge/yoga consummated as devoted refuge.
*Divergence:* Later chapters expand; here chapter 6 already seals dhyāna with yuktatama as faithful worshiper.

**Augustine’s “our heart is restless until it rests in you”:** Structural homology in technique and striving finding rest in personal divine destination.
*Divergence:* Augustinian rest is trinitarian grace; Gītā yuktatama is madgata antarātman with śraddhā-bhajana among yogins.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

End practice by becoming a yogin twice: once as effortful yoking cleaned of one small evil, and once as faithful worship with the inner self offered to the Lord — without ranking yourself against others.

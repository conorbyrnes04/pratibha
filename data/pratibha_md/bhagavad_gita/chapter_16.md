## The Inventory of Divine Wealth
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 16.1–16.3

The Blessed Lord said: Fearlessness, purity of sattva, steadfastness in the yoga of knowledge, giving, self-restraint, sacrifice, study, austerity, uprightness; non-harming, truth, absence of anger, renunciation, peace, absence of slander, compassion for beings, freedom from greed, gentleness, modesty, absence of restlessness; vigor, forgiveness, fortitude, purity, absence of malice, absence of excessive pride—these belong to one born to the divine endowment, O Bhārata.

---

### Devanagari

अभयं सत्त्वसंशुद्धिः ज्ञानयोगव्यवस्थितिः।

दानं दमश्च यज्ञश्च स्वाध्यायस्तप आर्जवम्
अहिंसा सत्यमक्रोधस्त्यागः शान्तिरपैशुनम्।दया भूतेष्वलोलुप्त्वं मार्दवं ह्रीरचापलम्
तेजः क्षमा धृतिः शौचमद्रोहो नातिमानिता।

भवन्ति सम्पदं दैवीमभिजातस्य भारत

### IAST

### Pratibha Translation
The Blessed Lord said: Fearlessness, purity of sattva, steadfastness in the yoga of knowledge, giving, self-restraint, sacrifice, study, austerity, uprightness; non-harming, truth, absence of anger, renunciation, peace, absence of slander, compassion for beings, freedom from greed, gentleness, modesty, absence of restlessness; vigor, forgiveness, fortitude, purity, absence of malice, absence of excessive pride—these belong to one born to the divine endowment, O Bhārata.

### Pratibha Commentary
The chapter opens not with metaphysics but with a catalogue of daivī sampad—divine endowment as a constellation of traits. Fearlessness stands first: terror is not a private feeling only but a spiritual climate. The contested move is to treat ethics as ontology of birth into a divine inheritance, without reducing freedom to genetics. These qualities are recognizable, trainable, and diagnostic. In the Gītā’s war setting, divine wealth is not softness; it includes tejas and dhṛti. Existentially, the list is a mirror: which items are living in you, which are costumes, which are absent? The teaching will soon contrast this endowment with the āsuric, so honesty now is mercy later. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, concept without existential cut becomes another ornament of bondage; therefore the verse must be read as a demand on perception, desire, and refuge, not as a museum label on a doctrine. The existential stake is that this teaching must change how you inhabit the next hour, not only how you narrate metaphysics.

### Key Terms
**daivī sampad (दैवी सम्पद्)** — divine endowment/wealth -> constellation of liberating traits -> 'being nice' misses vigor and fearlessness.
**abhaya (अभय)** — fearlessness -> first divine mark -> 'confidence' can be egoic counterfeit.
**jñāna-yoga-vyavasthiti (ज्ञानयोगव्यवस्थिति)** — steadfast standing in knowledge-yoga -> stable knowing -> 'having beliefs' is weaker.
**hrī (ह्री)** — modesty/shame before wrong -> delicate moral sensitivity -> 'embarrassment' psychologizes it.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Patañjali yama-niyama]:** Virtue lists as yoga foundation are homologous.
*Divergence:* Patañjali systematizes limbs; Krishna frames traits as divine vs āsuric endowments.
**[Paul, fruits of the Spirit]:** Catalogues of Spirit-born traits resonate.
*Divergence:* Pauline fruits are Christic; here traits index daivī sampad toward liberation.
**[Confucian virtue clusters]:** Recognizable ethical constellations shaping the person.
*Divergence:* Confucian cultivation aims at social harmony; the Gītā aims at mokṣa-oriented endowment.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Choose one trait from the list you lack. Practice it once today in a concrete scene, as endowment rather than self-improvement theater.

## Two Births, One Consolation
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 16.4–16.6

Hypocrisy, arrogance, excessive pride, anger, harshness, and ignorance belong to one born to the demonic endowment, O Pārtha. The divine endowment is for liberation; the demonic is considered for bondage. Do not grieve: you are born to the divine endowment, O son of Pāṇḍu. There are two kinds of created beings in this world: the divine and the demonic. The divine has been spoken of at length; hear from Me of the demonic, O Pārtha.

The contested move is typology without fatalism: two created streams exist, yet consolation is possible, and listening still matters. Existentially, the verse refuses both despair (“I am demonic”) and complacency (“I am divine, so I am done”). ### Key Terms
**āsurī sampad (आसुरी सम्पद्)** — demonic endowment -> traits that bind -> 'evil personality' cartoonishly essentializes.
**vimokṣāya / nibandhāya (विमोक्षाय / निबन्धाय)** — for liberation / for bondage -> teleology of endowments -> 'good/bad vibes' misses destiny-logic.
**mā śucaḥ (मा शुचः)** — do not grieve -> pastoral reassurance -> 'cheer up' trivializes the battlefield grief.
**bhūta-sarga (भूतसर्ग)** — creation of beings -> two streams of creatures -> 'personality types' shrinks cosmology.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Gītā 9.11–12]:** Āsuric disregard of the Lord’s embodiment foreshadows this typology.
*Divergence:* Chapter 16 expands into a full anthropology of the āsuric mind.
**[Augustine, two cities]:** Two loves making two cities resonates structurally.
*Divergence:* Augustine’s cities are eschatological-historical; here endowments are ethical-psychological streams.
**[Buddhist loka / gati mappings]:** Beings oriented toward higher/lower rebirths loosely parallel.
*Divergence:* Buddhist maps are karmic-intentional; Krishna’s consolation to Arjuna personalizes the teaching.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
When shame says 'I am āsuric,' answer with honest inventory and the refusal of despair. When pride says 'I am divine,' inventory again.

## The World Without a Lord
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 16.7–16.9

Demonic people do not know engagement or withdrawal; purity, right conduct, and truth are not found in them. They say the world is without truth, without foundation, without a Lord, not produced by regular causation, having desire as its only cause—what else? Holding this view, with lost selves and little understanding, they rise with fierce deeds as enemies for the destruction of the world.

## Insatiable Desire as Religion
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 16.10–16.12

Clinging to insatiable desire, full of hypocrisy, pride, and arrogance, holding false views through delusion, they proceed with impure resolves. Giving themselves to immeasurable cares ending only at dissolution, taking gratification of desire as the highest, convinced that this is all; bound by a hundred snares of hope, devoted to desire and anger, they seek to accumulate wealth by unjust means for the sake of sense-enjoyment.

## The Litany of the Inflated I

## Hell’s Net and Hatred of the Indwelling Lord
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 16.16–16.18

Clinging to egoism, power, pride, desire, and anger, these resentful ones hate Me in their own and others’ bodies.

## The Catechism of Departure
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 8.1–8.3

Arjuna said: What is that Brahman? What is adhyātma? What is karma, O Highest Person? What is called adhibhūta, and what is said to be adhidaiva? Who is adhiyajña here in this body, O Madhusūdana, and how are You to be known at the time of departure by those of restrained self?

The Blessed Lord said: Brahman is the imperishable, the supreme; one's own being is called adhyātma; karma is the offering-forth that causes the arising of the states of beings.

---

### Devanagari
अर्जुन उवाच

किं तद्ब्रह्म किमध्यात्मं किं कर्म पुरुषोत्तम।

अधिभूतं च किं प्रोक्तमधिदैवं किमुच्यते

अधियज्ञः कथं कोऽत्र देहेऽस्मिन्मधुसूदन।

प्रयाणकाले च कथं ज्ञेयोऽसि नियतात्मभिः

श्री भगवानुवाच

अक्षरं ब्रह्म परमं स्वभावोऽध्यात्ममुच्यते।

भूतभावोद्भवकरो विसर्गः कर्मसंज्ञितः

### IAST
arjuna uvāca

kiṃ tadbrahma kimadhyātmaṃ kiṃ karma puruṣottama|

adhibhūtaṃ ca kiṃ proktamadhidaivaṃ kimucyate

adhiyajñaḥ kathaṃ ko'tra dehe'sminmadhusūdana|

prayāṇakāle ca kathaṃ jñeyo'si niyatātmabhiḥ

śrī bhagavānuvāca

akṣaraṃ brahma paramaṃ svabhāvo'dhyātmamucyate|

bhūtabhāvodbhavakaro visargaḥ karmasaṃjñitaḥ

### Pratibha Translation
Arjuna said: What is that Brahman? What is adhyātma? What is karma, O Puruṣottama? What is declared as adhibhūta, and what is called adhidaiva? How and who is adhiyajña here in this body, O Madhusūdana — and at the hour of departure, how are You known by those whose self is restrained?

The Blessed Lord said: The imperishable [akṣara] is Brahman, the supreme; own-being [svabhāva] is called adhyātma; the emission [visarga] that causes beings' states to arise is named karma.

### Pratibha Commentary
Arjuna's questions are not academic taxonomy; they are a death-ready catechism. The contested move is to demand that metaphysics, inwardness, action, cosmos, deity, and sacrifice all become answerable together, and then to press the question that tests them: how is the Lord known at prayāṇa-kāla by a restrained self. Kṛṣṇa begins with three definitions that refuse modern splits: Brahman as supreme akṣara, adhyātma as svabhāva (own-being, not a mood), and karma as generative visarga — action as world-emitting, not mere moral bookkeeping. Existentially this resets study: if spiritual knowledge cannot survive the question of dying, it is not yet the knowledge this chapter is about. The dialogue frame matters: Arjuna asks as one who may die in battle tomorrow; the definitions are already pastoral. Knowing Brahman without knowing how action births states of being leaves a hole; knowing action without adhyātma becomes outer technique. The chapter will braid these registers until departure-knowing is possible. Honesty here is diagnostic: mark what is clear, foggy, or unlived before collecting more terms.

### Key Terms
**akṣara (अक्षर)** — imperishable / syllable -> Brahman as supreme imperishable -> "eternal" misses the later technical pair with kṣara.

**adhyātma (अध्यात्म)** — pertaining to the self -> identified as svabhāva, own-being -> "spirituality" is too soft; it is a register beside adhibhūta and adhidaiva.

**visarga (विसर्ग)** — emission/sending-forth -> karma as productive outpour that makes beings' states arise -> "action" as task-list misses cosmogonic force.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Phaedo (philosophy as practice of dying):** Both bind true knowing to readiness for death.
*Divergence:* Socrates aims at Forms; Arjuna demands a theistic map across adhi- registers.

**Kaṭha Upaniṣad (Naciketas on death):** Both stage ultimate teaching under death's pressure.
*Divergence:* Kaṭha centers ātman via Yama; Gītā 8 distributes questions across multiple registers at once.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Write Arjuna's questions as a checklist. Beside each mark one honest word: "clear," "foggy," or "unlived." Do not study further until all are marked.

## Perishable Field, Divine Person, Sacrifice in the Body
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 8.4

Adhibhūta is the perishable state; the Person is adhidaivata; I Myself am adhiyajña here in this body, O Best of embodied ones.

---

### Devanagari
अधिभूतं क्षरो भावः पुरुषश्चाधिदैवतम्।

अधियज्ञोऽहमेवात्र देहे देहभृतां वर

### IAST
adhibhūtaṃ kṣaro bhāvaḥ puruṣaścādhidaivatam|

adhiyajño'hamevātra dehe dehabhṛtāṃ vara

### Pratibha Translation
Adhibhūta is the perishable [kṣara] condition; the Person [puruṣa] is adhidaivata; I Myself am the Lord of sacrifice [adhiyajña] here in this body, O best of the embodied.

### Pratibha Commentary
Three registers, one sentence: the elemental field is perishable becoming; the cosmic Person is the divine register; and — most contested — Kṛṣṇa claims to be adhiyajña in this body. Sacrifice is not only outer ritual; its Lord is present in embodiment. This blocks both materialism (body as mere meat) and disembodied mysticism (sacrifice as elsewhere). Existentially, every act that offers, costs, or consecrates already stands inside a field the Lord claims to preside over. Knowing about God while treating the body as spiritually irrelevant fails this verse. The perishable is not denied; it is named kṣara and located. The Person is not collapsed into chemistry. The hinge is sacrificial presence in the living body — which will matter when departure time comes and the body is what is left behind. The line-level logic forces integration: perishability, Personhood, and sacrifice cannot be filed in separate drawers if departure-knowing is the aim. What you refuse to relate in life you will not suddenly relate in dying.

### Key Terms
**kṣara (क्षर)** — perishable/flowing away -> adhibhūta as the decaying condition of beings -> "material" misses the temporal leaking sense of kṣar.

**adhiyajña (अधियज्ञ)** — overlord of sacrifice -> Kṛṣṇa as sacrifice's Lord in the body -> "ritual expert" trivializes the ontological claim.

**puruṣa (पुरुष)** — Person/cosmic Man -> here adhidaivata -> "male person" misses the cosmic-personal register.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Romans 12.1 (bodies as living sacrifice):** Both locate sacrifice in embodied life.
*Divergence:* Paul frames ecclesial worship in Christ; Gītā names Kṛṣṇa Himself as adhiyajña in the body.

**Vedic yajña metaphysics:** Both treat sacrifice as world-ordering.
*Divergence:* Gītā personalizes the principle as the speaking Lord in dialogue.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Before one ordinary act today (eating, email, apology), silently name it as offering under adhiyajña in the body — then do it cleanly, without drama.

## Remembering at the End Is Destiny
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 8.5

And at the final time, whoever leaves the body remembering Me alone — that one goes to My state; of this there is no doubt.

---

### Devanagari
अन्तकाले च मामेव स्मरन्मुक्त्वा कलेवरम्।

यः प्रयाति स मद्भावं याति नास्त्यत्र संशयः

### IAST
antakāle ca māmeva smaranmuktvā kalevaram|

yaḥ prayāti sa madbhāvaṃ yāti nāstyatra saṃśayaḥ

### Pratibha Translation
At the end-time [anta-kāla], whoever departs, leaving the body while remembering Me alone, goes to My being [mad-bhāva]; of this there is no doubt.

### Pratibha Commentary
Death is not a blank interruption after which theology resumes; the content of remembrance at departure is destiny. The contested claim is brutally simple: who you remember when the body is released determines where you go — and for remembrance of Kṛṣṇa alone, the destination is mad-bhāva, His own state. "No doubt" is pastoral steel, not casual reassurance. Existentially this revalues ordinary memory-training: remembrance is not sentimental nostalgia but the skill that must be available when control thins. The verse does not say "whoever was once religious." It says remembering Me alone while leaving the body. Last-minute magic without prior joining is not the chapter's later teaching; this verse states the principle whose practice the next verses will demand in all times, not only the end. Fear of death often fixates on logistics; this verse fixates on the object of mind. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**anta-kāla (अन्तकाल)** — end-time/death-hour -> the decisive moment of departure -> "death" alone misses the temporal edge-character of kāla.

**mad-bhāva (मद्भाव)** — My being/state -> destination of those who remember Kṛṣṇa alone -> "my nature" as temperament understates ontological arrival.

**smaran (स्मरन्)** — remembering -> active recollection at departure -> "thinking of" is too weak for destiny-bearing remembrance.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Tibetan bar do teaching (recognition at death):** Both insist last cognition shapes transit.
*Divergence:* Bardo maps intermediate states; Gītā names destination as the Lord's own bhāva via remembrance.

**Christian ars moriendi:** Both train dying as spiritually decisive.
*Divergence:* Ars moriendi centers confession and Christ; Gītā centers exclusive remembrance of Kṛṣṇa.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Once today, while healthy, practice ten breaths of exclusive remembrance of the Divine — as rehearsal for a moment when rehearsal will no longer be available.

## Fight While Remembering
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 8.6–8.8

Whatever state one remembers when leaving the body at the end — to that alone one goes, O Son of Kuntī, always made into that state. Therefore at all times remember Me and fight. With mind and intellect fixed on Me, you will come to Me without doubt. With consciousness joined by practice-yoga, not going elsewhere, meditating, one goes to the supreme divine Person, O Pārtha.

---

### Devanagari
यं यं वापि स्मरन्भावं त्यजत्यन्ते कलेवरम्।

तं तमेवैति कौन्तेय सदा तद्भावभावितः

तस्मात्सर्वेषु कालेषु मामनुस्मर युध्य च।

मय्यर्पितमनोबुद्धिर्मामेवैष्यस्यसंशयम्

अभ्यासयोगयुक्तेन चेतसा नान्यगामिना।

परमं पुरुषं दिव्यं याति पार्थानुचिन्तयन्

### IAST
yaṃ yaṃ vāpi smaranbhāvaṃ tyajatyante kalevaram|

taṃ tamevaiti kaunteya sadā tadbhāvabhāvitaḥ

tasmātsarveṣu kāleṣu māmanusmara yudhya ca|

mayyarpitamanobuddhirmāmevaiṣyasyasaṃśayam

abhyāsayogayuktena cetasā nānyagāminā|

paramaṃ puruṣaṃ divyaṃ yāti pārthānucintayan

### Pratibha Translation
Whatever being [bhāva] one remembers when casting off the body at the end — to that very one goes, O Son of Kuntī, always cultivated into that state. Therefore at all times remember Me and fight. With mind and intellect offered to Me, you will come to Me without doubt. With awareness joined by practice-yoga [abhyāsa-yoga], not going elsewhere, contemplating, one reaches the supreme divine Person, O Pārtha.

### Pratibha Commentary
Remembrance at death is not an exception to psychology; it is the fruit of what one has been becoming always. The contested pastoral command is double and undivided: remember Me and fight. Contemplation is not licensed escape from Kurukṣetra; battle is not licensed amnesia of God. Mind and intellect offered (arpita) make arrival undoubted. Abhyāsa-yoga with a mind that does not wander elsewhere names the method: practice that trains single-pointed going-toward the divine Person. Existentially this destroys the split schedule — "spiritual mornings, worldly wars." The war is the field of remembrance. If your practice cannot survive conflict, it will not survive dying. The law of bhāva-continuity is severe mercy: you become what you rehearse; death reveals the rehearsal. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**abhyāsa-yoga (अभ्यासयोग)** — yoga of repeated practice -> the joining that carries meditation to the divine Person -> "habit" misses disciplined yogic joining.

**arpita (अर्पित)** — offered/entrusted -> mind-intellect placed in Kṛṣṇa -> "focused" understates gift-character of the offering.

**bhāva (भाव)** — state/being/becoming -> what is remembered and become at death -> "mood" is far too thin.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Marcus Aurelius (attention amid campaign life):** Both refuse to separate contemplation from duty under pressure.
*Divergence:* Stoic attention centers logos/nature; Gītā commands remembrance of a personal Lord while fighting.

**Sufi dhikr in the marketplace:** Both train remembrance inside action.
*Divergence:* Dhikr idioms vary by order; Gītā binds remembrance to battlefield dharma explicitly.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
In one difficult conversation or task today, keep a short remembrance-thread alive (one name, one breath-phrase) while you act — do not postpone remembrance until after the fight.

## The Poet Beyond Darkness
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 8.9–8.11

Who remembers the ancient Poet, the ruler, smaller than the atom, supporter of all, of unthinkable form, sun-colored, beyond darkness — at the time of departure, with unmoving mind, joined with devotion and the strength of yoga, placing the breath rightly between the brows — that one reaches that supreme divine Person. What knowers of Veda call the imperishable, what ascetics free of passion enter, what those who desire seek by living as brahmacarya — that path I will tell you in brief.

---

### Devanagari
कविं पुराणमनुशासितार

मणोरणीयांसमनुस्मरेद्यः।

सर्वस्य धातारमचिन्त्यरूप

मादित्यवर्णं तमसः परस्तात्

प्रयाणकाले मनसाऽचलेन

भक्त्या युक्तो योगबलेन चैव।

भ्रुवोर्मध्ये प्राणमावेश्य सम्यक्

स तं परं पुरुषमुपैति दिव्यम्

यदक्षरं वेदविदो वदन्ति

विशन्ति यद्यतयो वीतरागाः।

यदिच्छन्तो ब्रह्मचर्यं चरन्ति

तत्ते पदं संग्रहेण प्रवक्ष्ये

### IAST
kaviṃ purāṇamanuśāsitāra

maṇoraṇīyāṃsamanusmaredyaḥ|

sarvasya dhātāramacintyarūpa

mādityavarṇaṃ tamasaḥ parastāt

prayāṇakāle manasā'calena

bhaktyā yukto yogabalena caiva|

bhruvormadhye prāṇamāveśya samyak

sa taṃ paraṃ puruṣamupaiti divyam

yadakṣaraṃ vedavido vadanti

viśanti yadyatayo vītarāgāḥ|

yadicchanto brahmacaryaṃ caranti

tatte padaṃ saṃgraheṇa pravakṣye

### Pratibha Translation
Who remembers the Seer-Poet [kavi], the Ancient, the Ruler, subtler than the atom, the supporter of all, of unthinkable form, sun-colored, beyond the darkness — at departure, with unmoving mind, joined by devotion and yoga-strength, setting the vital breath [prāṇa] rightly between the brows — reaches that supreme divine Person. What Veda-knowers call the imperishable, what striving ones free of passion enter, desiring which they live brahmacarya — that footing [pada] I will declare to you in compressed form.

### Pratibha Commentary
The object of deathbed remembrance is not a vague light; it is a densely predicated Person: poet-seer, ancient, ruler, subtler than atom, all-supporter, unthinkable in form, sun-hued, beyond tamas. The contested synthesis joins bhakti, yoga-bala, steady mind, and prāṇa placed between the brows — devotion, power, stillness, and subtle physiology as one act of going. Verse 8.11 then embeds this in Upaniṣadic pedigree: the same akṣara the Veda-knowers name, the same entry the passion-free seek, the same desire that structures brahmacarya. Existentially this blocks two reductions: reducing dying to a breathing trick without devotion, and reducing devotion to emotion without steadied mind and breath. The "brief" teaching is not simplistic; it is compressed because the path is one. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**kavi (कवि)** — poet/seer -> the Lord as ancient visionary ruler -> "poet" as artist misses Vedic seer-authority.

**prāṇa (प्राण)** — vital breath -> placed between brows at departure with yoga-strength -> "breath" as air only misses life-force technique.

**brahmacarya (ब्रह्मचर्य)** — walking with Brahman / continence -> discipline of those who desire the imperishable footing -> "celibacy" alone misses the broader Vedic student-aim.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Plotinus on the flight of the alone to the Alone:** Both describe ascent to a principle beyond darkness.
*Divergence:* Plotinus' One is impersonal; Gītā's Person is sun-colored, devotionally approachable, with yogic breath-placement.

**Kaṭha Upaniṣad (thumb-sized person / beyond darkness):** Shared imagery of the Person beyond tamas.
*Divergence:* Gītā inserts explicit bhakti-yoga-bala and battlefield interlocutor.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Sit upright for five minutes. Steady the mind on the "sun-colored beyond darkness" image while keeping breath soft and central; if emotion arises, let it serve devotion rather than scatter attention.

## Om, Remembrance, and the Easy Lord
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 8.12–8.14

Closing all gates, confining the mind in the heart, placing one's prāṇa in the head, established in yoga-holding; uttering Om, the one-syllable Brahman, remembering Me — who departs, leaving the body, goes to the highest goal. For the one of undistracted mind who remembers Me constantly, always — I am easy to reach, O Pārtha, for that yogin ever joined.

---

### Devanagari
सर्वद्वाराणि संयम्य मनो हृदि निरुध्य च।

मूर्ध्न्याधायात्मनः प्राणमास्थितो योगधारणाम्

ओमित्येकाक्षरं ब्रह्म व्याहरन्मामनुस्मरन्।

यः प्रयाति त्यजन्देहं स याति परमां गतिम्

अनन्यचेताः सततं यो मां स्मरति नित्यशः।

तस्याहं सुलभः पार्थ नित्ययुक्तस्य योगिनः

### IAST
sarvadvārāṇi saṃyamya mano hṛdi nirudhya ca|

mūrdhnyādhāyātmanaḥ prāṇamāsthito yogadhāraṇām

omityekākṣaraṃ brahma vyāharanmāmanusmaran|

yaḥ prayāti tyajandehaṃ sa yāti paramāṃ gatim

ananyacetāḥ satataṃ yo māṃ smarati nityaśaḥ|

tasyāhaṃ sulabhaḥ pārtha nityayuktasya yoginaḥ

### Pratibha Translation
Restraining all the gates, holding the mind in the heart, placing one's own prāṇa in the head, established in yoga-concentration [yoga-dhāraṇā]; sounding Om, the one-syllable Brahman, remembering Me — who goes forth leaving the body reaches the supreme goal. I am easy to attain [sulabha], O Pārtha, for the yogin of undivided awareness who remembers Me constantly, always, ever joined.

### Pratibha Commentary
The hardest techniques — gate-closing, heart-holding, cranial prāṇa, Om, remembrance at exit — culminate in a soft claim: for the constantly remembering, ever-joined yogin, the Lord is sulabha, easy. The contested move is that ease is not the opposite of discipline; ease is the fruit of ananya-cetas continuity. Difficulty belongs to last-minute improvisation; ease belongs to nitya remembrance. Existentially this comforts without cheapening: you are not told technique is unnecessary; you are told that exclusive, continuous remembrance makes the Lord available in a way crisis-spirituality cannot. Om as one-syllable Brahman joins sonic Absolute to personal remembrance — sound and relation together. The practice question becomes: is my yoga only for special sittings, or has remembrance become the default climate? The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**sulabha (सुलभ)** — easy to attain -> the Lord for the ever-joined constant rememberer -> "easy" as lazy understates the condition: ananya-cetas, nitya.

**yoga-dhāraṇā (योगधारणा)** — yogic holding/concentration -> establishment at departure with prāṇa in the head -> "focus" is too thin for formal yogic holding.

**ananya-cetas (अनन्यचेतस्)** — mind with no other -> exclusive awareness in remembrance -> "loyal" misses cognitive undividedness.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Cloud of Unknowing (naked intent toward God):** Both make continuous intent the heart of attainment.
*Divergence:* Cloud is apophatic Christian; Gītā includes Om, prāṇa, and explicit sulabha for the joined yogin.

**Hesychast Jesus Prayer:** Both train unceasing remembrance.
*Divergence:* Hesychasm centers the Name of Jesus; Gītā joins Om-Brahman utterance to remembering Kṛṣṇa.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Choose one ordinary hour today and keep a quiet Om-remembrance thread through it (mental, not showy). Note whether the Lord feels "hard" or "near" when remembrance is continuous rather than sporadic.

## No Return to the House of Pain
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 8.15

Having reached Me, the great-souled ones who have gone to the highest perfection do not incur rebirth — that impermanent house of pain.

---

### Devanagari
मामुपेत्य पुनर्जन्म दुःखालयमशाश्वतम्।

नाप्नुवन्ति महात्मानः संसिद्धिं परमां गताः

### IAST
māmupetya punarjanma duḥkhālayamaśāśvatam|

nāpnuvanti mahātmānaḥ saṃsiddhiṃ paramāṃ gatāḥ

### Pratibha Translation
Having come to Me, the great-souled ones [mahātman] who have reached the highest perfection do not take again rebirth, the impermanent house of pain [duḥkhālaya].

### Pratibha Commentary
Liberation is defined negatively with surgical clarity: no return to rebirth as duḥkhālaya — pain's dwelling, and aśāśvata, unstable. The contested claim is that the highest siddhi is not a better room in saṃsāra but exit from the housing system of repeated birth. "Having reached Me" is the condition; mahātman status is the recognition of those who have arrived. Existentially this reorders desire: upgrading the house (better body, better world-period, better status) is not the aim if the house itself is pain's lodge. Devotion that still primarily seeks a nicer rebirth has not yet heard this verse. The pastoral force is hope without denial: pain's house is real; so is non-return for those who reach the Lord. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. If the claim does not alter rehearsal in ordinary time, it will not hold when time thins. Existential uptake is therefore part of fidelity to the argument, not an optional aftertaste.

### Key Terms
**duḥkhālaya (दुःखालय)** — abode of suffering -> rebirth characterized as pain's house -> "unhappy life" softens the structural claim about saṃsāra.

**saṃsiddhi (संसिद्धि)** — complete perfection/attainment -> the highest accomplishment that ends return -> "success" is too worldly.

**mahātman (महात्मन्)** — great-souled -> those who have reached Me and highest perfection -> "great person" misses spiritual magnitude.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Four Noble Truths (dukkha as pervasive):** Both refuse to treat conditioned existence as a satisfactory home.
*Divergence:* Buddhism aims at nirvāṇa without a theistic "reaching Me"; Gītā names non-return as arrival at the Lord.

**Augustine on restless heart:** Both imply finite dwellings cannot give final rest.
*Divergence:* Augustine's rest is in the Christian God; Gītā's non-return is mad-upetya.

## Even Brahmā's World Returns
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 8.16

From the realm of Brahmā downward, the worlds are returners, O Arjuna; but having reached Me, O Son of Kuntī, there is no rebirth.

---

### Devanagari
आब्रह्मभुवनाल्लोकाः पुनरावर्तिनोऽर्जुन।

### Pratibha Commentary
Cosmic hierarchy does not equal liberation. Even Brahmā's world — the summit of many religious imaginations — remains inside return. The contested absolute is exclusive: mām upetya alone ends punarjanma. This is not petty sectarianism about temples; it is metaphysical triage about destinations. The verse also comforts those who fail to romanticize celestial promotion: the aim is the Lord, not a longer vacation in subtle realms. The line-level force of the teaching is practical, not ornamental: what it claims about reality is meant to reshape how you attend, choose, and depart. ### Key Terms
**punar-āvartin (पुनरावर्तिन्)** — returning again -> character of all worlds through Brahmā's realm -> "reincarnating" is close but the stress is cyclic return of worlds/sojourners.

**Brahmā-bhuvana (ब्रह्मभुवन)** — Brahmā's world -> upper limit still inside return -> confusing Brahmā with Brahman is exactly the error this verse prevents.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Dante's celestial ascent:** Both map graded worlds.
*Divergence:* Dante's Empyrean is final beatitude in God; Gītā explicitly marks even Brahmā-loka as returning.

**Pure Land aspirations:** Both speak of going to a supreme destination.
*Divergence:* Pure Land grammar differs; Gītā's criterion is stark: only reaching Kṛṣṇa ends rebirth.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
When you notice a spiritual fantasy of "going somewhere better," ask: does this destination still return? Let the question prune the fantasy.

*Divergence:* Qoheleth's God-fear differs from Gītā's explicit cosmic day/night mechanism.

Look at one urgent project and silently place it against a thousand-yuga night. Act responsibly anyway — but drop the fantasy that the project founds reality.

### Key Terms
**ananya bhakti (अनन्य भक्ति)** — devotion with no other -> the means of attaining the supreme Person -> "loyalty" understates exclusivity of relational aim.

**Bhāgavata bhakti theology:** Structural kinship in exclusive devotion.

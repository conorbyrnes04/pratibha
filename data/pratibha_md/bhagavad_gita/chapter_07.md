**Corpus entry:** Bhagavad Gītā / Kṛṣṇa–Arjuna dialogue

**Units:** 13 (one per brief chunk)

## Attachment That Opens Whole Knowledge
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.1–7.3

The Blessed Lord said: With mind attached to Me, O Pārtha, practicing yoga and taking refuge in Me — hear how you will know Me entirely, without doubt. I will declare to you this knowledge together with realization [vijñāna], completely; knowing which, nothing further remains to be known here. Among thousands of humans, one somehow strives for perfection; among those who strive and even among the perfected, one somehow knows Me in truth [tattvataḥ].

---

### Devanagari
श्री भगवानुवाच

मय्यासक्तमनाः पार्थ योगं युञ्जन्मदाश्रयः।

असंशयं समग्रं मां यथा ज्ञास्यसि तच्छृणु

ज्ञानं तेऽहं सविज्ञानमिदं वक्ष्याम्यशेषतः।

यज्ज्ञात्वा नेह भूयोऽन्यज्ज्ञातव्यमवशिष्यते

मनुष्याणां सहस्रेषु कश्चिद्यतति सिद्धये।

यततामपि सिद्धानां कश्चिन्मां वेत्ति तत्त्वतः

### IAST
śrī bhagavānuvāca

mayyāsaktamanāḥ pārtha yogaṃ yuñjanmadāśrayaḥ|

asaṃśayaṃ samagraṃ māṃ yathā jñāsyasi tacchṛṇu

jñānaṃ te'haṃ savijñānamidaṃ vakṣyāmyaśeṣataḥ|

yajjñātvā neha bhūyo'nyajjñātavyamavaśiṣyate

manuṣyāṇāṃ sahasreṣu kaścidyatati siddhaye|

yatatāmapi siddhānāṃ kaścinmāṃ vetti tattvataḥ

### Pratibha Translation
The Blessed Lord said: With mind attached to Me, O Pārtha, joining yoga and taking Me as refuge — listen how you will know Me wholly, without doubt. I declare to you this knowledge [jñāna] together with realization [vijñāna], leaving nothing out; knowing which, nothing else remains here that must still be known. Among thousands of humans, someone strives for perfection [siddhi]; even among those who strive and among the perfected, someone knows Me as I am [tattvataḥ].

### Pratibha Commentary
Whole knowledge of the Divine is not the prize of detached curiosity but of attachment rightly placed. The contested move is that *āsakti* — usually treated as the glue of bondage — becomes here the very condition of *samagra* knowing: mind stuck to Kṛṣṇa, yoga practiced under His shelter, doubt already ruled out of the hearing. Knowledge (*jñāna*) without *vijñāna* would leave a remainder; *vijñāna* without *jñāna* would float as experience without map. Together they close the epistemic field: once this is known, "nothing further remains to be known here." Rarity is not elitism as social gatekeeping; it is the text's honest pedagogy about attention. Most humans never even strive; among strivers and even *siddhas*, few know the Lord *tattvataḥ* — as principle, not as rumor or borrowed devotion. Existentially this reframes spiritual ambition: the question is not "Am I special enough?" but "Where is my mind attached, and under whose refuge does my practice actually run?" Attachment to the finite fragments knowing; attachment to the Self that claims to be the ground of all fragments makes knowing whole.

### Key Terms
**jñāna (ज्ञान)** — from *jñā*, "to know" -> propositional/scriptural knowledge of the Divine in this chapter's opening frame -> "knowledge" alone misses that it is paired with *vijñāna* and incomplete without it.

**vijñāna (विज्ञान)** — *vi-* intensifier + *jñāna* -> discriminative, lived realization that seals knowledge so nothing remains unknown -> default "wisdom" blurs the technical pairing with *jñāna*.

**tattvataḥ (तत्त्वतः)** — *tattva* ("that-ness, principle") + adverbial *-taḥ* -> knowing Me *as I truly am*, not as projection -> "truly" is too thin; the stake is ontological accuracy, not sincerity.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Chāndogya Upaniṣad VI.1 (instruction by which the unheard is heard):** Both claim a knowledge that completes the field so residual "something else to know" collapses.
*Divergence:* Uddālaka teaches impersonal material-cause unity (clay/gold); Kṛṣṇa binds completion of knowledge to personal refuge and attachment of mind.

**Plotinus, Enneads VI.9 (on the Good):** Both treat ultimate knowing as rare and as requiring a turn of the soul rather than more information.
*Divergence:* Plotinus' ascent is noetic and impersonal; Gītā 7 opens with *mad-āśraya* — shelter in a speaking Lord.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
For one sitting, place your attention on a single phrase of refuge ("I take shelter here") and notice whether your mind is attached to outcomes or to the refuge itself; stay with the latter for ten breaths before any further study.

## Two Natures, One Womb of the World
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.4–7.6

Earth, water, fire, air, space, mind, intellect, and ego — this is My lower nature, divided eightfold. Know My other nature as higher than this, O Mighty-armed: the living being [*jīva-bhūtā*] by which this world is upheld. Know that all beings have their womb in these two; I am the origin and the dissolution of this entire world.

---

### Devanagari
भूमिरापोऽनलो वायुः खं मनो बुद्धिरेव च।

अहङ्कार इतीयं मे भिन्ना प्रकृतिरष्टधा

अपरेयमितस्त्वन्यां प्रकृतिं विद्धि मे पराम्।

जीवभूतां महाबाहो ययेदं धार्यते जगत्

एतद्योनीनि भूतानि सर्वाणीत्युपधारय।

अहं कृत्स्नस्य जगतः प्रभवः प्रलयस्तथा

### IAST
bhūmirāpo'nalo vāyuḥ khaṃ mano buddhireva ca|

ahaṅkāra itīyaṃ me bhinnā prakṛtiraṣṭadhā

apareyamitastvanyāṃ prakṛtiṃ viddhi me parām|

jīvabhūtāṃ mahābāho yayedaṃ dhāryate jagat

etadyonīni bhūtāni sarvāṇītyupadhāraya|

ahaṃ kṛtsnasya jagataḥ prabhavaḥ pralayastathā

### Pratibha Translation
Earth, water, fire, air, space, mind, intellect, and ego-sense — thus is My nature [*prakṛti*] divided eightfold. This is the lower; know My other nature as higher than this, O Mighty-armed: the living principle [*jīva-bhūtā*] by which this world is upheld. Understand that all beings have their womb in these; I am the arising and the dissolution of the entire world.

### Pratibha Commentary
Matter and life are not rivals to God; they are *His* natures, ranked. The counterintuitive move is double: the famous Sāṃkhya inventory (elements through *ahaṅkāra*) is claimed as *Me*-owned lower *prakṛti*, and a second, higher *prakṛti* is named as *jīva-bhūtā* — living being-ness itself — by which the world is held. Classical dualism of *puruṣa*/*prakṛti* is thus theologically rewritten: both natures belong to Kṛṣṇa, and He alone is origin and end of the whole. Creatures are not self-grounded; they are womb-born of this double matrix. Existentially this blocks two escapes: reducing spirit to chemistry, and floating spirit above nature as if embodiment were alien. You are upheld by the higher nature, configured through the lower, and both answer to one Source. The practical cut is against ownership of either body-mind or "my soul" as private property: both are *Me*-prakṛti. The eightfold list matters because it includes not only earth and fire but mind, intellect, and the I-maker: the inner instruments of knowing are themselves lower nature. That undercuts the common spiritual move of treating "my mind" as a neutral flashlight inspecting matter. Even the flashlight is prakṛti. The higher living nature upholds the world, yet still is not the Lord Himself; thus personhood is sacred without being absolute. Practice becomes lucid humility: inhabit life fully, refuse to crown either chemistry or ego as God, and return both matrices to their Source in recognition.

### Key Terms
**prakṛti (प्रकृति)** — *pra-* + *kṛ* ("to make") -> productive nature; here split into lower (eightfold) and higher (*jīva*) as Kṛṣṇa's own -> "nature" misses ownership and the dual ranking.

**ahaṅkāra (अहङ्कार)** — "I-maker" -> ego-function within lower *prakṛti*, not the Self -> "ego" as personality psychology understates its cosmological place in the eightfold list.

**jīva-bhūtā (जीवभूता)** — living-being as higher *prakṛti* -> the world-upholding life-principle belonging to the Lord -> "soul" as immortal spark misses that it is still *prakṛti*, not the Lord Himself.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Sāṃkhya-kārikā (eightfold *prakṛti* / *tattva* enumeration):** Same inventory of manifesting principles.
*Divergence:* Sāṃkhya keeps *puruṣa* plural and separate; Gītā folds both natures into Kṛṣṇa's self-disclosure and adds a higher living *prakṛti*.

**Dao De Jing 42 ("Dao generates One…"):** Both situate manifold beings in a generative matrix prior to their independence.
*Divergence:* Daoist language is non-theistic process; Gītā names personal origin and dissolution (*prabhava*/*pralaya*) as "I."

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Name one sensation (earth/water/fire/air/space), one mental event, and one "I"-thought today; silently mark each: "lower nature — not mine to own." Then feel the simple fact of being alive and mark: "higher nature — still not mine to own."

## Thread Through the Beads
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.7

There is nothing whatsoever higher than Me, O Winner of Wealth. All this is strung on Me like gems on a thread.

---

### Devanagari
मत्तः परतरं नान्यत्किञ्चिदस्ति धनञ्जय।

मयि सर्वमिदं प्रोतं सूत्रे मणिगणा इव

### IAST
mattaḥ parataraṃ nānyatkiñcidasti dhanañjaya|

mayi sarvamidaṃ protaṃ sūtre maṇigaṇā iva

### Pratibha Translation
Nothing at all exists higher than Me, O Dhanañjaya. All this is strung in Me like clusters of gems on a thread [*sūtra*].

### Pratibha Commentary
Absolute priority is not abstract transcendence floating above the world; it is the thread that makes the necklace a necklace. The contested image refuses both pantheism (gems *are* the thread) and deism (gems unrelated to the thread). Beads remain distinct; without the *sūtra* they scatter into meaningless pile. "Nothing higher than Me" then means: no rival Absolute, no parallel ultimate principle — including the world itself as a competing god. Existentially, crises of meaning often treat events as loose beads: career, love, loss, ideology. The verse trains a perceptual habit: look for the continuity that holds disparate moments without erasing their color. Devotion here is metaphysical literacy — seeing relation without collapsing difference. The simile also disciplines theology: if nothing is higher than Kṛṣṇa, then political gods, market gods, and family gods cannot occupy the thread-position without idolatry. Yet beads remain beads — relationships, duties, and beauties keep their color. The spiritual error is either melting all beads into mush or treating each bead as its own Absolute. Thread-awareness is middle path perception: continuity without erasure, devotion without world-hatred. Ask, in conflict, whether you are defending a bead as if it were the thread.

### Key Terms
**sūtra (सूत्र)** — "thread; aphorism" -> the stringing principle of the cosmos in this simile -> "thread" alone misses the secondary resonance of teaching-thread: reality is held by a principle that can be *followed*.

**paratara (परतर)** — comparative of *para*, "higher/beyond" -> nothing exists as a higher principle than Kṛṣṇa -> "superior" as moral rank misses ontological ultimacy.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Īśā Upaniṣad 1 (*īśāvāsyam idaṃ sarvam*):** Both assert all *this* as dwelling in/under the Lord.
*Divergence:* Īśā stresses renunciation-in-use; 7.7 stresses structural dependence via the necklace image.

**Plotinus, Enneads V.1 (the One as source of Intellect and Soul):** Both deny any principle above the ultimate.
*Divergence:* Plotinus' One is beyond being and speech; Kṛṣṇa speaks as the thread while remaining personally addressable.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Take three unrelated events from your day and write them as beads; then name, in one sentence, what "thread" would have to be true for them to belong to one life without forcing a false story.

## Immanence Named in Ordinary Radiance
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.8–7.10

I am the taste in water, O Son of Kuntī; the radiance in moon and sun; the sacred syllable in all the Vedas; sound in space; manhood in men. I am the pure fragrance in earth and the brilliance in fire; life in all beings and austerity in ascetics. Know Me as the eternal seed of all beings, O Pārtha; I am the intellect of the intelligent and the splendor of the splendid.

---

### Devanagari
रसोऽहमप्सु कौन्तेय प्रभास्मि शशिसूर्ययोः।

प्रणवः सर्ववेदेषु शब्दः खे पौरुषं नृषु

पुण्यो गन्धः पृथिव्यां च तेजश्चास्मि विभावसौ।

जीवनं सर्वभूतेषु तपश्चास्मि तपस्विषु

बीजं मां सर्वभूतानां विद्धि पार्थ सनातनम्।

बुद्धिर्बुद्धिमतामस्मि तेजस्तेजस्विनामहम्

### IAST
raso'hamapsu kaunteya prabhāsmi śaśisūryayoḥ|

praṇavaḥ sarvavedeṣu śabdaḥ khe pauruṣaṃ nṛṣu

puṇyo gandhaḥ pṛthivyāṃ ca tejaścāsmi vibhāvasau|

jīvanaṃ sarvabhūteṣu tapaścāsmi tapasviṣu

bījaṃ māṃ sarvabhūtānāṃ viddhi pārtha sanātanam|

buddhirbuddhimatāmasmi tejastejasvināmaham

### Pratibha Translation
I am the taste [*rasa*] in waters, O Son of Kuntī; I am the radiance in moon and sun; the *praṇava* in all the Vedas; sound in space; prowess in humans. I am the pure fragrance in earth and brilliance in fire; life in all beings and austerity [*tapas*] in those who practice austerity. Know Me as the eternal seed of all beings, O Pārtha; I am the intellect of the intelligent and the splendor of the splendid.

### Pratibha Commentary
Metaphysical non-duality must become perceptual devotion. The counterintuitive move is that Kṛṣṇa does not begin with empty transcendence; He trains seeing through concrete excellences — taste, light, sacred sound, vitality, intelligence, moral heat. The world ceases to be an inert object-field and becomes a *vibhūti*-field: patterned unveiling. This blocks materialism (excellence as mere mechanism) and escapism (fleeing manifestation for a distant Absolute). Existentially it heals the split between "sacred moments" and "ordinary time." Integrity in work, lucidity in thought, and courage under pressure become contemplative gates — not as ego trophies but as recognitions of Source shining through transient forms. Gratitude without possession follows: you do not worship success; you perceive what success can only borrow. Notice the list's moral texture: not only light and fragrance but tapas among ascetics and buddhi among the intelligent. Excellence is ethical and noetic, not merely aesthetic. The seed claim then prevents a shallow "find God in sunsets" spirituality: sunsets disclose, but the eternal bīja is the ongoing ground of beings, not a mood. Training consists in catching radiance without clinging, and in refusing to call ego-heat "divine tejas" when it is only aggression.

### Key Terms
**rasa (रस)** — sap, essence, aesthetic flavor -> here the very tastiness of water as divine self-presence -> "taste" as preference misses ontological claim: the quality *is* the Lord.

**praṇava (प्रणव)** — the syllable Om as Vedic quintessence -> Kṛṣṇa as the sonic essence of scripture -> reducing Om to a technique misses that the Lord *is* that essence.

**bīja (बीज)** — seed -> eternal generative principle of beings -> "origin" as past cause misses ongoing seed-character: beings keep depending on what they never outgrow.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Īśā Upaniṣad 1:** World pervaded by the Lord, requiring reverence rather than possession.
*Divergence:* Īśā is aphoristic; Gītā lists sensory and ethical excellences as pedagogical icons.

**Dao De Jing 21 & 34:** Inexhaustible source expressing through myriad forms.
*Divergence:* Daoist idiom is non-theistic and processive; Gītā is personal-theological self-revelation.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Identify three moments of excellence today (clarity, courage, beauty) and consciously name each: "This radiance is not mine — it is disclosure."

## Strength Without Lust, Desire Without Adharma
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.11–7.13

I am the strength of the strong, free from desire and passion; in beings I am desire that does not conflict with dharma, O Best of Bharatas. Know that all states of being — sattvic, rajasic, and tamasic — proceed from Me alone; yet I am not in them — they are in Me. Deluded by these three guṇa-born states, the whole world fails to recognize Me, who am beyond them and imperishable.

---

### Devanagari
बलं बलवतां चाहं कामरागविवर्जितम्।

धर्माविरुद्धो भूतेषु कामोऽस्मि भरतर्षभ

ये चैव सात्त्विका भावा राजसास्तामसाश्च ये।

मत्त एवेति तान्विद्धि नत्वहं तेषु ते मयि

त्रिभिर्गुणमयैर्भावैरेभिः सर्वमिदं जगत्।

मोहितं नाभिजानाति मामेभ्यः परमव्ययम्

### IAST
balaṃ balavatāṃ cāhaṃ kāmarāgavivarjitam|

dharmāviruddho bhūteṣu kāmo'smi bharatarṣabha

ye caiva sāttvikā bhāvā rājasāstāmasāśca ye|

matta eveti tānviddhi natvahaṃ teṣu te mayi

tribhirguṇamayairbhāvairebhiḥ sarvamidaṃ jagat|

mohitaṃ nābhijānāti māmebhyaḥ paramavyayam

### Pratibha Translation
I am the strength of the strong, free from desire and attachment [*kāma-rāga*]; among beings I am desire not opposed to dharma, O Best of the Bharatas. Know that whatever states are sattvic, rajasic, or tamasic proceed from Me alone — yet I am not in them; they are in Me. Deluded by these three guṇa-made states, this whole world does not recognize Me, who am beyond them and imperishable [*avyaya*].

### Pratibha Commentary
Power and desire are not banned from the sacred; they are purified by relation to dharma and detached from compulsive craving. The contested move is the asymmetric ontology of 7.12: qualities come *from* Me, rest *in* Me, but I am not confined *in* them. Immanence without encapsulation. Guṇa-delusion then explains atheism of perception: the world sees moods, drives, and inertias and mistakes the display for the whole. Existentially this gives a precise diagnosis of spiritual fog — not "I lack information about God" but "I am hypnotized by my current guṇa-weather." Strength without *kāma-rāga* and desire without *adharma* become ethical tests for whether force and wanting still disclose the Lord or only the ego's weather system. The line "I am not in them; they are in Me" is the chapter's metaphysical hinge for ethics. If guṇa-states contained God as a trapped resident, mood would equal revelation. Because they rest in God without enclosing God, weather can be witnessed without worshipping weather. Desire-not-opposed-to-dharma then becomes a practical filter: wanting that clarifies duty can be divine; wanting that bends duty cannot borrow the Lord's name. Live one decision under that filter and the verse stops being cosmology alone.

### Key Terms
**kāma (काम)** — desire/wish -> here both the rejected lust-attachment and the affirmed dharma-aligned desire -> flat "desire is bad" destroys the verse's discrimination.

**guṇa (गुण)** — strand/quality (sattva, rajas, tamas) -> the deluding modes of nature's display -> "quality" as adjective misses cosmological psychology.

**avyaya (अव्यय)** — imperishable, non-expendable -> the Lord beyond guṇa-states -> "eternal" misses the sense of not being used up by manifestation.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Stoic doctrine of preferred indifferents (Epictetus, Discourses):** Both allow natural impulses when they do not violate right relation.
*Divergence:* Stoicism centers *prohairesis*; Gītā centers the Lord's asymmetrical relation to the guṇas.

**Laozi on soft power / non-contention:** Both critique brute strength as spiritual measure.
*Divergence:* Daoist soft power is impersonal efficacy; Gītā names strength *as* the Lord when free of *kāma-rāga*.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Before one act of force today (speech, decision, physical effort), ask: "Is this strength free of craving to dominate?" If not, reduce the force by half and redo the act.

## Māyā Crossed Only by Refuge
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.14

This divine māyā of Mine, consisting of the guṇas, is hard to cross. Those who take refuge in Me alone cross this māyā.

---

### Devanagari
दैवी ह्येषा गुणमयी मम माया दुरत्यया।

मामेव ये प्रपद्यन्ते मायामेतां तरन्ति ते

### IAST
daivī hyeṣā guṇamayī mama māyā duratyayā|

māmeva ye prapadyante māyāmetāṃ taranti te

### Pratibha Translation
This divine [*daivī*] māyā of Mine, made of the guṇas, is hard to cross [*duratyayā*]. Those who take refuge in Me alone cross this māyā.

### Pratibha Commentary
The veil is not a demon's trick against God; it is *God's own* divine power, guṇa-constituted and hard to cross. The counterintuitive claim is that the obstacle to knowing God is God-authored — *mama māyā* — so technique aimed at "defeating illusion by myself" is structurally confused. Crossing happens by *prapadana*: taking refuge in the same One whose māyā binds. This is not anti-intellectualism; it is a diagnosis of agency. Guṇa-māyā cannot be out-clevered from inside guṇa-logic. Existentially, people try to think their way out of fog with the same restless or dull mind that generates fog. The verse redirects: change the relational base (refuge), and the crossing becomes possible — not because analysis stops, but because analysis is no longer the sole climber. Calling māyā daivī blocks the paranoia that the universe is a hostile trick. The same Lord who veils also receives refuge. Crossing, therefore, is not a jailbreak from God but a return into God against the grain of guṇa-habit. This re-reads spiritual struggle: the hardness is expected; the method is relational; the pride of "I will outsmart illusion" is itself a guṇa-move. Refuge is not passivity — it is the only agency that matches the problem's scale.

### Key Terms
**māyā (माया)** — measuring/artifice/power of appearance -> here the Lord's divine, guṇa-made world-veiling/enabling power -> "illusion" as fake world misses that it is *daivī* and *mama*.

**prapad- (प्रपद्)** — to approach/fall toward/take refuge -> exclusive resort to Kṛṣṇa as the crossing method -> "surrender" as emotional collapse misses the technical sense of changing where agency is lodged.

**duratyayā (दुरत्यया)** — hard to cross beyond -> difficulty is structural, not accidental -> "difficult" understates intractability from inside the guṇas.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Aśvaghoṣa / Mahāyāna on *māyā* as empty appearance:** Both treat appearance as not ultimately self-standing.
*Divergence:* Buddhist *māyā* serves emptiness pedagogy; Gītā's *māyā* is theistic power crossed by refuge in the Lord.

**Paul, Romans 7 (wretched man / law of mind vs flesh):** Both describe captivity that self-effort cannot simply overpower.
*Divergence:* Paul frames sin and grace in Christ; Gītā frames guṇa-māyā and *prapadana* to Kṛṣṇa.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
When you notice looping mental weather today, stop explaining it for one minute and say once, slowly: "This is hard to cross alone; I take refuge." Then take the next necessary action without waiting for the weather to clear.

## Four Who Worship — and the Knower Who Excels
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.15–7.17

The evil-doers, the deluded, the lowest of men do not take refuge in Me — their knowledge carried off by māyā, they take shelter in demonic disposition. Four kinds of people of good deeds worship Me, O Arjuna: the afflicted, the seeker of knowledge, the seeker of wealth, and the knower, O Best of Bharatas. Of these, the knower, ever joined, of single devotion, excels; for I am exceedingly dear to the knower, and he is dear to Me.

---

### Devanagari
न मां दुष्कृतिनो मूढाः प्रपद्यन्ते नराधमाः।

माययापहृतज्ञाना आसुरं भावमाश्रिताः

चतुर्विधा भजन्ते मां जनाः सुकृतिनोऽर्जुन।

आर्तो जिज्ञासुरर्थार्थी ज्ञानी च भरतर्षभ

तेषां ज्ञानी नित्ययुक्त एकभक्ितर्विशिष्यते।

प्रियो हि ज्ञानिनोऽत्यर्थमहं स च मम प्रियः

### IAST
na māṃ duṣkṛtino mūḍhāḥ prapadyante narādhamāḥ|

māyayāpahṛtajñānā āsuraṃ bhāvamāśritāḥ

caturvidhā bhajante māṃ janāḥ sukṛtino'rjuna|

ārto jijñāsurarthārthī jñānī ca bharatarṣabha

teṣāṃ jñānī nityayukta ekabhakitarviśiṣyate|

priyo hi jñānino'tyarthamahaṃ sa ca mama priyaḥ

### Pratibha Translation
Wrong-doers, the deluded, the lowest of men do not take refuge in Me — knowledge stolen by māyā, they resort to a demonic [*āsura*] disposition. Four kinds of well-doing people worship Me, O Arjuna: the afflicted [*ārta*], the one who wants to know [*jijñāsu*], the one who wants gain [*arthārthī*], and the knower [*jñānī*], O Best of Bharatas. Of these, the knower, ever yoked, of single devotion [*eka-bhakti*], excels; I am exceedingly dear to the knower, and he is dear to Me.

### Pratibha Commentary
Devotion is graded without being scorned at the lower rungs. The contested move is that crisis-prayer, curiosity, and even desire for gain are admitted as real *bhajana* of good-doers — yet *jñānī* with *eka-bhakti* is singled out as excelling, in mutual dearness with the Lord. Knowledge here is not cold doctrine; it is knowing that stabilizes into exclusive devotion. Refusal of refuge is explained not as mere atheism but as knowledge-theft by māyā plus shelter in *āsura* disposition — a moral-ontological climate. Existentially this legitimizes starting where you are (pain, questions, needs) while refusing to confuse starting motives with the mature form. The path does not shame need; it does not crown need as the end. The typology also protects community from purity-spirals. A temple full only of jñānīs is a fantasy; a temple that shames the ārta for needing help contradicts the Lord's own admission of four. Yet refusing all ranking collapses formation: without eka-bhakti as excellence, religion becomes crisis-management and wish-fulfillment forever. Mutual dearness between Lord and knower names love as the climate of mature knowing — not a trophy for IQ, but the warmth of non-dual recognition.

### Key Terms
**jijñāsu (जिज्ञासु)** — desirous of knowing -> seeker whose worship is inquiry-shaped -> "curious person" misses that inquiry itself is a mode of *bhajana*.

**eka-bhakti (एकभक्ति)** — single/exclusive devotion -> the knower's distinguishing mark with *nitya-yukta* -> "loyalty" understates epistemic exclusivity: one devotion because one real object.

**āsura (आसुर)** — demonic/anti-divine disposition -> shelter taken when knowledge is stolen -> "evil" as cartoon villainy misses a systematic orientation against the sacred.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Augustine, Confessions (restless heart / grades of desire for God):** Both admit lower loves as real motions toward the divine while ranking them.
*Divergence:* Augustine narrates personal conversion history; Gītā offers a typology inside a battlefield teaching.

**Confucian graded love / care with distinction:** Both refuse flat equality of all motives as spiritually identical.
*Divergence:* Confucian grading is familial-social; Gītā grades modes of worship toward the Lord.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Name which of the four currently dominates your prayer (pain, curiosity, gain, knowing). Practice one act today as if you were one step "up" the list — without pretending you have arrived.

## The Knower Is My Very Self
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.18

All these are noble, but the knower is My very Self, I hold; for he, with self yoked, is established in Me alone as the unsurpassed goal.

---

### Devanagari
उदाराः सर्व एवैते ज्ञानी त्वात्मैव मे मतम्।

आस्थितः स हि युक्तात्मा मामेवानुत्तमां गतिम्

### IAST
udārāḥ sarva evaite jñānī tvātmaiva me matam|

āsthitaḥ sa hi yuktātmā māmevānuttamāṃ gatim

### Pratibha Translation
All these are noble [*udāra*], but the knower is My very Self [*ātman*], so I hold; established, that yoked-selfed one has Me alone as the unsurpassed goal [*gati*].

### Pratibha Commentary
Ranking does not cancel generosity. The Lord first calls *all four* noble — then intensifies: the *jñānī* is not merely preferred devotee but *ātmaiva me* — "My very Self." The contested identity-claim is reciprocal recognition: the one who knows does not stand over against God as successful subject; knowing collapses distance into Self-recognition. Yet the verse still speaks of *gati* (goal) and *yuktātman*, so identity is not crude pantheistic erasure of practice. Existentially this warns against spiritual competition among "types" of devotee while clarifying the telos: nobility of need-based worship is real; non-dual intimacy of knowing is the unsurpassed arrival. "All these are noble" must be kept audible beside "the knower is My Self," or ranking becomes contempt. The yuktātman established in the unsurpassed gati shows identity as orientation, not as a metaphysical selfie. You do not announce "I am God" as a brand; you become so joined that the Lord recognizes His own. The existential test is quiet: does knowing increase refuge and soften scorn toward those still driven by pain or gain?

### Key Terms
**udāra (उदार)** — noble, generous, exalted -> evaluation of all four worshippers -> "good people" misses the Lord's refusal to despise lower motives.

**anuttamā gati (अनुत्तमा गति)** — unsurpassed goal/refuge/path -> Me alone as final destination of the knower -> "goal" as achievement object misses *gati* as going-and-refuge.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad ("This Self is Brahman"):** Both enact identity of knower and ultimate.
*Divergence:* Upaniṣadic formula is impersonal; Gītā embeds identity in Kṛṣṇa's first-person evaluation of the devotee-knower.

**Meister Eckhart on the birth of the Word in the soul:** Both speak of intimacy that exceeds ordinary piety.
*Divergence:* Eckhart's idiom is apophatic Christian mysticism; Gītā keeps personal Lord-devotee grammar even in *ātmaiva* claim.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Hold both thoughts for one minute without resolving them: "My current devotion is noble" and "The goal is Me alone as Self" — then act from the second without scorning the first.

## After Many Births: Vāsudeva Is All
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.19

At the end of many births, the one who has knowledge takes refuge in Me, knowing "Vāsudeva is all this"; that great soul is exceedingly hard to find.

---

### Devanagari
बहूनां जन्मनामन्ते ज्ञानवान्मां प्रपद्यते।

वासुदेवः सर्वमिति स महात्मा सुदुर्लभः

### IAST
bahūnāṃ janmanāmante jñānavānmāṃ prapadyate|

vāsudevaḥ sarvamiti sa mahātmā sudurlabhaḥ

### Pratibha Translation
At the end of many births, the one possessed of knowledge takes refuge in Me, knowing "Vāsudeva is all [*sarvam*]"; that great-souled one [*mahātman*] is exceedingly rare [*sudurlabha*].

### Pratibha Commentary
The summit formula is not "I am God" as ego inflation; it is "Vāsudeva is all" as world-recognition, sealed by refuge. The contested synthesis binds *jñāna* and *prapadana*: knowing issues in taking shelter, and shelter is informed by a universal claim. Rarity across many births refuses both instant enlightenment marketing and despair that rarity makes practice pointless. Existentially the verse gives a criterion: if your "non-dual insight" does not end in refuge, and if your refuge does not see all as Vāsudeva, something is still partial. The *mahātman* is rare because this double completion is rare — not because God is stingy. Many births as horizon saves the verse from both panic and laziness. Panic says "I must force the mahātman state tonight"; laziness says "rarity means not me." The middle is patient intensity: practice the formula and the refuge now, without fake completion. "Vāsudeva is all" judged only by how it treats the next person and the next duty — otherwise non-dual speech becomes a sophisticated dvandva of spiritual pride.

### Key Terms
**Vāsudeva (वासुदेव)** — "God of all / Kṛṣṇa as all-pervading" -> the name that can bear the predication *sarvam* -> reading as mere sectarian surname guts the ontological claim.

**sudurlabha (सुदुर्लभ)** — extremely hard to obtain -> rarity of the *mahātman* who knows and takes refuge -> "rare" as collectible status misses pedagogical honesty about time-depth (*bahūnāṃ janmanām*).

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Chāndogya's *sarvaṃ khalv idaṃ brahma*:** Both universalize the ultimate as all this.
*Divergence:* Chāndogya's Brahman is impersonal Absolute; here the all is named Vāsudeva and joined to refuge.

**Heraclitus (listening to the Logos):** Both imply most miss the one principle that is common.
*Divergence:* Heraclitus' Logos is not a refuge-granting person; Gītā's all is personally takeable as shelter.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
For ten minutes, look at ordinary objects and people and silently repeat "Vāsudeva is all this" — then end by bowing (head or hand) as refuge, so the formula does not stay as slogan.

## Stolen Knowledge and Delegated Faith
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.20–7.22

Those whose knowledge is carried off by this or that desire take refuge in other deities; they adopt this or that rule, constrained by their own nature. Whichever form a devotee desires to worship with faith — that very firm faith I make steady for him. Joined to that faith, he strives to worship that form and obtains his desires — those desires being ordained by Me alone.

---

### Devanagari
कामैस्तैस्तैर्हृतज्ञानाः प्रपद्यन्तेऽन्यदेवताः।

तं तं नियममास्थाय प्रकृत्या नियताः स्वया

यो यो यां यां तनुं भक्तः श्रद्धयार्चितुमिच्छति।

तस्य तस्याचलां श्रद्धां तामेव विदधाम्यहम्

स तया श्रद्धया युक्तस्तस्याराधनमीहते।

लभते च ततः कामान्मयैव विहितान् हि तान्

### IAST
kāmaistaistairhṛtajñānāḥ prapadyante'nyadevatāḥ|

taṃ taṃ niyamamāsthāya prakṛtyā niyatāḥ svayā

yo yo yāṃ yāṃ tanuṃ bhaktaḥ śraddhayārcitumicchati|

tasya tasyācalāṃ śraddhāṃ tāmeva vidadhāmyaham

sa tayā śraddhayā yuktastasyārādhanamīhate|

labhate ca tataḥ kāmānmayaiva vihitān hi tān

### Pratibha Translation
Those whose knowledge is stolen by various desires take refuge in other deities; adopting this or that discipline, they are constrained by their own nature [*prakṛti*]. Whichever form a devotee wants to worship with faith [*śraddhā*] — that very unshaking faith I establish for him. Joined to that faith he strives in that worship and obtains desires from it — and those desires are indeed arranged by Me alone.

### Pratibha Commentary
Plural worship is neither simply condemned nor simply endorsed. Desire-stolen knowledge explains why people resort to other deities and matching rule-sets: *prakṛti* channels the seeking. Yet Kṛṣṇa claims sovereignty inside that pluralism: He stabilizes the faith and dispenses the fruits. The contested theology is inclusive causality without equal ultimacy — other worship "works" because He underwrites it, not because every object of worship is equally the Absolute. Existentially this cuts two arrogances: sectarian denial that others' sincere faith has any efficacy, and relativist denial that desire-shaped deity-choice can be a knowledge-theft. Your faith may be steadied and your wants granted while your knowing remains partial. The Lord's underwriting of others' faith is a severe mercy: fruits may come, faith may stabilize, and still knowledge remain stolen by kāma. Success is not proof of ultimacy. This frees interreligious honesty from both hatred and flattery. You can admit efficacy without conceding equal finality, and you can critique desire-shaped deity-choice without denying that grace may be operating inside it. The personal question is sharper: which desire is currently appointing my god?

### Key Terms
**śraddhā (श्रद्धा)** — faith/trust/placing the heart -> here faith that Kṛṣṇa Himself makes unshaking toward a chosen form -> "belief" as opinion misses that faith is divinely stabilized.

**tanu (तनु)** — body/form -> the deity-form chosen for worship -> "idol" as pejorative misses the text's neutral technical use inside a hierarchy of ends.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Aquinas on grace perfecting nature / God as cause of secondary causes:** Both can affirm finite goods and finite cults without surrendering one first cause.
*Divergence:* Thomas works in Christian creation metaphysics; Gītā frames desire, *prakṛti*, and deity-forms inside *bhakti* typology.

**Confucian ritual plurality under Heaven (*tian*):** Both allow multiple rites under a higher order.
*Divergence:* Heaven is not a personal granter of *śraddhā* in the Gītā's first-person sense.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Notice one desire that currently shapes what you "pray to" (status, security, approval). Ask whether knowledge is being stolen by that desire; keep the desire visible rather than baptizing it as pure devotion.

## Finite Fruit, Hidden Lord
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.23–7.25

But the fruit of those of small understanding is finite; worshippers of the gods go to the gods; My devotees go to Me. The unintelligent think Me, the unmanifest, as having come to manifestation, not knowing My higher being, imperishable and unsurpassed. I am not evident to all, veiled by yoga-māyā; this deluded world does not recognize Me, unborn and imperishable.

---

### Devanagari
अन्तवत्तु फलं तेषां तद्भवत्यल्पमेधसाम्।

देवान्देवयजो यान्ति मद्भक्ता यान्ति मामपि

अव्यक्तं व्यक्ितमापन्नं मन्यन्ते मामबुद्धयः।

परं भावमजानन्तो ममाव्ययमनुत्तमम्

नाहं प्रकाशः सर्वस्य योगमायासमावृतः।

मूढोऽयं नाभिजानाति लोको मामजमव्ययम्

### IAST
antavattu phalaṃ teṣāṃ tadbhavatyalpamedhasām|

devāndevayajo yānti madbhaktā yānti māmapi

avyaktaṃ vyakitamāpannaṃ manyante māmabuddhayaḥ|

paraṃ bhāvamajānanto mamāvyayamanuttamam

nāhaṃ prakāśaḥ sarvasya yogamāyāsamāvṛtaḥ|

mūḍho'yaṃ nābhijānāti loko māmajamavyayam

### Pratibha Translation
But finite is the fruit of those of small understanding; worshippers of the gods go to the gods; My devotees come to Me as well. The unwise think Me, the unmanifest [*avyakta*], as having merely become manifest, not knowing My higher being — imperishable, unsurpassed. I am not luminous to all, enveloped by yoga-māyā; this deluded world does not recognize Me, the unborn, the imperishable.

### Pratibha Commentary
Like goes to like: finite worship yields finite worlds; devotion to Kṛṣṇa yields Kṛṣṇa. The contested epistemology is about misreading manifestation: fools take the visible form as the whole story of an arrival from unmanifest to manifest, missing *paraṃ bhāvam* — higher being that is never used up by appearance. Yoga-māyā as envelope explains non-obviousness without making God absent: veiled, not vacant. Existentially this rebukes both celebrity-spirituality (the form is the entire content) and abstract denial of form (as if appearance could not be divine). The test is fruit and destination: what does your worship *go to*, and does it end? Small understanding is measured by fruit-horizon, not by ritual complexity. Elaborate worship aimed at temporary heavens remains alpamedhas if the destination is finite. Yoga-māyā's envelope then explains why cleverness does not guarantee recognition: hiddenness is structural. The devotee's privilege is not visual flash but destination — "My devotees go to Me." Practice becomes destination-auditing: where does this act send me, even when it "works"?

### Key Terms
**avyakta (अव्यक्त)** — unmanifest -> Kṛṣṇa's being misread when form is taken as exhaustive -> "invisible" misses technical contrast with *vyakta* manifestation.

**yoga-māyā (योगमाया)** — the Lord's yogic veiling power -> why He is not *prakāśa* to all -> "magic trick" trivializes a systematic account of hiddenness.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**John 1 (light shines in darkness / world did not know him):** Both narrate divine presence unmet by recognition.
*Divergence:* Christian incarnation grammar differs from Gītā's *avyakta*/*vyakta* and yoga-māyā frame.

**Nagarjuna on emptiness of views:** Both warn that grasping appearance as ultimate misleads.
*Divergence:* Madhyamaka dissolves intrinsic nature; Gītā affirms an unsurpassed personal *bhāva* behind the veil.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
After any religious or aesthetic experience today, ask two questions: "What finite fruit am I chasing?" and "What higher being might this appearance be failing to disclose?"

## Desire-Aversion Fog and Firm Vow
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.26–7.28

I know the beings that are past, present, and future, O Arjuna; but no one knows Me. By the delusion of the pairs of opposites arising from desire and aversion, O Bhārata, all beings fall into delusion at birth, O Scorcher of Foes. But those people of meritorious deeds whose evil has come to an end, freed from the delusion of the pairs, worship Me with firm vows.

---

### Devanagari
वेदाहं समतीतानि वर्तमानानि चार्जुन।

भविष्याणि च भूतानि मां तु वेद न कश्चन

इच्छाद्वेषसमुत्थेन द्वन्द्वमोहेन भारत।

सर्वभूतानि संमोहं सर्गे यान्ति परन्तप

येषां त्वन्तगतं पापं जनानां पुण्यकर्मणाम्।

ते द्वन्द्वमोहनिर्मुक्ता भजन्ते मां दृढव्रताः

### IAST
vedāhaṃ samatītāni vartamānāni cārjuna|

bhaviṣyāṇi ca bhūtāni māṃ tu veda na kaścana

icchādveṣasamutthena dvandvamohena bhārata|

sarvabhūtāni saṃmohaṃ sarge yānti parantapa

yeṣāṃ tvantagataṃ pāpaṃ janānāṃ puṇyakarmaṇām|

te dvandvamohanirmuktā bhajante māṃ dṛḍhavratāḥ

### Pratibha Translation
I know beings past, present, and future, O Arjuna; but no one knows Me. Through the delusion of dualities [*dvandva-moha*] born of desire and aversion [*icchā-dveṣa*], O Bhārata, all beings go to confusion at their arising, O Scorcher of Foes. But those whose evil has ended, people of meritorious action, freed from duality-delusion, worship Me with firm vows [*dṛḍha-vrata*].

### Pratibha Commentary
Epistemic asymmetry is absolute: the Lord knows the temporal field of beings; beings, birth-deluded by liking and disliking, do not know Him. The contested anthropology locates primordial confusion not in lack of data but in *dvandva-moha* — hypnosis by pairs (gain/loss, praise/blame, pleasure/pain) sprung from *icchā* and *dveṣa*. Liberation of worship is therefore ethical-ascetical before it is speculative: evil ending, merit accumulating, duality-delusion releasing, vow firming. Existentially this explains why "I want to know God" stalls — wanting and not-wanting are still running the show. Firm vow is not stubborn dogma; it is practice that no longer renegotiates orientation every time a pair of opposites flares. "No one knows Me" is not skepticism about revelation; it is anthropology under dvandva. Omniscience on the Lord's side and birth-delusion on the creature's side set the stakes for vow. Firmness is possible when pāpa has been worked through enough that pairs no longer renegotiate the center every hour. This makes ethics preparatory for knowing: not moralism as ticket-purchase, but the clearing of reactive fog so bhajana can stabilize.

### Key Terms
**dvandva-moha (द्वन्द्वमोह)** — delusion of the dualities -> birth-deep confusion via paired opposites -> "confusion" alone misses the structured role of binary reactivity.

**dṛḍha-vrata (दृढव्रत)** — firm vow/observance -> mark of those freed enough to worship steadily -> "willpower" psychologizes what the text ties to exhausted *pāpa* and *puṇya*-action.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Buddha's Second Noble Truth (taṇhā as origin of suffering):** Both root bondage in craving/aversion dynamics.
*Divergence:* Buddhism aims at cessation of thirst; Gītā aims at firm-vowed *bhajana* of the Lord who knows all times.

**Epictetus on what is up to us vs not:** Both diagnose suffering in reactive pairing with externals.
*Divergence:* Stoic therapy centers *prohairesis*; Gītā centers release into worship under divine omniscience.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Catch one desire-aversion pair today (like/dislike). Do not suppress it; label it "*dvandva*" and take one vowed action you already know is right, regardless of the pair's pull.

## Knowing Me Through Death's Edge
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 7.29–7.30

Those who strive, taking refuge in Me, for liberation from old age and death — they know that Brahman entire, the whole of adhyātma, and action without remainder. Those who know Me as including adhibhūta, adhidaiva, and adhiyajña — even at the time of departure they know Me, their minds yoked.

---

### Devanagari
जरामरणमोक्षाय मामाश्रित्य यतन्ति ये।

ते ब्रह्म तद्विदुः कृत्स्नमध्यात्मं कर्म चाखिलम्

साधिभूताधिदैवं मां साधियज्ञं च ये विदुः।

प्रयाणकालेऽपि च मां ते विदुर्युक्तचेतसः

### IAST
jarāmaraṇamokṣāya māmāśritya yatanti ye|

te brahma tadviduḥ kṛtsnamadhyātmaṃ karma cākhilam

sādhibhūtādhidaivaṃ māṃ sādhiyajñaṃ ca ye viduḥ|

prayāṇakāle'pi ca māṃ te viduryuktacetasaḥ

### Pratibha Translation
Those who strive, taking refuge in Me, for liberation from old age and death — they know that Brahman, the entire field of inwardness [*adhyātma*], and action [*karma*] without remainder. Those who know Me together with the elemental [*adhibhūta*], the divine [*adhidaiva*], and the sacrificial [*adhiyajña*] — even at the hour of departure they know Me, their awareness yoked [*yukta-cetas*].

### Pratibha Commentary
Chapter 7 closes by turning knowledge toward death. Refuge-based striving for freedom from aging and dying yields a complete knowing: Brahman, inwardness, and action entire — a triad that refuses splitting metaphysics, psychology, and ethics. The further claim is that knowing Kṛṣṇa *with* the cosmic registers (elemental, divine, sacrificial) holds at *prayāṇa-kāla* — departure time — if the mind is yoked. The contested pastoral point: deathbed clarity is not a separate technique invented at the last minute; it is the fruition of a knowing already practiced as refuge. Existentially this makes preparation concrete: what you know only as idea will not be what you know when the body loosens. Yoked awareness is the continuity between study and dying. Chapter 7's closing triad — Brahman, adhyātma, karma entire — refuses the split between metaphysics, inwardness, and action that modern religiosity often accepts. Knowing that holds at departure must already include how you act and how you inhabit the inner field. The adhi- registers prevent a private mysticism that ignores world, gods, and sacrifice. Dying then becomes continuous with living: yukta-cetas is trained in ordinary hours or it will not appear in the last hour by wish.

### Key Terms
**adhyātma (अध्यात्म)** — pertaining to the inner self -> the whole inward field known with Brahman and karma -> "spirituality" is too vague; it is a technical register beside *adhibhūta* and *adhidaiva*.

**prayāṇa-kāla (प्रयाणकाल)** — time of departure/death -> the stress-test of knowing -> "death" alone misses the journeying sense of *prayāṇa*.

**yukta-cetas (युक्तचेतस्)** — yoked mind/heart -> condition for knowing at departure -> "calm" understates active yogic joining.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Phaedo (philosophy as practice of dying):** Both bind true knowing to readiness for death.
*Divergence:* Plato's Socrates aims at soul's release to Forms; Gītā aims at knowing the Lord across cosmic registers at departure.

**Tibetan *bar do* instructions:** Both insist that recognition at death depends on prior training.
*Divergence:* Bardo lore maps intermediate states; Gītā compresses the criterion to yoked knowing of Kṛṣṇa with *adhi-* registers.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Once today, while fully alive and ordinary, rehearse a single clear thought you would want to be true of your mind at departure: refuge, not panic. Hold it for ten breaths, then return to the next task.

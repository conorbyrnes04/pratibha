# Bhagavad Gītā — Chapter 13 (Kṣetra-Kṣetrajña Vibhāga Yoga)
**Corpus entry:** Bhagavad Gītā / Mahābhārata
**Units:** 15 (one per brief CHUNK)

## The Question That Opens the Field
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 13.1

Arjuna said: I wish to know nature [prakṛti] and the person [puruṣa], the field [kṣetra] and the knower of the field [kṣetrajña], and also knowledge [jñāna] and what is to be known [jñeya], O Keśava.

---

### Devanagari
अर्जुन उवाच

प्रकृतिं पुरुषं चैव क्षेत्रं क्षेत्रज्ञमेव च।

एतद्वेदितुमिच्छामि ज्ञानं ज्ञेयं च केशव

### IAST
arjuna uvāca

prakṛtiṃ puruṣaṃ caiva kṣetraṃ kṣetrajñameva ca|

etadveditumicchāmi jñānaṃ jñeyaṃ ca keśava

### Pratibha Translation
Arjuna said: I wish to know nature [prakṛti] and the person [puruṣa], the field [kṣetra] and the knower of the field [kṣetrajña], and also knowledge [jñāna] and what is to be known [jñeya], O Keśava.

### Pratibha Commentary
Liberation begins when the seeker stops asking for consoling images and asks instead for a map of experience itself. Arjuna’s sixfold request is not scholastic curiosity; it is the demand that philosophy name the difference between what appears and that which knows appearance. The contested move is already present in the pairing: prakṛti and puruṣa are not two substances floating in a cosmos, but two functions within the same lived field—productivity and witnessing. Kṣetra and kṣetrajña sharpen the same cut at the level of the body: experience is a terrain, and awareness is its knower. Jñāna and jñeya then refuse the fantasy that “knowledge” is a pile of facts; knowledge is a way of seeing, and the knowable is what that seeing opens. In the Gītā’s pedagogy, this chapter turns the battlefield inward. Before Krishna can teach freedom from action’s binding, he must teach the distinction that makes freedom intelligible. Existentially, the verse invites a ruthless inventory: which of my crises are field-events, and which confusion comes from mistaking the knower for the known?

### Key Terms
**prakṛti (प्रकृति)** — pra- + kṛ, “that which brings forth” -> productive nature as the matrix of experience -> “nature” as scenery misses its role as the active generator of thought, desire, and form.
**puruṣa (पुरुष)** — “person / dwelling in the city (of the body)” -> the witnessing principle amid prakṛti’s play -> “soul” as a ghostly substance misses its functional definition as knower.
**kṣetra (क्षेत्र)** — “field / cultivated ground” -> the body-world as experiential terrain -> softens into “body” alone and loses the agricultural metaphor of what can be worked and known.
**kṣetrajña (क्षेत्रज्ञ)** — “knower of the field” -> consciousness as that which cognizes the field -> “soul in the body” misses the epistemic cut the chapter will radicalize.
**jñeya (ज्ञेय)** — “that which is to be known” -> the ultimate knowable, later identified with Brahman -> “object of knowledge” flattens the soteriological stakes.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Patañjali, Yoga Sūtra I.2–3]:** Both texts open liberation by distinguishing citta-vṛtti from the seer’s resting in its own form.
*Divergence:* Patañjali’s seer is defined through stillness of fluctuation; Krishna answers Arjuna by expanding the field into a full ontology of nature, person, and knowable.
**[Plotinus, Enneads IV.3.9]:** The soul’s relation to body as a kind of habitation parallels kṣetra/kṣetrajña as terrain and dweller-knower.
*Divergence:* Plotinus climbs toward the One by ascent; the Gītā will keep the knower immanent in every field while still calling for discriminative seeing.
**[Zhuangzi, “Discussion on Making All Things Equal”]:** Both unsettle naive subject-object certainty before teaching a freer stance amid phenomena.
*Divergence:* Zhuangzi dissolves fixed standpoints into perspectival play; Krishna preserves a stable knower-field distinction as the condition of release.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
For one hour, label every strong experience as either “field” (sensation, thought, story, body-event) or “knowing” (the simple fact of awareness). Do not fix anything; only refuse to collapse the two.

## This Body Is Named the Field
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 13.2

The Blessed Lord said: This body, O son of Kuntī, is called the field. The one who knows it—those who know such things call that one the knower of the field.

---

### Devanagari
श्री भगवानुवाचइदं शरीरं कौन्तेय क्षेत्रमित्यभिधीयते।एतद्यो वेत्ति तं प्राहुः क्षेत्रज्ञ इति तद्विदः

### IAST
śrī bhagavānuvācaidaṃ śarīraṃ kaunteya kṣetramityabhidhīyate|etadyo vetti taṃ prāhuḥ kṣetrajña iti tadvidaḥ

### Pratibha Translation
The Blessed Lord said: This body, O son of Kuntī, is called the field. The one who knows it—those who know such things call that one the knower of the field.

### Pratibha Commentary
Krishna’s first cut is almost blunt: the body is not the self; it is a field. The contested move is not body-hatred but epistemic relocation. “Field” implies cultivation, boundaries, seasons, and harvest—an arena of processes rather than an identity. To call the knower kṣetrajña is to refuse the ordinary identification in which pain, aging, and social role swallow the subject whole. The tradition-specific force of the verse is Sāṃkhya-inflected Vedānta: knowledge begins when the seer is named as seer. Those who “know such things” (tadvidaḥ) are not a club of metaphysicians; they are anyone who can sustain the distinction under pressure. Existentially, the teaching lands in the moment you say “I am broken” and discover that brokenness is occurring in the field while knowing remains available. Freedom does not wait for a different body; it begins when the body is correctly named.  The naming is already a practice: each time pain or praise arrives, the word “field” keeps the knower from drowning in the event’s story.

### Key Terms
**śarīra (शरीर)** — “that which wastes away / is supported” -> the living body as field -> “body” alone misses the implication of composite, decaying process.
**kṣetra (क्षेत्र)** — field as cultivated terrain -> body as knowable process-space -> medical “body” language misses the contemplative agrarian image.
**vetti (वेत्ति)** — “knows / discerns” from vid -> knowing as discriminative recognition of the field -> “understands” can sound merely conceptual.
**kṣetrajña (क्षेत्रज्ञ)** — knower-of-field -> the cognizing principle relative to embodiment -> “soul” smuggles substance where the verse offers a relational title.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Chāndogya Upaniṣad VIII.1]:** The body as a city of Brahman parallels the body as field known by a subtler resident-knower.
*Divergence:* Chāndogya emphasizes the lotus of the heart as inner palace; Krishna’s first move is colder and more analytical: rename the body as field.
**[Marcus Aurelius, Meditations II.17]:** Stoic separation of body-events from the ruling faculty mirrors field vs knower.
*Divergence:* Stoicism trains assent within a providential cosmos; the Gītā will soon identify the knower with the Lord in all fields.
**[Dōgen, Genjōkōan]:** Studying the self by studying what appears and what studies appearance resonates with kṣetra/kṣetrajña.
*Divergence:* Dōgen dissolves study into non-dual practice-realization; Krishna keeps the distinction pedagogically sharp before non-dual climax.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Place a hand on your chest. Silently say: “This is the field.” Then notice the knowing that hears the sentence. Hold both for ten breaths without merging them.

## Know Me as the Knower in Every Field
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 13.3

Know Me also as the knower of the field in all fields, O Bhārata. Knowledge of the field and of the knower of the field—that, in My view, is knowledge.

---

### Devanagari
क्षेत्रज्ञं चापि मां विद्धि सर्वक्षेत्रेषु भारत।

क्षेत्रक्षेत्रज्ञयोर्ज्ञानं यत्तज्ज्ञानं मतं मम

### IAST
kṣetrajñaṃ cāpi māṃ viddhi sarvakṣetreṣu bhārata|

kṣetrakṣetrajñayorjñānaṃ yattajjñānaṃ mataṃ mama

### Pratibha Translation
Know Me also as the knower of the field in all fields, O Bhārata. Knowledge of the field and of the knower of the field—that, in My view, is knowledge.

### Pratibha Commentary
The verse performs a theological coup inside an analytic distinction. First Krishna separates field and knower; then he identifies the knower in every field with himself. The contested move is double: individuality is neither denied nor absolutized. There is a knower of this body, and that knower is not finally private property. True knowledge (jñāna) is therefore not information about matter, nor mystical ego-inflation, but simultaneous recognition of locality and universality: this field is known, and the knowing is the Lord’s knowing. In the Gītā’s theistic Sāṃkhya, discrimination becomes devotion’s intellect. Existentially, the claim rewires ethics: if the knower in every field is one, injury to another field is not merely “harm to another”; it is blindness about the shared knower. The verse also blocks spiritual narcissism: you do not become God by claiming the field; you recognize the knower already present.  This is why the chapter can later speak of ethics so sharply: shared knowing makes cruelty a form of metaphysical illiteracy, not merely bad manners.

### Key Terms
**sarvakṣetreṣu (सर्वक्षेत्रेषु)** — “in all fields” -> omnipresence of the knower across bodies -> “everywhere” misses the precise localization in each embodied field.
**viddhi (विद्धि)** — imperative of vid, “know!” -> recognition as command, not suggestion -> “understand” softens the urgency of re-identification.
**jñāna (ज्ञान)** — knowing -> here, knowledge as the field/knower distinction itself -> “wisdom” can float free of this exact cut.
**mataṃ mama (मतं मम)** — “My view / My teaching” -> Krishna’s authoritative definition of knowledge -> “opinion” trivializes the doctrinal stake.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Īśā Upaniṣad 1]:** The Lord’s pervasion of all that moves resonates with the knower present in every field.
*Divergence:* Īśā leads through renunciation and enjoyment; here the hinge is epistemic: redefine knowledge as field-knower insight.
**[Meister Eckhart, Sermon on poverty of spirit]:** The ground of the soul as one with God’s ground parallels one knower in all fields.
*Divergence:* Eckhart stresses detachment unto the Godhead beyond images; Krishna retains personal address (“know Me”) inside the non-dual claim.
**[Rūmī, Mathnawī II (elephant in the dark)]:** Multiple partial knowings of one reality echo many fields, one knower.
*Divergence:* Rūmī’s parable warns against partial sensory claims; Krishna asserts a positive identity of the knower with the Lord.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
When you meet another person today, silently note: “Another field; the same knower.” Watch what changes in tone, haste, and judgment.

## Hear the Field in Summary
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 13.4–13.6

Hear from Me in brief what that field is, of what kind it is, what its modifications are, whence it arises, and who he is, and what his powers are. This has been sung in many ways by seers, separately in various Vedic meters, and also in the reasoned and decisive statements of the Brahma-sūtras. The great elements, ego-sense [ahaṅkāra], intellect [buddhi], the unmanifest, the ten senses and one (mind), and the five sense-objects—

---

### Devanagari
तत्क्षेत्रं यच्च यादृक् च यद्विकारि यतश्च यत्।स च यो यत्प्रभावश्च तत्समासेन मे श्रृणु
ऋषिभिर्बहुधा गीतं छन्दोभिर्विविधैः पृथक्।ब्रह्मसूत्रपदैश्चैव हेतुमद्भिर्विनिश्िचतैः
महाभूतान्यहङ्कारो बुद्धिरव्यक्तमेव च।इन्द्रियाणि दशैकं च पञ्च चेन्द्रियगोचराः

### IAST
tatkṣetraṃ yacca yādṛk ca yadvikāri yataśca yat|sa ca yo yatprabhāvaśca tatsamāsena me śrṛṇu
ṛṣibhirbahudhā gītaṃ chandobhirvividhaiḥ pṛthak|brahmasūtrapadaiścaiva hetumadbhirviniśicataiḥ
mahābhūtānyahaṅkāro buddhiravyaktameva ca|indriyāṇi daśaikaṃ ca pañca cendriyagocarāḥ

### Pratibha Translation
Hear from Me in brief what that field is, of what kind it is, what its modifications are, whence it arises, and who he is, and what his powers are. This has been sung in many ways by seers, separately in various Vedic meters, and also in the reasoned and decisive statements of the Brahma-sūtras. The great elements, ego-sense [ahaṅkāra], intellect [buddhi], the unmanifest, the ten senses and one (mind), and the five sense-objects—

### Pratibha Commentary
Krishna refuses to let “body” remain a blunt noun. The field is a stratified inventory: elements, ego-making, intellect, unmanifest root, senses, mind, and objects. The contested move is psychological materialism without reductionism—experience is analyzable into constituents, yet analysis serves awakening, not dissection for its own sake. By citing ṛṣis and Brahma-sūtra reasoning, the Gītā claims continuity with both poetic revelation and discursive philosophy. Ahaṅkāra sits near the center of the list because the field includes the very mechanism that says “mine.” Existentially, this catalogue is a mirror: when rage arises, can you find element-tone, ego-clench, buddhi’s story, and sense-contact as field-events rather than as “who I am”? The verse trains a contemplative anatomy.  Catalogues like this are contemplative instruments; they train you to find ego-making and sense-range inside what you still call “just me.” Read as existential demand, the verse asks for a changed stance in the next difficult hour—not for ornamental agreement with a doctrine.

### Key Terms
**vikāra (विकार)** — “modification / alteration” -> the field’s changing modes -> “symptoms” medicalizes what is cosmological-psychological.
**ahaṅkāra (अहङ्कार)** — “I-making” -> ego-function as field-constituent -> “ego” as personality trait misses its structural role in Sāṃkhya.
**buddhi (बुद्धि)** — “awakening / discernment” -> intellect as determining faculty -> “mind” collapses buddhi into manas.
**avyakta (अव्यक्त)** — “unmanifest” -> prakṛti’s root latency -> “invisible” is too vague for primordial nature.
**indriyagocara (इन्द्रियगोचर)** — “range of the senses” -> sense-objects as field-content -> “sense data” strips the lived worldliness of gocara.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Sāṃkhya Kārikā 22–38]:** The tattva enumeration is the structural cousin of this field inventory.
*Divergence:* Classical Sāṃkhya keeps puruṣa plural and inactive; the Gītā will theistically recenter the supreme person.
**[Abhidharma skandha analysis]:** Both dissolve the apparent self into process-heaps available to insight.
*Divergence:* Abhidharma aims at not-self; Krishna analyzes the field to isolate a knower and later a Lord of knowers.
**[Aristotle, De Anima II]:** Faculties and objects of sense form a parallel map of living embodiment.
*Divergence:* Aristotle seeks natural philosophy of soul; the Gītā’s inventory is soteriological, aimed at release.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
When a strong emotion arises, name five layers aloud: body-tone, sense-contact, thought, “I/mine,” and the knowing of all four. Stop at naming.

## Desire, Aversion, and the Virtues That Are Knowledge
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 13.7–13.9

Desire, aversion, pleasure, pain, the aggregate, consciousness, and steadfastness—this, in summary, is the field with its modifications. Absence of pride, absence of pretension, non-harming [ahiṃsā], forbearance, uprightness, service of the teacher, purity, steadiness, self-restraint; dispassion toward sense objects, and also absence of egoism, and seeing clearly the defect of pain in birth, death, old age, and disease—

---

### Devanagari
इच्छा द्वेषः सुखं दुःखं सङ्घातश्चेतनाधृतिः।एतत्क्षेत्रं समासेन सविकारमुदाहृतम्
अमानित्वमदम्भित्वमहिंसा क्षान्तिरार्जवम्।आचार्योपासनं शौचं स्थैर्यमात्मविनिग्रहः
इन्द्रियार्थेषु वैराग्यमनहङ्कार एव च।जन्ममृत्युजराव्याधिदुःखदोषानुदर्शनम्

### IAST
icchā dveṣaḥ sukhaṃ duḥkhaṃ saṅghātaścetanādhṛtiḥ|etatkṣetraṃ samāsena savikāramudāhṛtam
amānitvamadambhitvamahiṃsā kṣāntirārjavam|ācāryopāsanaṃ śaucaṃ sthairyamātmavinigrahaḥ
indriyārtheṣu vairāgyamanahaṅkāra eva ca|janmamṛtyujarāvyādhiduḥkhadoṣānudarśanam

### Pratibha Translation
Desire, aversion, pleasure, pain, the aggregate, consciousness, and steadfastness—this, in summary, is the field with its modifications. Absence of pride, absence of pretension, non-harming [ahiṃsā], forbearance, uprightness, service of the teacher, purity, steadiness, self-restraint; dispassion toward sense objects, and also absence of egoism, and seeing clearly the defect of pain in birth, death, old age, and disease—

### Pratibha Commentary
The field is not only flesh and sense; it includes the affective weather of desire and aversion, pleasure and pain, and even cetanā and dhṛti as lived functions. Then the chapter pivots: “knowledge” will mean a set of virtues. The contested move is to treat ethics as epistemology. Humility, non-harm, teacher-service, and clear seeing of aging and death are not moral add-ons; they are the conditions under which the field becomes visible as field. Without them, intellect merely rearranges bondage. In the Gītā’s yoga, jñāna is a temperament of clarity. Existentially, this refuses the fantasy that one can keep contempt, craving, and denial while “understanding non-duality.” The defect-seeing (doṣānudarśana) of birth-death-age-disease is not morbidity; it is anti-illusion training that softens clutch.  If your metaphysics leaves pride, cruelty, and denial untouched, the Gītā will not grant it the name jñāna—however subtle the concepts. Read as existential demand, the verse asks for a changed stance in the next difficult hour—not for ornamental agreement with a doctrine.

### Key Terms
**icchā-dveṣa (इच्छा-द्वेष)** — desire-aversion pair -> bipolar binding of the field -> “likes and dislikes” understates their ontological grip.
**saṅghāta (सङ्घात)** — “aggregate / assemblage” -> the body as composite heap -> “organism” can smuggle false unity.
**amānitva (अमानित्व)** — non-pride -> humility as knowledge-condition -> “modesty” can become social performance.
**ahiṃsā (अहिंसा)** — non-harming -> refraining from injury as clarity’s ethics -> “nonviolence” as politics alone misses contemplative force.
**doṣānudarśana (दोषानुदर्शन)** — seeing-along-the-defect -> sustained contemplation of suffering’s fault-line in embodiment -> “pessimism” misreads the liberative intent.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Patañjali, Yoga Sūtra II.30–32]:** Yama-niyama virtues as foundation of yoga parallel knowledge-as-virtue here.
*Divergence:* Patañjali systematizes restraints for aṣṭāṅga; Krishna embeds them inside the definition of jñāna itself.
**[Buddha, Dhammacakkappavattana / aging-illness-death motif]:** Clear seeing of birth, aging, sickness, death as motivators of awakening.
*Divergence:* Early Buddhism aims at cessation of craving; the Gītā aims at field-knower discrimination within theistic yoga.
**[Evagrius Ponticus, Praktikos]:** Apatheia and humility as conditions of true knowledge resonate strongly.
*Divergence:* Evagrius orients toward pure prayer; Krishna orients virtues toward knowing kṣetra and kṣetrajña.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Choose one virtue from the list (forbearance, uprightness, or non-pride). Practice it in the next difficult conversation as if it were a form of seeing, not self-improvement theater.

## Unswerving Devotion and Love of Solitude
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 13.10–13.11

Non-attachment, absence of clinging to son, wife, home, and the like; and constant evenness of mind toward the occurrence of the desired and the undesired; and unswerving devotion to Me with yoga that has no other object; resorting to solitary places, and distaste for crowds of people—

---

### Devanagari
असक्ितरनभिष्वङ्गः पुत्रदारगृहादिषु।नित्यं च समचित्तत्वमिष्टानिष्टोपपत्तिषु
मयि चानन्ययोगेन भक्ितरव्यभिचारिणी।विविक्तदेशसेवित्वमरतिर्जनसंसदि

### IAST
asakitaranabhiṣvaṅgaḥ putradāragṛhādiṣu|nityaṃ ca samacittatvamiṣṭāniṣṭopapattiṣu
mayi cānanyayogena bhakitaravyabhicāriṇī|viviktadeśasevitvamaratirjanasaṃsadi

### Pratibha Translation
Non-attachment, absence of clinging to son, wife, home, and the like; and constant evenness of mind toward the occurrence of the desired and the undesired; and unswerving devotion to Me with yoga that has no other object; resorting to solitary places, and distaste for crowds of people—

### Pratibha Commentary
Knowledge continues to look like a way of loving and dwelling. Asakti and anabhiṣvaṅga do not abolish relationship; they cut the adhesive that turns people into private property of the ego. Samacittatva under desired and undesired outcomes is the emotional form of field-seeing: events arise in the field; the knower need not lurch. Then comes the chapter’s theistic spine: bhakti that does not stray, joined to ananya-yoga—yoga with no rival object. Solitude and distaste for crowds protect that single-pointedness from diffusion. The contested move is to place devotion inside jñāna’s definition, refusing the split between cold metaphysics and warm piety. Existentially, the verse asks whether your “understanding” survives family pressure and social noise, and whether your devotion has a second altar hidden in achievement or approval.  Solitude here is not misanthropy; it is protection of ananya-yoga from the market of identities that crowds sell and buy. Read as existential demand, the verse asks for a changed stance in the next difficult hour—not for ornamental agreement with a doctrine.

### Key Terms
**asakti (असक्ति)** — non-attachment -> freedom from adhesive clinging -> “detachment” can sound cold or dissociative.
**anabhiṣvaṅga (अनभिष्वङ्ग)** — non-adhesion / non-entanglement -> specifically toward household bonds -> “not caring” misses lucid love without glue.
**samacittatva (समचित्तत्व)** — same-mindedness -> evenness amid liked/disliked arrivals -> “indifference” misreads as apathy.
**ananya-yoga (अनन्ययोग)** — yoga with no other -> exclusive yogic orientation to Krishna -> “monotheistic loyalty” alone misses yogic concentration.
**avyabhicāriṇī bhakti (अव्यभिचारिणी भक्ति)** — unswerving devotion -> fidelity of love that does not stray -> “faithfulness” can sound merely moralistic.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Gītā 12.13–20]:** The devotee’s traits echo this knowledge-list, binding bhakti and jñāna.
*Divergence:* Chapter 12 ranks paths; chapter 13 absorbs devotion into the very definition of knowledge.
**[John of the Cross, Ascent of Mount Carmel I]:** Detachment from creatures for God’s sake parallels asakti and ananya devotion.
*Divergence:* John emphasizes dark nights of sense and spirit; Krishna keeps communal duty alive inside non-clinging.
**[Milarepa’s songs of solitude]:** Love of isolated places as support for realization resonates with vivikta-deśa.
*Divergence:* Milarepa’s solitude serves mahāmudrā recognition; here solitude serves unswerving bhakti-knowledge of the field’s Lord.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Spend twenty minutes in a quiet place with no phone. Each time a household worry arises, note “clinging,” return to the breath, and silently offer the worry to the One without a second object.

## This Alone Is Called Knowledge
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 13.12

Constancy in knowledge of the Self, and seeing the goal of knowledge of reality—this is declared to be knowledge. What is other than this is ignorance.

---

### Devanagari
अध्यात्मज्ञाननित्यत्वं तत्त्वज्ञानार्थदर्शनम्।एतज्ज्ञानमिति प्रोक्तमज्ञानं यदतोन्यथा

### IAST
adhyātmajñānanityatvaṃ tattvajñānārthadarśanam|etajjñānamiti proktamajñānaṃ yadatonyathā

### Pratibha Translation
Constancy in knowledge of the Self, and seeing the goal of knowledge of reality—this is declared to be knowledge. What is other than this is ignorance.

### Pratibha Commentary
After listing virtues, Krishna seals the definition: knowledge is constancy in adhyātma-jñāna and vision oriented to the aim of tattva-jñāna. The contested move is severity. Cleverness, information, and even spiritual experiences that do not stabilize Self-knowledge and reality-vision count as ajñāna. “Nityatva” matters: not a peak insight, but ongoingness. “Artha-darśana” matters: seeing must be teleological, aimed at what knowledge is for—release into reality, not ornamentation of the ego. ## Who Sees, Truly Sees
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 13.28–13.30

The one who sees the supreme Lord standing equally in all beings, the undying in the dying—he truly sees. Seeing the Lord established equally everywhere, one does not injure the Self by the self; then one goes to the highest goal. The one who sees that actions are done entirely by nature alone, and thus sees the Self as non-doer—he truly sees.

---

### Devanagari
समं सर्वेषु भूतेषु तिष्ठन्तं परमेश्वरम्।विनश्यत्स्वविनश्यन्तं यः पश्यति स पश्यति
समं पश्यन्हि सर्वत्र समवस्थितमीश्वरम्।न हिनस्त्यात्मनाऽऽत्मानं ततो याति परां गतिम्
प्रकृत्यैव च कर्माणि क्रियमाणानि सर्वशः।यः पश्यति तथाऽऽत्मानमकर्तारं स पश्यति

### IAST
samaṃ sarveṣu bhūteṣu tiṣṭhantaṃ parameśvaram|vinaśyatsvavinaśyantaṃ yaḥ paśyati sa paśyati
samaṃ paśyanhi sarvatra samavasthitamīśvaram|na hinastyātmanā''tmānaṃ tato yāti parāṃ gatim
prakṛtyaiva ca karmāṇi kriyamāṇāni sarvaśaḥ|yaḥ paśyati tathā''tmānamakartāraṃ sa paśyati

### Pratibha Translation
The one who sees the supreme Lord standing equally in all beings, the undying in the dying—he truly sees. Seeing the Lord established equally everywhere, one does not injure the Self by the self; then one goes to the highest goal. The one who sees that actions are done entirely by nature alone, and thus sees the Self as non-doer—he truly sees.

### Pratibha Commentary
“He who sees, sees” (sa paśyati) is the chapter’s refrain of authentic vision. Equal standing of Parameśvara in all beings—and the undying within the dying—redefines perception itself. From this seeing comes ethics: one does not strike the Self with the self. Violence is revealed as metaphysical suicide, not merely interpersonal harm. Then the non-doer teaching: actions are wrought wholly by prakṛti; the ātman is akartā. The contested move is to unite theistic equality-vision with Sāṃkhya non-doership without letting either cancel the other. Existentially, true seeing ends both cruelty and the anxious claim “I am the sole author of this life.”  Equal seeing is therefore the end of spiritual exceptionalism: if the Lord stands equally, your private drama of superiority loses its altar. Read as existential demand, the verse asks for a changed stance in the next difficult hour—not for ornamental agreement with a doctrine. Read as existential demand, the verse asks for a changed stance in the next difficult hour—not for ornamental agreement with a doctrine.

### Key Terms
**sama (सम)** — equal / even -> Lord’s equal presence in all beings -> “fairness” moralizes what is ontological.
**Parameśvara (परमेश्वर)** — supreme Lord -> the One standing in all fields -> “God” without equal immanence misses the ethical hinge.
**na hinasti (न हिनस्ति)** — does not injure -> non-injury as consequence of equal seeing -> “becomes nice” misses Self-on-Self stakes.
**akartā (अकर्ता)** — non-doer -> Self as not the agent of prakṛti’s actions -> “passive” wrongly implies limpness.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Īśā Upaniṣad 6–7]:** Seeing all beings in the Self and the Self in all ends grief and delusion—close ethical homology.
*Divergence:* Īśā is compact and ritual-renunciant; the Gītā explicitly ties equal seeing to non-doership of prakṛti’s actions.
**[Spinoza, Ethics IV–V]:** Seeing things under a species of eternity reduces harm born of partial passion.
*Divergence:* Spinoza’s God is substance known geometrically; Krishna’s Lord is personally addressed and equally stationed in beings.
**[Śāntideva, BCA VIII (exchange of self and other)]:** Equalizing vision undermines harm.
*Divergence:* Śāntideva uses imaginative exchange and emptiness; Krishna uses Lord-equality and prakṛti-agency.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Before speaking critically of someone, pause and see the undying in the dying field before you. Speak only after that seeing is even briefly available.

## Space Untouched, Self Untouched
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 13.31–13.33

When one sees the separate being of beings as abiding in the One, and from that alone their expansion, then one becomes Brahman. Because it is beginningless and without guṇas, this supreme Self is imperishable. Though dwelling in the body, O son of Kuntī, it neither acts nor is stained. As the all-pervading space is not stained because of its subtlety, so the Self, seated everywhere in the body, is not stained.

---

### Devanagari
यदा भूतपृथग्भावमेकस्थमनुपश्यति।तत एव च विस्तारं ब्रह्म सम्पद्यते तदा
अनादित्वान्निर्गुणत्वात्परमात्मायमव्ययः।शरीरस्थोऽपि कौन्तेय न करोति न लिप्यते
यथा सर्वगतं सौक्ष्म्यादाकाशं नोपलिप्यते।

सर्वत्रावस्थितो देहे तथाऽऽत्मा नोपलिप्यते

### IAST
yadā bhūtapṛthagbhāvamekasthamanupaśyati|tata eva ca vistāraṃ brahma sampadyate tadā
anāditvānnirguṇatvātparamātmāyamavyayaḥ|śarīrastho'pi kaunteya na karoti na lipyate
yathā sarvagataṃ saukṣmyādākāśaṃ nopalipyate|

sarvatrāvasthito dehe tathā''tmā nopalipyate

### Pratibha Translation
When one sees the separate being of beings as abiding in the One, and from that alone their expansion, then one becomes Brahman. Because it is beginningless and without guṇas, this supreme Self is imperishable. Though dwelling in the body, O son of Kuntī, it neither acts nor is stained. As the all-pervading space is not stained because of its subtlety, so the Self, seated everywhere in the body, is not stained.

### Pratibha Commentary
The climax of discrimination is unitive: separate beings are seen as established in the One, and their expansion as from that One alone—then one becomes Brahman. This is not metaphor; it is the fruit of seeing. The Paramātman, beginningless and nirguṇa, neither acts nor is stained though body-dwelling. The ākāśa analogy is exact: space pervades and is intimate with all forms yet unsoiled by them because of subtlety. The contested move is embodied non-staining: spirituality that needs to flee the body misunderstands subtlety. Existentially, guilt, praise, and defilement narratives can be felt as weather in space, not as tattoos on the Self.  The space analogy also disciplines guilt-spirituality: stains are real in the field, yet the Self’s subtlety is not improved by self-hate. Read as existential demand, the verse asks for a changed stance in the next difficult hour—not for ornamental agreement with a doctrine. Read as existential demand, the verse asks for a changed stance in the next difficult hour—not for ornamental agreement with a doctrine.

### Key Terms
**eka-stha (एकस्थ)** — abiding in the One -> multiplicity rooted in unity -> “oneness” as slogan misses located establishment.
**brahma sampadyate (ब्रह्म सम्पद्यते)** — becomes / attains Brahman -> realization as ontological fitting -> “goes to Brahman” can postpone what is present fruit.
**na lipyate (न लिप्यते)** — is not smeared / stained -> non-contamination amid embodiment -> “unaffected” can sound dissociative.
**ākāśa (आकाश)** — space / ether -> analogy for pervading unstained presence -> “sky” visualizes wrongly as overhead only.
**saukṣmya (सौक्ष्म्य)** — subtlety -> the reason space and Self remain unsoiled -> “thinness” is too physical.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Chāndogya Upaniṣad III.14 / VI]:** Becoming what one knows (Brahman) resonates with brahma sampadyate.
*Divergence:* Chāndogya often uses gradual teaching and metaphors of clay/gold; here the hinge is seeing multiplicity in the One.
**[Zhuangzi, “Fasting of the Mind”]:** Emptiness that receives the world without being soiled parallels ākāśa-like Self.
*Divergence:* Zhuangzi distrusts fixed metaphysical selves; the Gītā affirms Paramātman while using space as image of non-staining.
**[Meister Eckhart, on the ground of the soul]:** The soul’s ground remains virgin amid images.
*Divergence:* Eckhart pushes toward God beyond God; Krishna keeps the teaching inside body-dwelling non-action and non-stain.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Imagine awareness as space in the room of the body. Let sounds, thoughts, and sensations arise like birds through space. Note: space is not stained. Rest there for five minutes.

## The Sun of the Field, the Eye of Knowledge
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 13.34–13.35

As the one sun illumines this whole world, so the Lord of the field illumines the whole field, O Bhārata. Those who know, with the eye of knowledge, this distinction between field and knower of the field, and the liberation of beings from nature—they go to the Supreme.

---

### Devanagari
यथा प्रकाशयत्येकः कृत्स्नं लोकमिमं रविः।

क्षेत्रं क्षेत्री तथा कृत्स्नं प्रकाशयति भारत
क्षेत्रक्षेत्रज्ञयोरेवमन्तरं ज्ञानचक्षुषा।

भूतप्रकृतिमोक्षं च ये विदुर्यान्ति ते परम्

### IAST
yathā prakāśayatyekaḥ kṛtsnaṃ lokamimaṃ raviḥ|

kṣetraṃ kṣetrī tathā kṛtsnaṃ prakāśayati bhārata
kṣetrakṣetrajñayorevamantaraṃ jñānacakṣuṣā|

bhūtaprakṛtimokṣaṃ ca ye viduryānti te param

### Pratibha Translation
As the one sun illumines this whole world, so the Lord of the field illumines the whole field, O Bhārata. Those who know, with the eye of knowledge, this distinction between field and knower of the field, and the liberation of beings from nature—they go to the Supreme.

### Pratibha Commentary
The chapter closes with light. One sun, whole world; one kṣetrī, whole field. Illumination is not an extra act layered onto matter; it is the knower’s very presence as light. The eye of knowledge (jñāna-cakṣus) sees the antara—the difference—between field and knower, and also bhūta-prakṛti-mokṣa: liberation of beings from nature. The contested final claim is that discrimination and release belong together. Seeing the difference is already the beginning of freedom from prakṛti’s claim. Existentially, you do not wait to leave the world to be free of nature’s bondage; you learn to see the sun that was lighting the field all along, and in that seeing, go to the Supreme.  Going to the Supreme is thus not a travel plan after discrimination; discrimination itself is already sunlight learning to notice the sun. Read as existential demand, the verse asks for a changed stance in the next difficult hour—not for ornamental agreement with a doctrine.

### Key Terms
**kṣetrī (क्षेत्री)** — lord/owner of the field -> the illumining knower -> “soul” misses the solar metaphor’s public radiance.
**prakāśayati (प्रकाशयति)** — illumines -> knowing as lighting-up of the field -> “reveals” can sound merely informational.
**jñāna-cakṣus (ज्ञानचक्षुस्)** — eye of knowledge -> discriminative vision as organ of liberation -> “insight” can stay psychological.
**antara (अन्तर)** — distinction / interval / difference -> the liberating cut between field and knower -> “inner” mistranslates the philosophical sense here.
**prakṛti-mokṣa (प्रकृतिमोक्ष)** — liberation from nature -> release of beings from prakṛti’s binding -> “escape from matter” can sound dualistically crude.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**[Plato, Republic VI–VII (sun and cave)]:** The sun as analogue of the Good that lights knowables is structural kin.
*Divergence:* Plato’s sun serves dialectic ascent to forms; Krishna’s sun-knower illumines the field for discriminative release into the Supreme.
**[Kaṭha Upaniṣad II.2.15 / Śvetāśvatara VI.14]:** When That shines, all shines after it—light-of-lights homology.
*Divergence:* Those Upaniṣads climax in metaphysical radiance; the Gītā ties the solar image explicitly to kṣetra/kṣetrajña difference.
**[Dzogchen “self-knowing awareness” as lamp]:** Awareness lighting appearance without being identical to appearance.
*Divergence:* Dzogchen emphasizes primordial purity beyond effort; the Gītā still foregrounds the eye of knowledge and going to the Supreme.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
At sunset or beside a lamp, contemplate: “As this light shows the room, awareness shows the field.” Rest in the lighting, not in the furniture of experience.

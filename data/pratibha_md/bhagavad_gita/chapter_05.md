## Renouncer Always, Even While Acting
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 5.1–5.3

Arjuna said: You praise renunciation of actions, Kṛṣṇa, and again yoga; tell me for certain which one of these is better. The Blessed Lord said: Renunciation and karma-yoga both bring the highest good; but of the two, karma-yoga is better than renunciation of action. Know that one as the perpetual renouncer who neither hates nor desires; free from the dualities, O mighty-armed, that one is easily released from bondage.

---

### Devanagari

अर्जुन उवाच

संन्यासं कर्मणां कृष्ण पुनर्योगं च शंससि।

यच्छ्रेय एतयोरेकं तन्मे ब्रूहि सुनिश्िचतम्

श्री भगवानुवाच

संन्यासः कर्मयोगश्च निःश्रेयसकरावुभौ।

तयोस्तु कर्मसंन्यासात्कर्मयोगो विशिष्यते

ज्ञेयः स नित्यसंन्यासी यो न द्वेष्टि न काङ्क्षति।

निर्द्वन्द्वो हि महाबाहो सुखं बन्धात्प्रमुच्यते

### IAST

arjuna uvāca

saṃnyāsaṃ karmaṇāṃ kṛṣṇa punaryogaṃ ca śaṃsasi|

yacchreya etayorekaṃ tanme brūhi suniśicatam

śrī bhagavānuvāca

saṃnyāsaḥ karmayogaśca niḥśreyasakarāvubhau|

tayostu karmasaṃnyāsātkarmayogo viśiṣyate

jñeyaḥ sa nityasaṃnyāsī yo na dveṣṭi na kāṅkṣati|

nirdvandvo hi mahābāho sukhaṃ bandhātpramucyate

### Pratibha Translation

Arjuna said: You praise renunciation of actions, Kṛṣṇa, and again you praise yoga; tell me for certain which one of these two is the better good. The Blessed Lord said: Renunciation and the yoga of action both effect the highest good; but of the two, karma-yoga excels over renunciation of action. Know that one as the perpetual renouncer [nityasaṃnyāsī] who neither hates nor desires; free from the dualities, O mighty-armed, that one is easily released from bondage.

### Pratibha Commentary

Arjuna again demands a forced ranking; Krishna grants both as niḥśreyasa-producing, then ranks karma-yoga above karmasaṃnyāsa for the operable path. The contested redefinition follows: the true perpetual renouncer is not the one who has dropped deeds but the one who has dropped hatred and desire. Nirdvandva — free of the dualities — becomes the interior criterion of saṃnyāsa. In the Gītā’s ongoing correction of quietism, this prevents equating holiness with occupational exit. Existentially, check your renunciation: have you left a role, or have you left dveṣa and kāṅkṣā? Easy release from bondage attaches to the second. Outer quitting without affective renunciation is a costume; inner non-hatred/non-craving while acting is the standing Krishna calls nityasaṃnyāsa. Both paths produce the highest good, so the ranking is pastoral rather than absolute metaphysics: for those still in the field of deeds, karma-yoga excels over occupational exit. Redefining the perpetual renouncer as non-hatred and non-desire prevents costume holiness. Duality-freedom makes release “easy” because the pairs that generate craving and aversion have lost sovereignty. Check your quitting: did you leave a job, or did you leave the engines of bondage? Only the second is nityasaṃnyāsa.

### Key Terms

**nityasaṃnyāsī (नित्यसंन्यासी)** — perpetual renouncer -> defined by non-hatred and non-desire, not by non-work -> redefines renunciation as affective structure

**niḥśreyasa (निःश्रेयस)** — highest good/ultimate beatitude -> fruit shared by both paths -> blocks total dismissal of either

**nirdvandva (निर्द्वन्द्व)** — free from dualities -> the condition of easy release -> names freedom from the pairs that generate hate and craving

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Stoic apatheia vs withdrawal:** Structural homology in locating freedom in affective independence rather than change of address.
*Divergence:* Stoicism centers prohairesis under logos; the Gītā names nityasaṃnyāsa and ranks karma-yoga for the path still in deeds.

**Buddhist anagārika / lay arahat debates:** Structural homology in questioning whether going-forth alone equals release.
*Divergence:* Buddhist frameworks stress monastic vinaya and path-factors; here the decisive mark is nirdvandva non-hate/non-desire.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Keep your current responsibilities for a day, and practice renunciation only as non-hatred and non-craving toward outcomes — then notice whether bondage loosens without a costume change.

## Children Split What Knowers Unify
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 5.4–5.6

Fools say Sāṃkhya and Yoga are different, not the wise; established rightly in even one, one gains the fruit of both. The standing reached by the Sāṃkhyas is also reached by the Yogas; who sees Sāṃkhya and Yoga as one — that one sees. But renunciation, O mighty-armed, is hard to attain without yoga; the sage yoked in yoga reaches Brahman before long.

---

### Devanagari

सांख्ययोगौ पृथग्बालाः प्रवदन्ति न पण्डिताः।

एकमप्यास्थितः सम्यगुभयोर्विन्दते फलम्

यत्सांख्यैः प्राप्यते स्थानं तद्योगैरपि गम्यते।

एकं सांख्यं च योगं च यः पश्यति स पश्यति

संन्यासस्तु महाबाहो दुःखमाप्तुमयोगतः।

योगयुक्तो मुनिर्ब्रह्म नचिरेणाधिगच्छति

### IAST

sāṃkhyayogau pṛthagbālāḥ pravadanti na paṇḍitāḥ|

ekamapyāsthitaḥ samyagubhayorvindate phalam

yatsāṃkhyaiḥ prāpyate sthānaṃ tadyogairapi gamyate|

ekaṃ sāṃkhyaṃ ca yogaṃ ca yaḥ paśyati sa paśyati

saṃnyāsastu mahābāho duḥkhamāptumayogataḥ|

yogayukto munirbrahma nacireṇādhigacchati

### Pratibha Translation

Children say Sāṃkhya and Yoga are separate, not the wise; established properly in even one, one finds the fruit of both. The place reached by the Sāṃkhyas is also gone to by the Yogas; who sees Sāṃkhya and Yoga as one — that one sees. But renunciation, O mighty-armed, is hard to attain without yoga; the sage yoked to yoga reaches Brahman before long.

### Pratibha Commentary

Sectarian splitting of Sāṃkhya and Yoga is called childish. The contested seeing is identity of destination: the same sthāna is reached by both when rightly established, and only the one who sees them as one truly sees. Then a practical warning: saṃnyāsa without yoga is duḥkha to attain; the yoga-yukta muni reaches Brahman swiftly. In the Gītā’s synthesis, contemplative discrimination and engaged yoga are not rival brands but convergent means. Existentially, stop collecting identities (knower vs doer) as status. If your renunciation lacks yogic yoking, expect suffering, not arrival. Vision that unifies the two is itself a mark of maturity. Childish splitting of Sāṃkhya and Yoga protects identity brands at the cost of seeing. Right establishment in either yields both fruits because the destination is one standing. Renunciation without yoga is painful precisely because unyoked quitting leaves desire intact while removing structure. The sage yoked in yoga reaches Brahman swiftly because means and end are already aligned. Mature vision unifies what immature speech divides.

### Key Terms

**sāṃkhya (सांख्य)** — enumeration/discriminative school -> here the knowledge-orientation converging with yoga -> not merely classical dualist system in this verse’s pedagogical use

**sthāna (स्थान)** — standing/place -> shared destination of both approaches -> grounds anti-sectarian claim

**ayogataḥ (अयोगतः)** — without yoga -> condition that makes renunciation hard and painful -> warns against unyoked quitting

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Augustine on Mary and Martha:** Structural homology in refusing final rivalry between contemplative and active lives when ordered to one end.
*Divergence:* Christian ordering is love of God in Christ; here Sāṃkhya-Yoga identity is pedagogical toward Brahman via yoga-yoking.

**Zhu Xi’s unity of knowledge and action:** Structural homology in attacking splits that treat knowing and doing as separable completions.
*Divergence:* Neo-Confucian unity is moral-li cultivation; the Gītā frames unity as shared sthāna and warns saṃnyāsa without yoga.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

If you favor “knowing” over “doing” (or the reverse), practice the neglected pole for one hour as the same path’s other foot, aiming at one destination.

## Sense-Objects Move; the Knower Does Not Claim
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 5.7–5.9

Yoked in yoga, self purified, self conquered, senses conquered, the self become the self of all beings — even while acting, is not stained. “I do nothing at all” — so the yoked knower of truth should think, seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, walking, sleeping, breathing, speaking, releasing, grasping, opening and closing the eyes — holding that the senses move among the sense-objects.

---

### Devanagari

योगयुक्तो विशुद्धात्मा विजितात्मा जितेन्द्रियः।

सर्वभूतात्मभूतात्मा कुर्वन्नपि न लिप्यते

नैव किंचित्करोमीति युक्तो मन्येत तत्त्ववित्।

पश्यन् श्रृणवन्स्पृशञ्जिघ्रन्नश्नन्गच्छन्स्वपन् श्वसन्

प्रलपन्विसृजन्गृह्णन्नुन्मिषन्निमिषन्नपि।

इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेषु वर्तन्त इति धारयन्

### IAST

yogayukto viśuddhātmā vijitātmā jitendriyaḥ|

sarvabhūtātmabhūtātmā kurvannapi na lipyate

naiva kiṃcitkaromīti yukto manyeta tattvavit|

paśyan śrṛṇavanspṛśañjighrannaśnangacchansvapan śvasan

pralapanvisṛjangṛhṇannunmiṣannimiṣannapi|

indriyāṇīndriyārtheṣu vartanta iti dhārayan

### Pratibha Translation

Yoked in yoga, purified in self, self-conquered, senses conquered, whose self has become the self of all beings — even while acting, is not stained. “I do nothing at all” — so the integrated knower of truth should think, while seeing, hearing, touching, smelling, eating, going, sleeping, breathing, speaking, releasing, grasping, opening and closing the eyes — holding: the senses are moving among the sense-objects.

### Pratibha Commentary

Non-staining while acting requires a full inner profile: yoga-yoked, purified, self-mastered, sense-mastered, and identified as the self of all beings. The contested mantra — naiva kiṃcit karomi — is not denial of biology but refusal of ahaṅkāra’s authorship during the entire sensorimotor stream. The long participle chain makes the claim concrete: blinking itself is included. Holding “senses among sense-objects” is the cognitive seal of tattva-vit practice. Existentially, try the mantra only where purification and non-harm are real; otherwise it becomes spiritualized irresponsibility. Where the profile holds, the world can move through you without leaving stain-claims on the “I.” The long list of participles — including blinking — makes non-doership concrete enough to survive ordinary life. “I do nothing at all” while senses move among objects is a held insight (dhārayan), not a one-time idea. It requires the prior profile of purification and mastery; otherwise it becomes spiritualized negligence. Where the profile holds, action can flood the day without staining the claim-making “I.” Practice the mantra inside care, not instead of care.

### Key Terms

**sarvabhūtātmabhūtātman (सर्वभूतात्मभूतात्मन्)** — self that has become the self of all beings -> identity expanded beyond private doer -> the compassion-structure of non-staining action

**dhārayan (धारयन्)** — holding/bearing in mind -> active maintenance of the senses-objects insight -> not a one-time idea

**na lipyate (न लिप्यते)** — is not stained -> non-adhesion of action’s binding -> the fruit of the yoked profile

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Zhuāngzi’s Cook Ding:** Structural homology in activity without egoic forcing, as if process moves itself.
*Divergence:* Cook Ding’s wuwei is skill-spontaneity; the Gītā’s formula is tattva-holding amid a conquered-sense yoga profile.

**Nietzschean critique of doer-fiction (GM I) inverted:** Structural homology in prying the “doer” off the deed.
*Divergence:* Nietzsche historicizes the fiction for genealogy; the Gītā uses the insight for yogic non-staining and liberation.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

During a routine sequence (walking, eating, speaking), repeat quietly “senses among objects; I do nothing at all,” while keeping care and ethics intact.

## Lotus-Leaf Action on Brahman
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 5.10

Who places actions on Brahman, abandoning attachment, and acts — that one is not stained by evil, as a lotus leaf by water.

---

### Devanagari

ब्रह्मण्याधाय कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा करोति यः।

लिप्यते न स पापेन पद्मपत्रमिवाम्भसा

### IAST

brahmaṇyādhāya karmāṇi saṅgaṃ tyaktvā karoti yaḥ|

lipyate na sa pāpena padmapatramivāmbhasā

### Pratibha Translation

Who places actions upon Brahman, having abandoned attachment, and acts — that one is not stained by evil, as a lotus leaf by waters.

### Pratibha Commentary

The method is twofold and simultaneous: ādhāya karmāṇi brahmaṇi — depositing actions on Brahman — and saṅgaṃ tyaktvā — abandoning attachment — while still acting. The contested image is the lotus leaf: in water, not wet. Engagement without absorption of stain is possible when the metaphysical deposit and affective release coincide. In Vedānta-inflected karma-yoga, Brahman is not an excuse to dump consequences onto the Absolute while clinging privately; the tyāga of saṅga is the seal. Existentially, act inside the wet world without becoming soggy with pāpa-claim. The leaf’s secret is structure, not distance from the pond. Depositing actions on Brahman without abandoning attachment is unfinished consecration; abandoning attachment without the deposit can become dry indifference. The lotus image needs both: contact with the wet world and non-wetting structure. Pāpa-stain is adhesion, not mere proximity to difficulty. Act inside messy necessity with the twin moves until the leaf’s logic becomes your habit. The pond is not the enemy; soaking is.

### Key Terms

**brahmaṇy ādhāya (ब्रह्मण्याधाय)** — having placed upon Brahman -> consecrating deposit of deeds -> more than mood; metaphysical relocation of ownership

**padmapatra (पद्मपत्र)** — lotus leaf -> classic image of non-adhesion amid contact -> purity as non-wetting, not as non-contact

**saṅga (सङ्ग)** — attachment/adhesion -> what must be abandoned for the leaf-effect -> the wetting agent in moral psychology

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Christian “in the world but not of it”:** Structural homology in contact without constitutive capture.
*Divergence:* Christian framing is ecclesial-eschatological in Christ; here the deposit is onto Brahman with explicit saṅga-tyāga.

**Lotus symbolism in Buddhism:** Structural homology in blooming unstained from mud/water.
*Divergence:* Buddhist lotus often figures purity of mind amid saṃsāra; the Gītā ties the image specifically to brahman-deposit of karma.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Before a messy necessary task, mentally place the action on Brahman, release one adhesive hope or fear, then act — and check afterward whether stain-thinking reclaimed the deed.

## Body-Mind Offerings for Self-Purification
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 5.11–5.13

Yogins perform action with body, mind, understanding, and even with the senses alone, abandoning attachment, for self-purification. The integrated one, abandoning the fruit of action, attains steadfast peace; the non-integrated, acting from desire, attached to fruit, is bound. Resigning all actions with the mind, the embodied one sits happily as master in the nine-gated city, neither acting nor causing action.

---

### Devanagari

कायेन मनसा बुद्ध्या केवलैरिन्द्रियैरपि।

योगिनः कर्म कुर्वन्ति सङ्गं त्यक्त्वाऽऽत्मशुद्धये

युक्तः कर्मफलं त्यक्त्वा शान्तिमाप्नोति नैष्ठिकीम्।

अयुक्तः कामकारेण फले सक्तो निबध्यते

सर्वकर्माणि मनसा संन्यस्यास्ते सुखं वशी।

नवद्वारे पुरे देही नैव कुर्वन्न कारयन्

### IAST

kāyena manasā buddhyā kevalairindriyairapi|

yoginaḥ karma kurvanti saṅgaṃ tyaktvā''tmaśuddhaye

yuktaḥ karmaphalaṃ tyaktvā śāntimāpnoti naiṣṭhikīm|

ayuktaḥ kāmakāreṇa phale sakto nibadhyate

sarvakarmāṇi manasā saṃnyasyāste sukhaṃ vaśī|

navadvāre pure dehī naiva kurvanna kārayan

### Pratibha Translation

Yogins perform action by body, mind, understanding, and even by the senses alone, abandoning attachment, for purification of the self. The integrated, abandoning action’s fruit, attains steadfast peace; the non-integrated, by desire’s doing, attached to fruit, is bound. Having renounced all actions with the mind, the embodied one sits happily as master in the nine-gated city, neither acting nor causing to act.

### Pratibha Commentary

Karma-yoga uses every instrument — kāya, manas, buddhi, indriyas — toward ātma-śuddhi, not toward harvest. The contested contrast is stark: yukta gains naiṣṭhikī śānti by fruit-abandonment; ayukta is bound by kāma-driven fruit-attachment. Then the royal image: the dehin sits as vaśī in the nine-gated city, mentally renouncing all actions — neither doing nor causing. This is not corpse-quietism; it is masterful inhabiting of the body-city without authorship fever. Existentially, purification is the right purpose of technique. If peace is unstable, check fruit-clinging. If authorship fever burns, practice mental saṃnyāsa while the gates still open and close in ordinary life. Multi-instrument action for self-purification relocates the purpose of technique away from harvest. Steadfast peace belongs to fruit-abandonment; bondage belongs to desire-driven fruit-attachment — a clean experimental contrast you can test this week. Mental renunciation in the nine-gated city then describes mastery: gates operate, authorship fever does not. Sit as master of the body-city without confusing stillness for corpsehood. Purification, peace, and non-causing are one curriculum.

### Key Terms

**ātmaśuddhi (आत्मशुद्धि)** — purification of the self -> purpose of yogins’ multi-instrument action -> relocates why one acts

**naiṣṭhikī śānti (नैष्ठिकी शान्ति)** — steadfast/final peace -> peace of the yukta who dropped fruit -> not mood calm; established stillness

**navadvāra pura (नवद्वार पुर)** — nine-gated city -> body with nine openings -> classical image for embodied life under inner mastery

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Marcus Aurelius on the body’s citadel:** Structural homology in inhabiting the bodily post without confusing it for the ruling center.
*Divergence:* Stoic hegemonikon ethics differs from Gītā mental saṃnyāsa toward Brahman-peace.

**Theravāda kāyagata sati:** Structural homology in working with body-gates without ownership fever.
*Divergence:* Satipaṭṭhāna aims at insight into tilakkhaṇa; here the nine-gated sitting is framed as mental renunciation and non-causing.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Sit for ten minutes as “master in the nine-gated city”: notice doors of sense opening, renounce authorship mentally, then resume one task solely for purification rather than fruit.

## The Lord Creates Neither Doership nor Fruit-Link
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 5.14–5.16

The Lord creates neither doership nor actions for the world, nor the link of action to fruit; it is nature that operates. The all-pervading does not take anyone’s evil or good deed; knowledge is covered by ignorance — by that beings are deluded. But for those whose ignorance of the Self is destroyed by knowledge, knowledge like the sun illuminates that highest.

---

### Devanagari

न कर्तृत्वं न कर्माणि लोकस्य सृजति प्रभुः।

न कर्मफलसंयोगं स्वभावस्तु प्रवर्तते

नादत्ते कस्यचित्पापं न चैव सुकृतं विभुः।

अज्ञानेनावृतं ज्ञानं तेन मुह्यन्ति जन्तवः

ज्ञानेन तु तदज्ञानं येषां नाशितमात्मनः।

तेषामादित्यवज्ज्ञानं प्रकाशयति तत्परम्

### IAST

na kartṛtvaṃ na karmāṇi lokasya sṛjati prabhuḥ|

na karmaphalasaṃyogaṃ svabhāvastu pravartate

nādatte kasyacitpāpaṃ na caiva sukṛtaṃ vibhuḥ|

ajñānenāvṛtaṃ jñānaṃ tena muhyanti jantavaḥ

jñānena tu tadajñānaṃ yeṣāṃ nāśitamātmanaḥ|

teṣāmādityavajjñānaṃ prakāśayati tatparam

### Pratibha Translation

The Lord does not create doership or actions for the world, nor the conjunction of action with fruit; rather, inherent nature operates. The all-pervading does not take up anyone’s evil or good deed; knowledge is covered by ignorance — by that, beings are deluded. But for those whose ignorance of the Self is destroyed by knowledge, knowledge lights up that highest like the sun.

### Pratibha Commentary

A sharp theological correction: prabhu does not author kartṛtva, karmāṇi, or karma-phala-saṃyoga; svabhāva operates. Vibhu does not collect pāpa or sukṛta. Delusion comes from jñāna covered by ajñāna; when Self-ignorance is destroyed, knowledge sun-lights tat param. The contested claim protects divine non-partiality while assigning process to nature and bondage to ignorance. In Gītā theology this must be held together with earlier theistic activism — a tension productive of depth rather than a simple system. Existentially, stop recruiting God as accountant of your ledger and start destroying the covering that makes the ledger feel like ultimate reality. The sun does not negotiate with night; it reveals what night covered. If the Lord does not create doership, deeds, or fruit-linkage, then recruiting God as cashier of your ledger is already a covering-move of ignorance. Svabhāva operates; vibhu does not take up pāpa or sukṛta as inventory. Delusion is knowledge covered; liberation is ignorance of the Self destroyed until knowledge sun-lights the highest. Stop bargaining with a divine accountant and start destroying the cover. The sun’s work is revelation, not negotiation.

### Key Terms

**kartṛtva (कर्तृत्व)** — doership -> not created by the Lord for the world -> relocates authorship debates into svabhāva and ignorance

**karmaphalasaṃyoga (कर्मफलसंयोग)** — link of action to fruit -> also not Lord’s creation -> challenges crude theistic merit machinery

**svabhāva (स्वभाव)** — own-being/inherent nature -> what actually operates -> the process-field beneath moral theater

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Spinoza’s God without passions or judgments:** Structural homology in an Absolute not taking up human good/evil as a merchant.
*Divergence:* Spinoza’s Deus sive Natura collapses into impersonal geometry; the Gītā still speaks of prabhu/vibhu within a teaching of grace and yoga.

**Madhyamaka / emptiness of agent-action-fruit:** Structural homology in prying apart naive moral metaphysics.
*Divergence:* Madhyamaka empties via dependent designation; here covering-ignorance and solar knowledge of the Self carry the soteriology.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

When you catch yourself making God the cashier of a deed, return to seeing svabhāva operating and ask what ignorance is covering — then seek one clarifying insight rather than one cosmic receipt.

## Minded as That, Gone Beyond Return
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 5.17

Those whose understanding is That, whose self is That, who are established in That, who have That as supreme aim — their stains shaken off by knowledge, they go to non-return.

---

### Devanagari

तद्बुद्धयस्तदात्मानस्तन्निष्ठास्तत्परायणाः।

गच्छन्त्यपुनरावृत्तिं ज्ञाननिर्धूतकल्मषाः

### IAST

tadbuddhayastadātmānastanniṣṭhāstatparāyaṇāḥ|

gacchantyapunarāvṛttiṃ jñānanirdhūtakalmaṣāḥ

### Pratibha Translation

Those whose understanding is That, whose self is That, who are established in That, who take That as highest refuge — their stains shaken off by knowledge, they go to non-return.

### Pratibha Commentary

Liberation’s anthropology is total alignment with tat: buddhi, ātman, niṣṭhā, parāyaṇa — all That. Non-return follows when knowledge has shaken stains off, not when That is admired from a distance. Inventory buddhi, self-sense, standing, and final leaning as one fidelity. Recycling ends for a life already unified. Apunarāvṛtti is the geography of a completed orientation, not a travel upgrade purchased by belief alone.

### Key Terms

**apunarāvṛtti (अपुनरावृत्ति)** — non-return -> freedom from rebirth-recycling -> the destination of total That-alignment

**jñānanirdhūtakalmaṣa (ज्ञाननिर्धूतकल्मष)** — stains shaken off by knowledge -> purification mechanism -> knowledge as winnowing, not ornament

**tatparāyaṇa (तत्परायण)** — having That as supreme aim/refuge -> teleological orientation of the whole person -> completes buddhi/ātman/niṣṭhā

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**John 17 (being in the Father/Son):** Structural homology in total relational alignment as salvific standing.
*Divergence:* Johannine unity is trinitarian-christological; here tat-alignment is Brahman-ward within jñāna purification.

**Plotinus’ return without return to lower:** Structural homology in ascent that ends cyclic descent.
*Divergence:* Plotinian return is hypostatic; Gītā non-return is framed through shaken-off stains and fourfold That-mindedness.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

## Even Here Creation Is Conquered
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 5.19–5.21

Even here creation is conquered by those whose mind is established in equality; for Brahman is faultless and equal, therefore they are established in Brahman. One should not rejoice on obtaining the pleasant nor tremble on obtaining the unpleasant; with firm understanding, unconfused, the Brahman-knower is established in Brahman. Whose self is unattached to outer contacts finds the happiness that is in the Self; that one, yoked in Brahman-yoga, enjoys imperishable happiness.

---

### Devanagari

Even here creation is conquered by those whose mind stands in equality; for Brahman is faultless and equal — therefore they stand in Brahman. ### Pratibha Commentary

Ihaiva — even here — sarga is conquered by sāmya-sthita mind, because Brahman itself is nirdoṣa and sama. The contested claim collapses the postponement of liberation to afterlife geography: establishment in equality is establishment in Brahman now. Emotional discipline follows: no praharṣa at priya, no udvega at apriya. Then the happiness shift: unattached to bāhya-sparśa, one finds ātman-sukha and, through brahma-yoga, akṣaya sukha. Existentially, equality is not blandness; it is ontological fidelity to Brahman’s own sameness. If contact-happiness still rules, creation still conquers you. If Self-happiness is found, even here the revolving show has lost its final claim. Conquering creation even here means the revolving show loses final claim over a mind established in equality. Brahman’s own faultless sameness is the template; exultation and trembling at contacts are refusals of that template. Happiness then shifts inward through non-attachment to outer touches, ripening into undying joy via Brahman-yoga. Postpone-nothing liberation is the verse’s dare. If timed contacts still define your joy, creation still conquers you.

### Key Terms

**sāmya (साम्य)** — equality/evenness -> mind’s standing that conquers creation here -> mirrors Brahman’s own sama nature

**bāhyasparśa (बाह्यस्पर्श)** — outer contact -> sensory-world touching that falsely monopolizes sukha -> the field of non-attachment

**akṣaya sukha (अक्षय सुख)** — undying happiness -> fruit of brahma-yoga yoking -> against perishable contact-pleasures

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Epictetus on preferred indifferents:** Structural homology in refusing exultation/trembling at externals.
*Divergence:* Stoic frame is prohairesis and logos; here equality establishes one in Brahman and yields undying Self-happiness.

**Augustine on restless heart:** Structural homology in redirecting joy from outer contacts to the true ground.
*Divergence:* Augustine’s rest is in God through Christ; the Gītā names brahma-vid establishment and brahma-yoga.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

On the next pleasant and unpleasant message, withhold exultation and trembling for thirty seconds; turn attention to evenness and look for a quieter happiness that does not depend on the contact.

## Contact-Born Joys Are Wombs of Pain
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 5.22–5.24

The enjoyments born of contact are only wombs of pain; they have beginning and end, Kaunteya — the wise one does not rejoice in them. Who can endure here, before release from the body, the urge born of desire and anger — that one is integrated, that one is a happy human. Who has happiness within, delight within, and likewise light within — that yogin becomes Brahman and attains Brahman-nirvāṇa.

---

### Devanagari

ये हि संस्पर्शजा भोगा दुःखयोनय एव ते।

आद्यन्तवन्तः कौन्तेय न तेषु रमते बुधः

शक्नोतीहैव यः सोढुं प्राक्शरीरविमोक्षणात्।

कामक्रोधोद्भवं वेगं स युक्तः स सुखी नरः

योऽन्तःसुखोऽन्तरारामस्तथान्तर्ज्योतिरेव यः।

स योगी ब्रह्मनिर्वाणं ब्रह्मभूतोऽधिगच्छति

### IAST

ādyantavantaḥ kaunteya na teṣu ramate budhaḥ

śaknotīhaiva yaḥ soḍhuṃ prākśarīravimokṣaṇāt|

kāmakrodhodbhavaṃ vegaṃ sa yuktaḥ sa sukhī naraḥ

yo'ntaḥsukho'ntarārāmastathāntarjyotireva yaḥ|

sa yogī brahmanirvāṇaṃ brahmabhūto'dhigacchati

### Pratibha Translation

For the enjoyments born of contact are only wombs of pain; they have beginning and end, Kaunteya — the wise one does not delight in them. Who is able, even here, before release from the body, to endure the surge born of desire and anger — that one is integrated, that one is a happy human. Who is happy within, delighting within, and likewise light within — that yogin, become Brahman, attains Brahman-nirvāṇa.

### Pratibha Commentary

Contact-born bhogas are not mild distractions; they are duḥkha-yonayaḥ — wombs of pain — because they are ādyanta-vat, timed things. The contested happiness criterion is endurance: who can bear, before death, the vega of kāma-krodha is already yukta and sukhī. Then the interior trinity — antaḥ-sukha, antar-ārāma, antar-jyotis — marks the yogin who, brahma-bhūta, reaches brahma-nirvāṇa. In the Gītā’s late chapter-5 ascent, nirvāṇa-language enters a Brahmanic key. Existentially, stop calling surge-management “repression” when it is the doorway of happiness, and stop calling timed pleasures “life” when they gestate pain. The real festival is inward light. Calling contact-joys wombs of pain is not anti-life; it is obstetric honesty about what timed pleasures gestate. Enduring kāma-krodha’s surge before death is already happiness and integration — a criterion that relocates “the happy human” from entertainment to capacity. Inward happiness, delight, and light then mark the yogin who becomes Brahman and attains Brahman-nirvāṇa. The festival moves inside. Surge-endurance is the doorway, not a denial of joy.

### Key Terms

**duḥkhayoni (दुःखयोनि)** — womb of pain -> contact-enjoyment as generative source of suffering -> stronger than “leads to pain”

**vega (वेग)** — surge/impulse -> kāma-krodha’s force to be endured -> names kinetic affliction, not abstract vice

**brahmanirvāṇa (ब्रह्मनिर्वाण)** — Brahman-nirvāṇa -> extinction/bliss-attainment in Brahman idiom -> cross-traditional term re-keyed to brahma-bhūta

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Buddha’s First Noble Truth / vedanā analysis:** Structural homology in contact-feeling as structurally painful in its timed nature.
*Divergence:* Early Buddhism aims at nibbāna via path without ātman/Brahman; here endurance and inward light culminate in brahmanirvāṇa.

**Evagrius on enduring logismoi:** Structural homology in happiness tied to enduring passionate surges before death.
*Divergence:* Evagrian goal is apatheia in prayer; the Gītā’s goal is brahma-bhūta attainment of brahmanirvāṇa.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

When a contact-pleasure calls, label it “timed womb of pain,” endure one kāma or krodha surge without acting it out, and rest attention in inward happiness-light for one minute.

## Freed Always; Knowing the Friend of All
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 5.28–5.29

Knowing me as the enjoyer of sacrifices and austerities, the great lord of all worlds, the friend of all beings — one reaches peace.

---

### Devanagari

यतेन्द्रियमनोबुद्धिर्मुनिर्मोक्षपरायणः।

विगतेच्छाभयक्रोधो यः सदा मुक्त एव सः

भोक्तारं यज्ञतपसां सर्वलोकमहेश्वरम्।

Chapter 5 seals with a double climax: the muni whose instruments are restrained, aimed at mokṣa, emptied of icchā-bhaya-krodha, is sadā mukta — always free, not free later. Peace then arrives through knowing Krishna as enjoyer of offerings, lord of worlds, and friend of all beings. Sovereignty and friendship together prevent both cold metaphysics and sentimental piety. Chapter 5’s impersonal and personal pedagogies seal each other. Recognize the Friend who receives austerity without ceasing to befriend every being.

### Key Terms

**sadā mukta (सदा मुक्त)** — always liberated -> freedom as present standing of the restrained sage -> against postponed salvation fantasy

**suhṛd (सुहृद्)** — friend/good-hearted one -> Krishna as friend of all beings -> softens pure sovereignty into companionship

**bhoktṛ (भोक्तृ)** — enjoyer/consumer of offerings -> the Lord as recipient of yajña-tapas -> completes the sacrificial arc of chapters 3–5

*Divergence:* Christian friendship is covenantal in Christ; the Gītā’s suhṛd is enjoyer of sacrifice and lord of worlds within yoga’s mokṣa aim.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

**Edition basis:** André Padoux with Roger-Orphé Jeanty, *The Heart of the Yoginī: The Yoginīhṛdaya, a Sanskrit Tantric Treatise* (Oxford University Press, 2013), translating the Dvivedī (Motilal Banarsidass, 1988) edition with Amṛtānanda's *Dīpikā*.

The authoritative source-language basis is the IAST.

## The Question That Opens the Absolute
**Source:** Yoginīhṛdaya 1.1 (*Cakrasaṃketa*); Śrī Devī's address to Bhairava.

O God of gods, great God, expanding in total fullness, there are in this Vāmakeśvaratantra many unknown elements; please reveal them entirely, O Bhairava!

---

### Devanagari
*(editorial reconstruction from IAST; not source-verified)*

देवदेव महादेव परिपूर्णप्रथामय ।
वामकेश्वरतन्त्रेऽस्मिन्नज्ञातार्थस्त्वनेकशः ॥१॥
तांस्तानर्थानशेषेण वक्तुमर्हसि भैरव ।

### IAST

devadeva mahādeva paripūrṇaprathāmaya /
vāmakeśvaratantre 'sminn ajñātārthas tv anekaśaḥ //1//
tāṃs tān arthān aśeṣeṇa vaktum arhasi bhairava /

### Pratibha Translation

God of gods, Great God, you whose very being is total fullness unfolding — in this Vāmakeśvara teaching many meanings remain unknown [ajñātārtha]. Speak them, all of them, leaving nothing out, O Bhairava.

### Pratibha Commentary

The text stakes its authority on a structural reversal: revelation begins not with the male god's pronouncement but with the Goddess's *demand*. In a tradition where Tripurā is the supreme reality and Bhairava her co-present aspect, the opening question is not ignorance seeking information — it is the Absolute interrogating itself. This is the contested move: the dialogue "takes place out of time," so the Goddess who already *is* total fullness (paripūrṇa) nonetheless asks, dramatizing that even plenitude has an unspoken interior that must be brought to word. The request targets *ajñātārtha*, the "unknown meanings" of the Vāmakeśvaratantra — not missing data but the esoteric sense that scripture withholds until it is solicited within a relationship. The grammar of "leaving nothing out" (aśeṣeṇa) signals that what follows claims completeness: the YH presents itself as the heart that makes an entire textual corpus intelligible. For the practitioner this frames knowing as relational and erotic rather than propositional: truth is drawn out by desire and address, not seized. The first existential lesson is therefore postural — one does not master the teaching; one petitions it, and the petition is itself already inside the divine self-knowing.

### Key Terms

**ajñātārtha (अज्ञातार्थ)** — *a-jñāta* ("not-known") + *artha* ("meaning, object, purpose"). Not "facts I lack" but the *latent sense* of a revealed text; in initiatory traditions the surface text is public while its *artha* is sealed until transmitted. Default renderings ("unknown matters") miss that *artha* is a target of realization, not information.

**paripūrṇa (परिपूर्ण)** — *pari* ("around, completely") + *pūrṇa* ("full"). Total, surrounding fullness; here a *qualifier of Bhairava's being* (prathā-maya, "made of unfolding"). It matters because the questioner addresses fullness *as* fullness — the asking is internal to plenitude, not a deficiency outside it.

**Vāmakeśvaratantra (वामकेश्वरतन्त्र)** — the scriptural matrix (or its larger Tripurā/Śrīvidyā ensemble) of which the YH is the "heart." Naming it locates this text as commentary-revelation upon a prior whole.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Kena Upaniṣad 1.1 ("By whom impelled does the mind fly forth?"):** Both open by *questioning the source of awareness* rather than asserting it, staging revelation as response to a precise interrogation.
*Divergence:* The Kena's questioner is a human pupil seeking the unknowable Brahman behind the senses; the YH's questioner is the Goddess herself, so the "unknown" is not epistemically inaccessible but deliberately *withheld intimacy* — secrecy, not transcendence, is the barrier.

**Bhagavad Gītā 1–2 (Arjuna's appeal to Kṛṣṇa):** A divine teaching is elicited by a beloved interlocutor's pressing request, and the teacher's authority is constituted by being *asked*.
*Divergence:* Arjuna asks from collapse and confusion; Śrī Devī asks from fullness and free will (cf. *svecchā*, śloka 9). The YH thus models inquiry as sovereign play rather than crisis-resolution.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Before study today, do not begin by asserting what you know. Form one precise question about the matter before you and address it aloud, as to a presence: "Speak this, completely." Then read as one who has petitioned, not as one who already owns.

## Whispered Ear to Ear: Secrecy and the Sky-Going State
**Source:** Yoginīhṛdaya 1.2–5 (*Cakrasaṃketa*); Śrī Bhairava's reply.

Hear, O Goddess, the great secret, the Heart of the Yoginī, supreme. What I tell you now out of love for you is to be kept carefully hidden. On earth, it has [always] been taught and received by word of mouth. It must not be given to the disciples of other [masters], nor to unbelievers, O Goddess! nor to those who do not wish intensely to hear it, or who do not give over riches. It can be given to [someone] who has been examined and tested during half a year. As soon as one knows it, O Woman with beautiful hips! one attains the state of khecara.

---

### Devanagari
*(editorial reconstruction from IAST; not source-verified)*

शृणु देवि महागुह्यं योगिनीहृदयं परम् ॥२॥
त्वत्प्रीत्या कथयाम्यद्य गोपितव्यं विशेषतः ।
कर्णात्कर्णोपदेशेन सम्प्राप्तमवनीतलम् ॥३॥
न देयं परशिष्येभ्यो नास्तिकेभ्यो न चेश्वरि ।
न शुश्रूषालसानां च नैवानर्थप्रदायिनाम् ॥४॥
परीक्षिताय दातव्यं वत्सरार्धोषिताय च ।
एतज्ज्ञात्वा वरारोहे सद्यः खेचरतां व्रजेत् ॥५॥

### IAST

śṛṇu devi mahāguhyaṃ yoginīhṛdayaṃ param //2//
tvatprītyā kathayāmy adya gopitavyaṃ viśeṣataḥ /
karṇāt karṇopadeśena samprāptam avanītalam //3//
na deyaṃ paraśiṣyebhyo nāstikebhyo na ceśvari /
na śuśrūṣālasānāṃ ca naivānarthapradāyinām //4//
parīkṣitāya dātavyaṃ vatsarārdhoṣitāya ca /
etaj jñātvā varārohe sadyaḥ khecaratāṃ vrajet //5//

### Pratibha Translation

Listen, Goddess, to the great secret — the supreme Heart of the Yoginī. Out of love for you I speak it now; guard it closely. On earth it travels only mouth to ear, ear to mouth. Do not hand it to others' disciples, to scoffers, to the spiritually lazy who will not strain to hear, or to those who withhold their substance. Give it to one tested through half a year. The instant one truly knows this, fair one, one moves as the sky-goer [khecara].

### Pratibha Commentary

The passage makes a strong claim: the teaching's *content is inseparable from its mode of transmission*. What is to be known cannot be detached from the relational conditions — love, secrecy, oral lineage, testing — under which it passes; knowledge here is not a portable proposition but an initiatic event. The counterintuitive move is the abrupt collapse of the long ethical preamble (whom to refuse, how long to test) into a single soteriological promise: "the instant one knows this, one becomes *khecara*." Secrecy is not mere institutional gatekeeping; it encodes a phenomenological truth — esoteric meaning that is published flatly is not the same object as meaning received "ear to ear" within trust. The mention of riches, jarring to modern readers, situates the text in a world where lavish expenditure on the deity is itself a discipline against the stinginess of the contracted ego. Most important is *khecaratā*: "moving in the sky." Padoux notes that *kha* is the void of the wheel's hub transposed to the central void (*śūnya*) of consciousness; the one who "moves in *kha*" is identified with Śiva. So the verse equates knowing-as-initiation with liberation itself — the sky one travels is the open interior of awareness, not outer space. Existentially: realization is gated less by intelligence than by capacity for sustained, tested, loving attention.

### Key Terms

**khecaratā (खेचरता)** — *khe* (locative of *kha*, "void, sky, the hub-hole of a wheel") + *cara* ("moving") + *-tā* ("-ness"). Literally "the state of moving in the void/sky." A *siddhi* of free movement through space, but esoterically *fusion with the central void of consciousness*, identification with Śiva — hence the highest liberation. Rendering it merely as "flight" or a magic power misses that the "sky" is the heart-void (*kha = śūnya*) of awareness itself.

**guhya / gopitavya (गुह्य / गोपितव्य)** — from √*guh* ("to conceal"). *mahāguhya*, "great secret"; *gopitavya*, "to be kept hidden." Marks the teaching as structurally esoteric: concealment is a condition of its efficacy, not an accident of policy.

**karṇāt karṇopadeśa (कर्णात्कर्णोपदेश)** — "instruction from ear to ear." The technical name for oral, lineage-bound transmission (*śrutiparamparā* in practice); the knowledge is carried by living breath between bodies, not by text.

**parīkṣita / vatsarārdha (परीक्षित / वत्सरार्ध)** — "tested" over "half a year." Initiation is conditioned on demonstrated fitness over time, encoding that the recipient must be transformed into a vessel before receiving.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Bṛhadāraṇyaka / Chāndogya *upaniṣad* (the very word "sitting-near-secretly"):** The Upaniṣadic genre is named for secret, proximate transmission, and likewise reserves the highest teaching for the tested pupil.
*Divergence:* Upaniṣadic secrecy guards *brahmavidyā*, knowledge of an impersonal Absolute; the YH guards a *power-laden ritual identity* with the Goddess, so its secret is operative (it *does* something — confers khecaratā) rather than purely gnostic.

**Gospel of Mark 4:11 / 4:34 ("to you is given the secret… privately to his own disciples he explained everything"):** A teacher reserves an inner explanation for an intimate circle while the crowd receives only the surface.
*Divergence:* The Markan secret is eschatological and will be openly proclaimed; the Tantric *guhya* is constitutively permanent — disclosure to the unfit doesn't merely risk misunderstanding, it voids the transmission's power.

**Epictetus, *Enchiridion* (the long probation of the Stoic *prokoptōn*):** Both gate advanced teaching behind sustained testing of character rather than mere assent.
*Divergence:* The Stoic withholds until the student can *reason* rightly; the YH withholds until the student can *receive* rightly within devotion — the criterion is fitness-for-intimacy, not argumentative competence.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Choose one insight you hold most precious and, for today, do not broadcast it. Carry it silently. Notice how withholding concentrates it — how the unspoken truth gathers heat in the "void" of the chest. Let that gathered attention be your first taste of moving in *kha*.

## Threefold Co-Presence: Cakra, Mantra, Worship
**Source:** Yoginīhṛdaya 1.6 (*Cakrasaṃketa*).

O Supreme Goddess! Threefold is the agreed co-presence of the goddess Tripurā: the agreement concerning the cakra, and those concerning the mantra and the worship.

---

### Devanagari
*(editorial reconstruction from IAST; not source-verified)*

चक्रसंकेतको मन्त्रपूजासंकेतकौ तथा ।
त्रिविधस्त्रिपुरदेव्याः संकेतः परमेश्वरि ॥६॥

### IAST

cakrasaṃketako mantrapūjāsaṃketakau tathā /
trividhas tripuradevyāḥ saṃketaḥ parameśvari //6//

### Pratibha Translation

Supreme Sovereign — the *saṃketa* of the goddess Tripurā is threefold: the co-presence within the cakra, and likewise those within the mantra and within the worship.

### Pratibha Commentary

The verse names the architecture of the entire treatise, and its key word resists translation on purpose. *Saṃketa* means at once "agreement/convention," "appointed meeting-place," and "co-presence" — Padoux deliberately renders it doubly. The philosophical claim is that the Goddess is not located in a diagram, a sound, and a rite as three separate objects; rather, each is a *rendez-vous* where Śiva-Bhairava and the Goddess are mutually present *by agreement*, and that very co-presence is what makes the form efficacious. This is the contested, anti-representational move: the śrīcakra does not *depict* the Goddess, the mantra does not *refer* to her, the pūjā does not *commemorate* her — each is a site where the two poles of reality actually meet, so the sign and the signified coincide. The threefold division is therefore not a syllabus of topics but three modulations of a single presence: spatial-diagrammatic (cakra), sonic-vibratory (mantra), and enacted-temporal (pūjā). The three chapters of the YH simply unfold these. Existentially, *saṃketa* reframes symbol: a true symbol is a place you can *go to* and be met, not a token you decode. The practitioner's task is to keep the appointment.

### Key Terms

**saṃketa (संकेत)** — *sam* ("together") + √*kit*/*ket* ("to intend, appoint, signal"). Simultaneously (1) a convention/agreement, (2) an appointed tryst or meeting-place, (3) co-presence. The YH exploits all three: the cakra/mantra/pūjā are *agreed meeting-places* where deity and adept are co-present. Single-word renderings ("symbol," "code," "convention") each amputate two of the three senses; the term's whole force is that meaning *is* a rendez-vous.

**Tripurā / Tripurasundarī (त्रिपुरा)** — "She of the three cities/fortresses"; the supreme Goddess of the Śrīvidyā tradition, whose triplicity (will–knowledge–action; the three levels of Word; the three saṃketas here) structures the cosmos.

**trividha (त्रिविध)** — "threefold." Here not an enumeration of parts but a triad of *modes* of one presence (spatial, sonic, ritual).

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Pratyabhijñā / Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam (Kṣemarāja): consciousness as self-recognition manifesting as the world:** Both hold that the divine is *present in* its manifestations, not merely signified by them; the cakra "is" the Goddess as recognition (*vimarśa*) takes form.
*Divergence:* Pratyabhijñā centers a single act of recognition (*pratyabhijñā*) of the Self; the YH distributes presence across three operative registers (diagram, sound, rite), making *saṃketa* a ritual-practical, not only gnostic, category.

**Eastern Orthodox theology of the icon (the icon as window, not portrait):** Like *saṃketa*, the icon is a site of real presence and encounter rather than mimetic representation.
*Divergence:* The icon mediates a *personal* presence one venerates across an ontological gap; the cakra is the *cosmic body* of the Goddess in which the adept seeks identity (becoming khecara) — encounter aims at fusion, not relation across distance.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Pick one of your habitual sacred forms — an image, a phrase you repeat, a small daily rite. Today, treat it not as a reminder *of* something absent but as an *appointment*: arrive at it on time, fully, expecting to be met there.

## What Knowing Confers: The Seat of Supreme Authority
**Source:** Yoginīhṛdaya 1.7 (*Cakrasaṃketa*).

As long as one does not know this threefold agreement, one will not be recipient of the supreme authority [inherent] in the cakra of Tripurā.

---

### Devanagari
*(editorial reconstruction from IAST; not source-verified)*

यावदेतन्न जानाति संकेतत्रयमुत्तमम् ।
न तावत्त्रिपुराचक्रे परमाज्ञाधरो भवेत् ॥७॥

### IAST

yāvad etan na jānāti saṃketatrayam uttamam /
na tāvat tripurācakre paramājñādharo bhavet //7//

### Pratibha Translation

So long as one does not know this supreme triad of co-presences, just so long one does not become a bearer of the highest authority [paramājñā] within Tripurā's cakra.

### Pratibha Commentary

The verse advances a precise claim about the relation between knowledge and power: authority in the cakra is not granted from outside but *conferred by knowing*. The construction *yāvat… na… na tāvat* ("so long as not… just so long not") makes the dependency strict and temporal — there is no partial credit and no bypass. The contested move is the word *ājñā*. It means "command, order, sovereign authority," but in this Tantric register *paramājñā* is the *divine command-power itself*, the Goddess's own sovereign will operative within the diagram. To "bear" it (*-dhara*) is to become a conduit of that will, not merely to gain permission to use the cakra. So the verse is not bureaucratic ("you need the password") but ontological: to *know* the threefold saṃketa is to be assimilated to the agency that animates the cakra, such that one's own command participates in hers. This is why the preceding verse promised khecaratā "the instant one knows": knowing here is transformative cognition, *jñāna* that reconstitutes the knower. The existential edge is sharp and unsentimental — incomprehension is not innocent; it is exclusion from one's own latent sovereignty. The diagram remains inert and the rite empty until understanding turns the practitioner into a bearer of the very authority the form contains.

### Key Terms

**ājñā / paramājñā (आज्ञा / परमाज्ञा)** — √*jñā* with *ā*: "command, order, sanction, sovereign authority." *paramā ājñā* = the "supreme command," the Goddess's own will-as-power operative in the cakra. Distinct from *ājñā* as the "command center" (brow) *cakra* of haṭha systems; here it is sovereignty itself. Translating it "permission" or "instruction" guts its sense of *participated divine agency*.

**-dhara (धर)** — "bearer, holder" (√*dhṛ*, "to hold, sustain"). *ājñādhara* is one who *carries and sustains* the command, i.e., becomes its living vehicle — not one who merely obeys it.

**yāvat … tāvat (यावत् … तावत्)** — correlative "as long as … so long as," marking a strict conditional dependency between *knowing* the triad and *bearing* authority.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Śiva Sūtra 1.1 *caitanyam ātmā* / "jñānaṃ bandhaḥ" (1.2):** The Śaiva insistence that the quality of one's knowing *is* the difference between bondage and sovereignty parallels the YH's claim that knowing the saṃketa confers command.
*Divergence:* The Śiva Sūtra warns that ordinary "knowledge" (limited *jñāna*) is itself the bondage; the YH speaks positively of a determinate esoteric knowing that *empowers* — the two together frame a spectrum from contracted to expanded cognition.

**Plato, *Republic* (only the one who knows the Good is fit to rule):** Authority is grounded in a specific cognition unavailable to the many, and rule without that knowledge is illegitimate.
*Divergence:* Platonic knowledge of the Good is dialectical and in principle publicly arguable; the YH's *paramājñā* is initiatic and conferred through ritual co-presence — authority here is participatory identification with a divine will, not the conclusion of an argument.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Identify one practice you perform without understanding *why its form is shaped as it is*. Today, withhold the autopilot: ask of the form, "What presence are you the meeting-place of?" Let authority over the practice wait upon that understanding.

## Five Energies, Four Fires: The Cakra as Pulse of Emanation and Return
**Source:** Yoginīhṛdaya 1.8 (*Cakrasaṃketa*).

This [cakra includes] five energies directed toward emanation (sṛṣṭyā), and four fires turned toward resorption (layena). The cakra is [therefore] produced by the conjunction of five energies and four fires.

---

### Devanagari
*(editorial reconstruction from IAST; not source-verified)*

तच्छक्तिपञ्चकं सृष्ट्या लयेनाग्निचतुष्टयम् ।
पञ्चशक्तिचतुर्वह्निसंयोगाच्चक्रसम्भवः ॥८॥

### IAST

tacchaktipañcakaṃ sṛṣṭyā layenāgnicatuṣṭayam /
pañcaśakticaturvahnisaṃyogāc cakrasambhavaḥ //8//

### Pratibha Translation

Its five energies tend toward emission [sṛṣṭi]; its four fires, toward resorption [laya]. From the conjunction of the five energies and the four fires the cakra comes to be.

### Pratibha Commentary

The verse asserts that the śrīcakra is not a static figure but a *frozen dynamism*: it exists only as the conjunction (*saṃyoga*) of two opposed cosmic tendencies. The five triangles apex-downward are *śakti*, the feminine, creative pulse toward emanation (*sṛṣṭi*); the four apex-upward are the "fires" (*vahni/agni*), the masculine pulse toward dissolution (*laya*). The counterintuitive claim is that the diagram's very *being* (*cakra-sambhava*) is their intersection — remove the tension between outflow and reabsorption and there is no cakra at all. This encodes a metaphysics in which reality is intrinsically rhythmic: creation and destruction are not sequential events but simultaneous, mutually constituting vectors, and the Goddess is the whole oscillation rather than either pole. Numerically, five against four refuses tidy symmetry; the surplus of creative energies over destructive fires quietly privileges manifestation — the cosmos leans toward appearing. For the practitioner, the śrīcakra thus becomes a contemplative instrument for seeing one's own life as the live interpenetration of emitting and dissolving: every breath, perception, and thought arises (sṛṣṭi) and subsides (laya), and the "self" is not a thing but the standing pattern their conjunction makes — exactly as the cakra is.

### Key Terms

**śakti (शक्ति)** — √*śak* ("to be able"): "power, capacity, energy"; the feminine, dynamic aspect of the godhead, here the five emanative triangles. Not "force" in a mechanical sense but the deity's own *will-to-manifest*; the cosmos is her play.

**sṛṣṭi / laya (सृष्टि / लय)** — √*sṛj* ("to emit, let go") vs. √*lī* ("to dissolve, cling, melt into"). The paired cosmic movements of *emission* and *reabsorption*. Crucially *sṛṣṭi* is "emission/letting-forth," not creation *ex nihilo*; *laya* is "melting back," not annihilation — the dyad is respiration, not manufacture-and-destruction.

**vahni / agni-catuṣṭaya (वह्नि / अग्निचतुष्टय)** — the "four fires," the masculine triangles oriented to *laya*. Fire here is the resorptive, in-gathering principle (consuming back into source), counterpart to the out-flowing energies.

**saṃyoga (संयोग)** — "conjunction, union." The cakra's existence is *relational*: it is the event of two tendencies meeting, not a substance.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Spandakārikā (the doctrine of *spanda*, the divine "vibration/pulse" of consciousness):** Both make reality fundamentally *rhythmic* — being is a throb of expansion and contraction, and stable forms are standing waves of that pulse.
*Divergence:* *Spanda* is primarily the subtle quiver of consciousness *prior* to any diagram; the YH's verse spatializes the pulse into a *geometry* of five-plus-four triangles, binding the metaphysics of vibration to a constructible figure one can worship.

**Heraclitus, DK B51 ("a back-stretched connection, as of bow and lyre"):** A thing's identity is held together precisely by opposed tensions; harmony *is* strife in balance.
*Divergence:* Heraclitus's *palintonos harmoniē* is impersonal *logos*; the YH's tension is the self-willed play of a personal Goddess, so the opposition is not blind cosmic law but desire (*svecchā*) taking form.

**Daoist generative cosmology (Dao De Jing 42, "the Dao gives birth… the ten thousand things carry yin and embrace yang"):** Manifestation arises from the conjunction of complementary polarities rather than from a single principle acting alone.
*Divergence:* The Daoist polarity tends toward quiet equilibrium and *wu wei*; the Śrīvidyā polarity is asymmetric (five vs. four) and *expressive* — it leans into manifestation as the Goddess's exuberant self-display, not toward minimal interference.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Sit and watch three full breaths. On each in-breath sense reabsorption (laya), the world melting toward your center; on each out-breath sense emission (sṛṣṭi), the world flung outward. Do not stop either. Rest as the conjunction itself — the place where the two meet and the "cakra" of this moment stands.

## Sphurattā: The Cakra Springs from the Goddess Seeing Herself
**Source:** Yoginīhṛdaya 1.9 (*Cakrasaṃketa*).

When She, the Supreme Power, [becoming] out of her own free will embodied as all that exists (viśvarūpinī), perceives herself as flashing forth, the cakra then appears.

---

### Devanagari
*(editorial reconstruction from IAST; not source-verified)*

यदा सा परमा शक्तिः स्वेच्छया विश्वरूपिणी ।
स्फुरत्तामात्मनः पश्येत्तदा चक्रस्य सम्भवः ॥९॥

### IAST

yadā sā paramā śaktiḥ svecchayā viśvarūpiṇī /
sphurattām ātmanaḥ paśyet tadā cakrasya sambhavaḥ //9//

### Pratibha Translation

When the Supreme Power, by her own free will having become she-whose-form-is-all, beholds her own flashing-forth [sphurattā], then the cakra comes to be.

### Pratibha Commentary

This is the metaphysical heart of the opening: the cakra originates in an act of *self-perception*. The Supreme Power, freely (*svecchayā*) taking the form of the universe (*viśvarūpiṇī*), "beholds her own *sphurattā*" — her own throbbing flash of self-luminosity — and *in that seeing* the cakra arises. The radical claim is that manifestation is reflexive: the world is not made *by* the Goddess as an external product but *is* her looking at her own radiance. This rests on the Pratyabhijñā/Trika distinction between *prakāśa* (the light of consciousness, sheer shining) and *vimarśa* (consciousness's active self-awareness, its "I-touch"); *sphurattā* names the dynamic moment where light becomes aware *of itself as light* and that very self-awareness emits the cosmos. Amṛtānanda glosses it exactly: "the intensely luminous flashing forth of the Supreme Power is nothing else than the emission of the cosmos." Note the priority of *svecchā*: nothing compels the act — creation is sovereign freedom, play (līlā), not necessity. The cakra, then, is the diagram of an event of self-recognition, which is why knowing it (śloka 7) confers the Goddess's own authority: to understand the cakra is to re-enact, in oneself, consciousness recognizing its own flashing. The existential implication is vertiginous — your own moments of suddenly *catching yourself being aware* are micro-instances of the same sphurattā by which a universe springs forth.

### Key Terms

**sphurattā (स्फुरत्ता)** — √*sphur* ("to throb, quiver, flash, burst forth") + *-tā*. The "flashing-forth," the vibrant self-luminous pulsation of consciousness becoming aware of itself; near-synonym of *spanda* but stressing *radiant scintillation*. No single English word holds both "flash of light" and "throb of life"; "vibration" loses the luminosity, "radiance" loses the movement.

**svecchā (स्वेच्छा)** — *sva* ("own") + *icchā* ("will, desire"). "Of her own free will/desire." Marks creation as *autonomous play*, not response to any external cause or lack; *icchā* is the first of the Goddess's three powers (will–knowledge–action).

**viśvarūpiṇī (विश्वरूपिणी)** — *viśva* ("all, universe") + *rūpa* ("form") + feminine *-iṇī*: "she whose form is the All." The Goddess does not make the universe at a distance; she *becomes* it as her own body.

**vimarśa / prakāśa (विमर्श / प्रकाश)** — *prakāśa*, the static "shining" light of awareness; *vimarśa*, its reflexive self-"touching" or self-awareness. *Sphurattā* is the live hinge between them: light grasping itself. The world is *vimarśa* of *prakāśa*.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam, sūtra 1 (*citiḥ svatantrā viśvasiddhihetuḥ*, "Consciousness, independent, is the cause of the accomplishment of the universe"):** Near-identical doctrine — autonomous (*svatantra* ≈ *svecchā*) consciousness produces the world by its own self-awareness.
*Divergence:* Kṣemarāja's formulation is austerely philosophical (a sūtra about *citi*); the YH *embodies* the same insight as a Goddess beholding her radiance and *crystallizes it into a worshippable diagram* — metaphysics becomes liturgy.

**Plotinus, *Enneads* (the One's *energeia* and Intellect's self-vision; reality unfolds as the prior "overflows" and the posterior turns to contemplate its source):** Manifestation is tied to an act of intellectual *seeing*, and being proceeds from a self-related luminosity.
*Divergence:* For Plotinus emanation is hierarchical and the One does not strictly will or "see" itself into multiplicity (it overflows without diminution); the YH makes the first act explicitly *volitional and feminine self-perception* (*svecchayā… ātmanaḥ paśyet*) — creation is desired self-recognition, not impersonal overflow.

**Genesis 1 ("Let there be light… and God saw that it was good"):** Creation is bound up with light and with a divine *seeing*.
*Divergence:* In Genesis God sees the light *as object* and pronounces it good, preserving creator–creation distance; in the YH the Goddess sees *her own* flashing and that seeing *is* the emanation — no gap opens between the seer, the seeing, and the seen.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Once today, the instant you notice you are aware — catch the small "flash" of realizing you are present. Do not analyze it; simply let that self-catching be recognized as *sphurattā*, the same flash from which a world is said to spring. Stay with it for one breath.

**Source:** Yoginīhṛdaya 1.10cd–13 (*Cakrasaṃketa*).

From the void letter A and from that which ends by emission, [that is] from the bindu, vibrating consciousness whose supreme nature is light and which is united with the flashing flow [of divine power, appears] the throne of the bindu (baindavāsana) which is the [birth]place of the flow made up of the three mātṛkās. It then assumes a threefold aspect. [Thus appear] dharma and adharma, then the [four] ātman, the knower, what is to be known and knowledge: the cakra is thus a ninefold womb. It is immense, a compact mass of consciousness and bliss. A division of mantras in nine [corresponds to] this ninefold cakra.

### Pratibha Translation

Out of the void-shaped A and out of what culminates in emission — out of the bindu, vibrant awareness whose deepest truth is light, joined to the surging wave [of power] — streams the throne-of-the-bindu, the seat of the threefold cosmic wave, holding the three mother-energies. ### Pratibha Commentary

The passage makes a generative claim: the entire field of experience is born from the meeting of the unmanifest and its first point of self-concentration. "Void-A" (*śūnyākāra*) is supreme Śiva *before* creation — pure potential, called void because it precedes all content; the *bindu* is the dot of fused Śiva-Śakti, energy contracted to a luminous point. From their union "streams" not a thing but a *seat* (*baindavāsana*) — the womb-throne from which the three levels of the Word (the *mātṛkās*: paśyantī, madhyamā, vaikharī) flow. The counterintuitive move is what counts as the cosmos's first offspring: not earth, water, or matter, but *epistemic and ethical structure* — dharma and adharma, the four selves, and above all the triad knower–knowable–knowing (*pramātṛ–prameya–pramā*). Reality, on this account, is not first physical and then cognized; it is *cognitive at its root*, a "ninefold womb" (*navayoni*) that is simultaneously a geometry (nine triangles) and a phenomenology (nine constituents of any act of awareness). And this womb is *cidānandaghana* — a "compact mass of consciousness and bliss," refusing any split between being aware and being glad to be. The existential force: the structures you take as the neutral furniture of experience — subject, object, the law you obey or break — are themselves the first emanation of a luminous point, not brute givens but the womb's own children.

### Key Terms

**bindu (बिन्दु)** — "drop, point." The concentrated point in which Śiva and Śakti fuse; both the totality of the Absolute and the seed of manifestation. Always described as luminous and *throbbing* (vibrating), so "point" must be heard dynamically — a pulsing concentration of creative power, not a static dot.

**navayoni (नवयोनि)** — *nava* ("nine") + *yoni* ("womb, source, matrix"). The "ninefold womb," the central nine-triangle figure understood as the generative matrix of nine cosmic constituents. *Yoni* keeps its literal genital-maternal force: the cakra is not a symbol of a womb but functions *as* one.

**cidānandaghana (चिदानन्दघन)** — *cit* ("consciousness") + *ānanda* ("bliss") + *ghana* ("dense mass, compact"). A "solid mass of consciousness-bliss." *Ghana* resists the idea of consciousness as thin or empty awareness: here it is *dense*, packed, substantial — and inseparably blissful.

**pramātṛ / prameya / pramā (प्रमातृ / प्रमेय / प्रमा)** — knower / object-of-knowledge / knowing (or means of knowing, *pramāṇa*). The standard Indian epistemic triad, here treated as a *cosmological* product — the world just *is* the standing structure of subject, object, and the act between them.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Sāṃkhya/Vedānta epistemology (the *pramātṛ–prameya–pramāṇa* triad):** The YH borrows the classical knowledge-triad but *re-sites* it — what philosophy treats as the analysis of cognition, the YH treats as the very content of creation.
*Divergence:* For the epistemologists the triad describes how we *know* an independently existing world; for the YH the triad *is* the world's first emergence from the bindu, collapsing the line between metaphysics and epistemology.

**Plotinus, *Enneads* V.1 (the One, then Intellect in which knower and known coincide):** Both derive a structured intelligible order from a simple prior source, and both make the knower–known relation foundational to manifest reality.
*Divergence:* Plotinus's Intellect is *below* the One and its self-knowing marks a fall from absolute simplicity; the YH's knower–known triad is the Goddess's *bliss-laden self-display* (cidānanda), not a declension — multiplicity here is celebration, not loss.

**Daoist "the named is the mother of the ten thousand things" (Dao De Jing 1):** A nameless source gives rise to a first articulation ("the named") which then mothers all particulars — structurally parallel to void-A → bindu → the ninefold womb.
*Divergence:* The Daoist mother births *things* (the ten thousand things); the YH's womb births *the structure of knowing itself* first, foregrounding consciousness where the Daoist text foregrounds spontaneous natural emergence.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Take any single perception right now — a sound, a patch of color. Without changing it, notice its three "children": that there is a *knower* (you), a *known* (the object), and the live *knowing* binding them. Hold all three at once for three breaths, sensing them as one dense field of awareness rather than three separate things.

## The Expanding Triangles: Consciousness Differentiates into the Senses

[This ninefold cakra] is present in the [next cakra], of eight triangles, in the form of Ambikā, surrounded by vowels; it is the dynamism of consciousness, the fire of resorption rising from the throne of the bindu. The [first of the two cakra] of ten triangles is a shining form born from the flashing forth of the nine triangles. It causes the luminosity of the ten phonemes, from the one preceding śakti to the last of the nine [following ones]. It is the support of the light of the ten subtle and gross elements. The flashing form of the second ten-pointed [cakra is associated with the ten] phonemes beginning with krodhīśa. The transformation of the luminescence of the [first] four cakras conjoined results in the fourteen-angled one whose nature is that of sense perception and of the sense organs. [The next cakra] is made up of the expansion, in the highest sense, of the phonemes from khecarī to jayā, its nature being therefore that of the luminescence of Raudrī flashing forth as fire and energy.

### Pratibha Commentary

The verses assert a single, radical identity: *the geometry of the diagram is the structure of perception itself*. As the central womb expands outward through the eight-triangle, two ten-triangle, and fourteen-triangle figures, what is being generated is not ornament but the apparatus of experience — the phonemes (the Word), the ten elements (*bhūta* and *tanmātra*, the objective world), and finally "sense-perception and the sense-organs" (*saṃvitti-karaṇa*). The contested move is the welding of three normally separate registers: *sound* (Sanskrit phonemes), *world* (gross and subtle elements), and *cognition* (the senses) are laid onto a single nested figure, asserting that they are one differentiation seen three ways. Equally striking is that the expansion is governed by *fire* — *saṃvartānala*, the "fire of resorption" — even as it creates: each outward differentiation already carries the counter-movement of return. For the practitioner the teaching dissolves the assumed boundary between perceiver, perceived, and the medium of perception: when you see a color or hear a word, you are reading one of these triangles, a localized flash of the same consciousness that the whole diagram maps.

### Key Terms

**Ambikā (अम्बिका)** — "Mother." The Goddess as origin who gathers the three first energy-deities (Vāmā, Jyeṣṭhā, Raudrī); here she is the still maternal center of the eight-triangle figure, encircled by the vowels. Not merely a name of the Goddess but a *structural function*: the unifying source prior to the triad of powers.

**svara / varṇa (स्वर / वर्ण)** — *svara*, "vowel" (lit. "that which sounds of itself"); the vowels are the *śakti*-energies of Śaiva phonematics, the seed-powers from which the consonants (the manifest world) unfold. To be "ringed by the vowels" is to be surrounded by pure creative potency.

**saṃvitti / karaṇa (संवित्ति / करण)** — *saṃvitti*, "perception, awareness"; *karaṇa*, "instrument, sense-organ." The fourteen-triangle figure *is* perception-plus-its-instruments — cognition is here a geometric region of the deity, not a private mental event.

**saṃvartānala (संवर्तानल)** — *saṃvarta* ("dissolution, the cosmic end") + *anala* ("fire"). The "fire of resorption" present *within* the creative expansion — manifestation and reabsorption co-arise; even outflow is shadowed by return.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Abhinavagupta's phonematic emanation (the *mātṛkā*/*mālinī* doctrine: the cosmos unfolds as the Sanskrit alphabet from A to KṢA):** Both make the phonemes the literal substance of manifestation, so that perceiving the world is "reading" graded sound.
*Divergence:* Abhinavagupta's scheme is primarily *linguistic-metaphysical*; the YH binds the same phonematic emanation to a *constructible visual diagram* and to the bodily senses, making it operable in worship and meditation rather than only contemplable.

**Aristotle, *De Anima* II–III (the senses as the form-receiving powers that "become" their objects):** Both treat sense-perception as a structured set of powers central to how reality becomes present, and both refuse to separate the perceiver sharply from the perceived form.
*Divergence:* For Aristotle the senses receive forms *from* an independently existing world; for the YH the senses and their objects are *co-emanated* from the bindu, so perception is the cosmos perceiving itself, not a subject sampling an external given.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Choose one sense right now — hearing, say. For ten breaths, attend not to *what* you hear but to the *act* of hearing as a live radiance. Sense it as one "triangle" of awareness flashing outward from a still center, and let the heard sound and the hearing reveal themselves as a single event.

### Pratibha Translation

The square is a form of Jyeṣṭhā; the threefold circle, a form of Vāmā. The inmost triangle shares in pure consciousness [cit]; the eight triangles belong to the *śāntyātīta* portion. The two ten-triangle figures and the fourteen-triangle one belong to *śānti*; the eight-petaled lotus is woven of the radiance of the *vidyā* portion. The clearly shining sixteen-petaled lotus is embodied in *pratiṣṭhā*; and when the *nivṛtti* portion shines forth, the square shines.

### Pratibha Commentary

The claim here is exact and consequential: *the śrīcakra is a scale model of the entire descent of reality, read from center to edge*. The five *kalās* — "portions" of the cosmos in the Tantric *ṣaḍadhvan* scheme — are mapped one-to-one onto the nested figures: the central triangle is pure consciousness (*cit*); the eight-triangle figure is *śāntyātīta* (Śiva-Śakti, "beyond peace"); the ten- and fourteen-triangle figures are *śānti*; the eight-petal lotus is *vidyā*; the sixteen-petal lotus is *pratiṣṭhā*; and the outermost square is *nivṛtti* — earth. The counterintuitive structural fact is the *direction*: the highest reality sits at the smallest, innermost point, and the grossest, most extended reality (earth, the square) occupies the largest outer ring. Spatial size is inversely proportional to ontological rank — magnitude is a mark of descent, not of importance. This is geometry doing theology: to move inward across the diagram is to ascend through the levels of being toward the source; to move outward is to watch consciousness condense into world. The Goddess is named at each rung (Jyeṣṭhā as the square, Vāmā as the circles), so the map is never abstract — every "portion" is a face of her. For contemplative life this licenses a precise discipline: the world's solidity (the square, *nivṛtti*) is not opposed to spirit but is its outermost shining, the place where consciousness has become earth and can be traced back home.

### Key Terms

**kalā (कला)** — "portion, segment, part" (also "art, the digit of the moon"). In the *ṣaḍadhvan* cosmology, the five *kalās* — *nivṛtti, pratiṣṭhā, vidyā, śānti, śāntyātīta* — are graded bands of reality from earth to Śiva-Śakti. "Portion" undersells it: each *kalā* is a whole *world-level*, encompassing tattvas, worlds, and phonemes.

**śāntyātīta / nivṛtti (शान्त्यतीत / निवृत्ति)** — *śāntyātīta*, "transcending peace," the highest *kalā* (Śiva-Śakti); *nivṛtti*, "cessation/turning-back," the lowest, = earth. The pair names the two poles of the descent the diagram encodes, from pure source to dense matter.

**cit (चित्)** — "consciousness" in its pure, undifferentiated sense (√*cit*, "to be aware"). Assigned to the inmost triangle: the center of the figure is awareness as such, prior to the graded portions that ring it.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Neoplatonic hierarchy of hypostases (One → Intellect → Soul → Nature → Body):** Both arrange reality as graded emanative levels descending from a simple, supreme source to extended materiality, and both treat ascent as a return inward/upward.
*Divergence:* Plotinus's levels are *ontologically distinct hypostases* with real declension; the YH's *kalās* are *all the Goddess*, simultaneously present in one diagram — descent is her self-display, so "lower" never means alienated from the source.

**The Kabbalistic Tree of Sefirot (a single figure mapping the descent from Keter to Malkhut/earth):** Like the śrīcakra, a *drawable* diagram encodes the full graded emanation of divinity into world, usable for contemplative ascent.
*Divergence:* The Sefirot are typically arranged top-to-bottom with Malkhut at the base; the śrīcakra arranges descent *center-to-periphery*, so transcendence is figured as *innerness* rather than *height* — a different spatial metaphor for the same conviction that the world is graded divinity.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Look at the most ordinary solid object near you — a wall, a floor, the table. Instead of treating it as dead matter, regard it as *nivṛtti*: the outermost shining of consciousness, where awareness has condensed into earth. Trace, in attention, one step inward — from the object's solidity to your bare awareness of it — and rest there for three breaths.

### IAST

trailokyamohanādye tu navacakre sureśvari /
nādo binduḥ kalā jyeṣṭhā raudrī vāmā tathā punaḥ //22//
viṣagni dūtarī caiva sarvānandā kramāt sthitāḥ /
nirāṃśau nādabindū ca kalā cecchāsvarūpakam //23//

### Pratibha Translation

In the nine cakras — Trailokyamohana and the rest — O Sovereign of the gods, abide, in order: nāda, bindu, kalā, Jyeṣṭhā, Raudrī, Vāmā, then Viṣagnī, Dūtarī, and Sarvānandā. Nāda and bindu are partless; kalā [Kālī] is of the nature of will; Jyeṣṭhā is knowledge; the rest are activity. Thus the cakra is threefold. It is a form of *kāmakalā*; its deepest nature is expansion.

### Pratibha Commentary

The verses execute a deliberate reversal that overturns the diagram's apparent hierarchy: the deities and powers are now enumerated *from the outer square inward* (the order of resorption, *saṃhārakrama*), and the result is the doctrine of inverted omnipresence. The peaceful, highest energy (*śāntā*, consciousness) was just placed at the center — yet here, counted by the goddesses inhabiting them, the highest powers are found pervading the *outermost* cakras, and the dynamic energy of *action* (*kriyā*) is concentrated at the *center*. This inversion is the point: the supreme is not "up there" or "in there" exclusively but saturates every level, so that the periphery holds the peace of the source and the core holds the heat of activity. The whole figure is then declared a form of *kāmakalā* — the diagrammatic glyph of the desire-union of Śiva and Śakti — and its "deepest nature is *prasāra*," expansion. Two strong claims follow. First, the cosmos is not a neutral structure but an *erotic* one: it exists because two poles desire and unite, and the diagram is that union made visible. Second, the cakra's truth is *verb*, not noun — its essence is *expanding*, the live act of pouring forth, not a static arrangement of parts. Existentially, this dismantles the spiritual cliché that depth means withdrawal to a still inner point: here the same divinity is wholly present in the outermost, busiest ring, and its nature is generous overflow.

### Key Terms

**kāmakalā (कामकला)** — *kāma* ("desire, eros") + *kalā* ("part, art, the creative digit"). The glyph/principle of the desiring union of Śiva and Śakti that both *is* and *causes* the emanation of the cosmos. To call the cakra *kāmakalārūpa* is to say the universe is structured as consummated desire — not metaphorically but metaphysically.

**saṃhārakrama (संहारक्रम)** — "the sequence of resorption." Enumerating the cakra from periphery to center follows the *return* movement (world → source); the reverse of *sṛṣṭikrama* (emanation). The order in which one *counts* the diagram enacts a spiritual direction.

**icchā / jñāna / kriyā (इच्छा / ज्ञान / क्रिया)** — will / knowledge / action: the three fundamental *śakti*s of Śiva. Here distributed across the cakra (will, knowledge, activity), so the whole figure is the triadic energy of the deity. They are not faculties of a subject but the cosmos-generating powers themselves.

**prasāra (प्रसार)** — "spreading out, expansion." Named as the cakra's *paramārtha* (deepest reality): the diagram's essence is the *act* of expanding, not the static pattern — being, here, is fundamentally verbal.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Tantric *yāmala* iconography and the Ardhanārīśvara motif (Śiva-Śakti as inseparable union):** Both encode the conviction that reality originates in a bipolar erotic union rather than a single principle, and that this union is generative of the world.
*Divergence:* Ardhanārīśvara fuses the two into one *body*; *kāmakalā* keeps the polarity *dynamic and diagrammatic* (a glyph of ongoing union-as-expansion), emphasizing the *act* of desiring union over a finished androgyne.

**Plotinus, *Enneads* (the One's self-sufficient "overflow" that generates all things without diminution):** Both ground manifestation in a surplus that *expands* outward (the YH's *prasāra*), reality being the pouring-forth of a fullness.
*Divergence:* Plotinus's overflow is explicitly *non-erotic* and impersonal — the One neither desires nor unites with another; the YH makes the generative principle *kāma*, desire between two poles, so creation is consummation rather than effortless emanation.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Find the busiest, most "peripheral" part of your day — a crowded task, a noisy place. Instead of seeking depth by withdrawing from it, look for the supreme *in* it: silently affirm that the same peace you'd seek in stillness saturates this outer ring too. Let one ordinary act of activity be felt as the expansion (*prasāra*) of a fullness, not a distraction from it.

## Interiorizing the Diagram: The Cakra in the Subtle Body
**Source:** Yoginīhṛdaya 1.25–27, 35 (*Cakrasaṃketa*).

In akula, in what is called viṣu, in energy and in fire, then in [the cakra of] the navel, in the anāhata, the [vi]ṣuddha, the uvula, the forehead, in the moon and the half [moon], in rodhinī, nāda, nādānta, and śakti, then in vyāpikā and in the domain of samanā and unmanā, then in the mahābindu, one must meditate in this way the [śrī]cakra in a threefold manner. Up to the ājñā [cakra, the meditation] is said to be with parts (sakala), then up to unmanā it is with and without parts (sakalaniṣkala). On the supreme level, it is without parts (niṣkala). It is [therefore] threefold. […] Higher still, [there is] the Supremely Great, transcending the limitations of time or space, of innate beauty, overflowing with supreme bliss.

### Pratibha Translation

In *akula*, in what is called *viṣu*, in energy and in fire; then at the navel, the heart [anāhata], the throat [viśuddha], the uvula, the brow; in the moon and half-moon, in *rodhinī*, *nāda*, *nādānta*, and *śakti*; then in *vyāpikā* and the range of *samanā* and *unmanā*; and then in the *mahābindu* — thus one is to contemplate the cakra in three modes. Up to *ājñā* the meditation is "with parts" [*sakala*]; thence to *unmanā*, "with-and-without parts"; at the supreme level, "partless" [*niṣkala*]. So it is threefold. […] Higher still is the Supremely Great — unbounded by space or time, beautiful by its own nature, awhirl with supreme bliss.

### Pratibha Commentary

The decisive turn here is *internalization*: the śrīcakra, hitherto drawn and analyzed as an external diagram, is now to be re-found within the practitioner's own subtle body, its nine parts distributed along the *suṣumnā* from the base lotus (*akula*) to the *mahābindu* above the head. The contested claim is the identity of cosmos, diagram, and body — the same nine-fold structure is at once the universe's emanation, the figure on the altar, and the meditator's inner axis; to "build" the cakra in worship and to "build" oneself in yoga are one operation. The passage then grades the ascent by a subtle phenomenology of dissolution: meditation "with parts" (*sakala*) up to the brow, where forms and images still hold; "with-and-without parts" (*sakalaniṣkala*) through the increasingly tenuous phonic *kalās* of the seed-mantra HRĪṂ (*nāda*, *nādānta*, *śakti*, *vyāpikā*, *samanā*); and finally "partless" (*niṣkala*) at the summit. The vanishingly small time-fractions assigned to the upper *kalās* (a sixteenth, a sixty-fourth of a mora) are, as the commentary concedes, "entirely theoretical" — their function is not measurement but the evocation of a subtlety beyond duration. The terminus is not a higher *form* but the formless "Supremely Great" (*paramaṃ mahat*), *deśakālānavacchinna*: cut off from neither place nor time, self-beautiful, "awhirl with supreme bliss." Existentially, the path it sketches is a controlled de-thickening of attention — from image, to sound, to the silence past sound — ending not in blankness but in an unbounded, self-delighting plenitude.

### Key Terms

**sakala / niṣkala (सकल / निष्कल)** — *sa-kala* ("with parts/portions") vs. *niṣ-kala* ("without parts"). The two poles of contemplative form: the divine grasped *with* image and differentiation, and grasped *as* partless absolute, with *sakalaniṣkala* (both-and) between. Maps the ascent from form-bound to formless awareness.

**suṣumnā / akula / mahābindu (सुषुम्ना / अकुल / महाबिन्दु)** — the central subtle channel; *akula*, the base lotus ("not-*kula*," the ground beneath differentiated reality); *mahābindu*, the "great point" at the summit. The body's vertical axis along which the cakra is interiorized and ascended.

**kalā [phonic] (कला)** — here the subtle phonic "portions" of the *bīja* HRĪṂ (bindu, *nāda*, *nādānta*, *śakti*, *vyāpikā*, *samanā*, *unmanā*): the progressively subtler resonances into which a seed-syllable dissolves as sound returns to silence. Worship of the mantra is thus an *acoustic ascent*.

**deśakālānavacchinna (देशकालानवच्छिन्न)** — *deśa* ("space") + *kāla* ("time") + *an-avacchinna* ("un-delimited, un-cut-off"). The supreme is "unbounded by space and time" — not located, not timed; the goal is explicitly *acosmic* even as it is reached *through* the embodied cakra.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances

**Haṭha/Tantric kuṇḍalinī yoga (the ascent of energy through the cakras of the suṣumnā):** Both interiorize a cosmic map as a bodily axis and stage liberation as an upward movement from base to crown.
*Divergence:* Standard cakra-yoga uses a relatively fixed six-or-seven-cakra scheme; the YH installs *its own nine-fold śrīcakra* in the body and grades the ascent by the *phonic kalās of HRĪṂ*, fusing diagram-worship with subtle-body yoga in a way generic kuṇḍalinī schemes do not.

**The Pseudo-Dionysian / Christian apophatic ascent (the soul rising through affirmations to the "brilliant darkness" beyond all names):** Both prescribe a graded reduction from image-laden (*kataphatic / sakala*) to imageless (*apophatic / niṣkala*) contemplation, culminating beyond form.
*Divergence:* The Dionysian summit is a *darkness* and *unknowing* before a transcendent God; the YH's summit is *nisargasundara* and *parānandavighūrṇita* — self-beautiful and whirling with bliss — luminous plenitude rather than dazzling negation.

**Patañjali, *Yoga Sūtra* I.17 (*samprajñāta* samādhi with its grades, toward *asamprajñāta* without object):** Both describe a ladder of contemplation from object-supported ("with parts") to object-less ("without parts").
*Divergence:* Patañjali's terminus is the isolation (*kaivalya*) of pure consciousness *from* nature; the YH's terminus is *fusion* with a blissful, world-pervading Goddess — partlessness as supreme intimacy, not as separation.

### Practice (Abhyasa)

Sit upright and silently intone a single syllable (use "hrīṃ," or simply "mmm"). Follow it as it fades: first the vowel, then the humming nasal, then the bare vibration, then the silence after. Each time it dissolves, do not restart immediately — rest one breath in the partless quiet "above" the sound before sounding it again.

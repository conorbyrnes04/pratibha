# Pratibha — Milarepa Songs (Wave B)
**Anchor:** Evans-Wentz 1928 / Dawa-Samdup

## Contented Would I Be in This Solitude
**Source:** Milarepa, *Tibet's Great Yogi Milarepa*, Chapter X, Song on Yogic Satisfactions (MIL_SATIS_009; Evans-Wentz 1928 / Dawa-Samdup)

My putrid flesh sucked by the flies,
My dissolving muscles eaten by the worms—
Could thus I die, amid this Solitude,
Contented would I be, I the devotee.

“ With no human foot-print by my door,
With no mark of blood within [the Cave] !—
Could thus I die, amid this Solitude,
Contented would I be, I the devotee.

“ With none to crowd about my corpse [or bier],
With none to lament o’er my death—
Could thus I die, amid this Solitude,
Contented would I be, I the devotee.

With none to ask where I had gone,
And with no place which one might point to as my goal—
Could thus I die, amid this Solitude,
Contented would I be, I the devotee.

### Original
*Tibetan not line-aligned in this pass.*

### Pratibha Translation
If my putrid flesh were sucked by flies and my dissolving muscles eaten by worms — could I die thus amid this solitude, contented would I be, I the devotee. With no human footprint by my door, no blood-mark in the cave — contented would I be. With none to crowd about my corpse, none to lament my death — contented would I be. With none to ask where I had gone, and no place to point to as my goal — could I die thus amid this solitude, contented would I be, I the devotee.

### Pratibha Commentary
Liberation here is not postponement of death but the end of bargaining about conditions. Milarepa's refrain refuses the usual spiritual insurance policy — witnesses, rites, a known destination — and treats solitary death as already acceptable. The counterintuitive move is to locate contentment inside exposure: flies, worms, unmarked cave. Existentially: notice what funeral-audience you still require before you will release control.

### Key Terms
**dben pa (solitude)** — mountain solitude as laboratory, not scenery.

**contentment (bde ba / chog shes)** — here, willingness to die without social completion.

**devotee (sgrub pa po)** — practitioner whose practice includes unfinished death.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Plato, *Phaedo* 64a–67b:** Both treat death-readiness as a measure of practice, not morbidity.
*Divergence:* Socrates frames philosophical separation of soul; Milarepa frames yogic solitude without leftover audience.

**Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* 4.48:** Both normalize unmarked ending.
*Divergence:* Stoic composure is civic-inner; Milarepa's is cave-ascetic and anti-fame.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Imagine dying tonight with no one told. Name the one residual wish for witness. Hold it for three breaths without fixing it.

## Essence Is Purity of Faith
**Source:** Milarepa, *Tibet's Great Yogi Milarepa*, Chapter X, Song on Interdependence (MIL_INTER_010; Evans-Wentz 1928 / Dawa-Samdup)

In the Sacred Guru's grace,
And the active meditation of the zealous shishya,
Lieth opportunity to uphold the Truth [the Hierarchy] ;
Of this, the essence is the Purity of Faith.!

In the Rites Initiatory, which confer and bless with Occult
Power,
And in the prayer, earnest and sincere [of the devotee],
Lieth opportunity of meeting speedily [Spiritual Com-
munion] ;
Of this, the essence is the Benediction.”

 Lord Dorje-Chang, O Thou the Immutable,
The weal and woe of this mendicant Thou knowest.

### Original
*Tibetan not line-aligned in this pass.*

### Pratibha Translation
In the sacred guru's grace and the zealous disciple's active meditation lies opportunity to uphold the Truth; of this, the essence is purity of faith. In initiatory rites that confer blessing-power, and in earnest prayer, lies opportunity of speedy spiritual communion; of this, the essence is benediction. Lord Vajradhara, Immutable One — this mendicant's weal and woe Thou knowest.

### Pratibha Commentary
Interdependence here is not abstract emptiness-talk; it is a two-pole circuit: guru-grace and disciple-zeal. Neither pole alone 'upholds the Truth.' The contested claim is that *essence* of the circuit is purity of faith (*dad pa*), not technique display. Initiation and prayer likewise collapse to blessing only when sincerity is intact. Existentially: audit whether your practice still tries to run on one pole — self-effort without receptivity, or devotion without work.

### Key Terms
**bla ma'i byin rlabs** — guru's grace/blessing as enabling field.

**dad pa** — faith/purity of trust as essence, not accessory.

**byin rlabs / benediction** — blessing-power that seals communion.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Rāmānuja / bhakti:** Both refuse technique without relational trust.
*Divergence:* Milarepa embeds faith in mahāmudrā solitude; Vaiṣṇava bhakti embeds it in temple-relation.

**Christian grace/works tension (Augustine):** Both deny solo self-salvation.
*Divergence:* Augustine's frame is sin/justification; Milarepa's is guru-disciple continuum in practice.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Today, do one practice-act that is pure effort, then one that is pure receptivity. Notice which one you secretly prefer.

## Where Liars Are Prized
**Source:** Milarepa, *Tibet's Great Yogi Milarepa*, Chapter X, Song to the Maidens (MIL_MAIDEN_011; Evans-Wentz 1928 / Dawa-Samdup)

In the Palaces Celestial of the Gahdan Gods,!
Truths Spiritual are prized not, but Truths Scientific are ;
In the Regions Lower, in the Naga’s Palaced City,
Truths Profound and Deep are prized not, but Riches are ;?
In this World of Human Beings,
The Wise and Learned are not prized, but Liars are.

In the Provinces of U and Tsang and the Four Districts,
Meditation is not prized, but Exposition is ;
In the dreg-like remainder of these evil times [of Darkness],
Good men are not prized, but the wicked are.

### Original
*Tibetan not line-aligned in this pass.*

### Pratibha Translation
In the celestial palaces of the gods of Tuṣita, spiritual truths are not prized — scientific truths are. In the nāga realms below, profound truths are not prized — riches are. In this human world, the wise and learned are not prized — liars are. In Ü, Tsang, and the four districts, meditation is not prized — exposition is. In these dreg-like remainder times of darkness, good men are not prized — the wicked are.

### Pratibha Commentary
Milarepa maps value-inversion across realms: each domain has a preferred currency that displaces truth. The human punchline is sharpest — liars prized over the wise — and Tibet's own religious culture is not exempt: exposition over meditation. The contested move is to treat market preference as diagnosis, not fate. Existentially: name the currency your circle pays for, and what it quietly demotes.

### Key Terms
**exposition (bshad)** — talk about Dharma displacing practice.

**meditation (sgom)** — the demoted good in late times.

**snyigs ma'i dus** — degenerate/dreg-age as moral climate, not calendar fatalism.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Laozi, *Dao De Jing* 18–20:** Both diagnose cultures that reward the wrong virtues.
*Divergence:* Laozi withdraws into uncarved simplicity; Milarepa stays a singing critic inside the market.

**Plato, *Republic* 8–9 (timocracy to tyranny):** Both track cascading value-degradation.
*Divergence:* Plato offers constitutional typology; Milarepa offers realm-by-realm satire ending in practice.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
List three things your community applauds. Mark which one most often replaces quiet integrity. Refuse one applause-bid today.

## Eschew Worldliness — Come to Lapchi-Kang
**Source:** Milarepa, *Tibet's Great Yogi Milarepa*, Chapter X, Song on Worldliness (MIL_WORLD_012; Evans-Wentz 1928 / Dawa-Samdup)

But these are worldly things, and I’ve eschewed them,
And, by my thus eschewing worldliness, my Sun of Happi-
ness shineth gloriously.
Likewise, do thou, O Peta, all worldliness eschew,
And come to meditate in Lapchi-Kang:
Let us together go to Lapchi-Kang, to meditate.

### Original
*Tibetan not line-aligned in this pass.*

### Pratibha Translation
But these are worldly things, and I have eschewed them; and by thus eschewing worldliness, my sun of happiness shines gloriously. Likewise, Peta, eschew all worldliness and come meditate at Lapchi-Kang: let us go together to Lapchi-Kang to meditate.

### Pratibha Commentary
Happiness is not postponed to better conditions; it is the *result* of eschewing worldliness. The invitation to Peta is not moral scolding alone — it is companionship in practice at a named place (Lapchi-Kang). The contested claim: shared solitude can be love's highest form. Existentially: ask whether your care for someone still keeps them in the market you yourself have left.

### Key Terms
**'jig rten** — worldliness; the abandoned currency.

**Lapchi-Kang** — practice place; geography as vow.

**bde ba'i nyi ma** — sun of happiness arising from renunciation, not acquisition.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Gospel calling of disciples (Mark 1:16–20):** Both invite leaving nets for a shared path.
*Divergence:* Gospel call is to follow Jesus in mission; Milarepa calls a sister into mountain meditation.

**Buddha's going forth (pravrajyā):** Both treat leaving home as luminous, not merely tragic.
*Divergence:* Milarepa's leave includes familial repair-invitation rather than unilateral exit.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Name one 'worldly' thread you still use to hold a loved one close. For one day, care without that thread.

## Not Conventional Shame
**Source:** Milarepa, *Tibet's Great Yogi Milarepa*, Chapter X, Song on What Is Shameful (MIL_SHAME_013; Evans-Wentz 1928 / Dawa-Samdup)

Those hermits who have given up the worldly life
And taken to the practice of the Spiritual Truths,
Found in the Sacred Teachings of the Mystic Path,
They who have vowed to pass their life in meditation,
See not the need to hold to codes of shame conventional.
Therefore, do not, O Peta, seek to add unto thy present
miseries,
But let thine understanding flow within its natural channels.

### Original
*Tibetan not line-aligned in this pass.*

### Pratibha Translation
Those hermits who have given up worldly life and taken to practicing the spiritual truths of the mystic path — who have vowed to pass their life in meditation — see no need to hold to codes of conventional shame. Therefore, Peta, do not add to your present miseries; let your understanding flow in its natural channels.

### Pratibha Commentary
Milarepa distinguishes *conventional shame* (social costume) from ethical clarity. The hermit's vow does not abolish conscience; it refuses second-hand humiliation as a spiritual metric. Peta's misery is worsened when she tries to force him back into respectability. Existentially: separate the shame that protects integrity from the shame that polices appearance.

### Key Terms
**ngo tsha** — shame; here conventional vs path-aligned.

**natural channels (rang bzhin)** — understanding flowing without forced respectability.

**sgom pa'i dam bca'** — meditation vow that reorders social codes.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Zhuangzi, 'breaking the pot' / mourning rites critique:** Both loosen ritualized social feeling.
*Divergence:* Zhuangzi plays with cosmic transformation; Milarepa protects a sister from added misery.

**Paul, 1 Cor 1:27–28 (foolishness of God):** Both invert prestige metrics.
*Divergence:* Paul centers cross-scandal; Milarepa centers meditation vow against family respectability.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Catch one flash of social shame today. Ask: does this protect truth, or only image? Act from the answer once.

## Illness Which Becomes Me So Well
**Source:** Milarepa, *Tibet's Great Yogi Milarepa*, Chapter XII, Discourse/Hymn on Illness (MIL_ILLNESS_014; Evans-Wentz 1928 / Dawa-Samdup)

The Round of Birth and Death and the Deliverance are seen
[or understood] within the Realm of the Clear Light ;
When the hands attain their natural posture,!
The Great Mudré upon them placeth its seal.?
Thus is there [in me] greatness of indifference,
And courage knowing not impediment.

 Diseases, evil spirits, sins, and obscurations,
But tend to beautify me greatly ;
They lie within me, shaped as nerves, as humours, and as seed.
Gifts I use to ornament the signs of my perfection ;
May the sins of evil thoughts be expiated.®
This illness, which becometh me so well,
I could transfer, but no need is there to do so.

### Original
*Tibetan not line-aligned in this pass.*

### Pratibha Translation
The round of birth and death and deliverance are seen within the realm of Clear Light; when the hands attain their natural posture, Mahāmudrā places its seal upon them. Thus there is in me a greatness of indifference, and courage that knows no impediment. Diseases, evil spirits, sins, and obscurations only beautify me; they lie within me as nerves, humours, and seed. Gifts I use to ornament the signs of perfection — may the sins of evil thoughts be expiated. This illness, which becomes me so well, I could transfer — but there is no need to do so.

### Pratibha Commentary
Poisoned and dying, Milarepa refuses the usual spiritual emergency response — exorcism, humour-balancing, transfer of karma onto the enemy. Illness is reclassified as adornment under Clear Light / Mahāmudrā. The contested claim is not masochism; it is non-transfer: he *could* move the illness and will not. Existentially: when harm arrives, notice the reflex to export it. What would 'no need to transfer' mean for one hour?

### Key Terms
**'od gsal** — Clear Light; the field in which saṃsāra/nirvāṇa are seen together.

**phyag rgya chen po** — Mahāmudrā; seal of natural posture.

**illness as adornment** — adversity incorporated without revenge-transfer.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Rūmī, *Mathnawī* VI (*die before you die*):** Both convert threat into transformative occasion.
*Divergence:* Rūmī urges chosen ego-death; Milarepa receives poison without retaliatory magic.

**Job (refusal of friends' explanations):** Both reject tidy theodicies of illness.
*Divergence:* Job protests toward God; Milarepa rests in Clear Light and declines transfer.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
When irritation rises, ask once: am I trying to transfer this? Keep it for ten breaths without exporting blame.

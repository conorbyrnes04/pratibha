## The One Necessary Thing Is Detachment
**Source:** Meister Eckhart, *Von Abegescheidenheit* (§1 — opening)

Ich hân der geschrift vil gelesen, beidiu von den heidenischen meistern und von den wîssagen und von der alten und niuwen ê, und hân mit ernste und mit ganzem vlîze gesuochet, welhiu diu hœhste und diu beste tugent sî, dâ mite der mensche sich ze got allermeist und aller næhest gevüegen müge und mit der der mensche von gnâden werden müge, daz got ist von natûre, und dâ mite der mensche aller glîchest stande dem bilde, als er in gote was, in dem zwischen im und gote kein underscheit was, ê daz got die crêatûre geschuof. Und sô ich alle die geschrift durchgründe, als verre mîn vernunft erziugen und bekennen mac, sô envinde ich niht anders, wan daz lûteriu abegescheidenheit ob allen dingen sî, wan alle tugende hânt etwaz ûfsehennes ûf die crêatûre, sô stât abegescheidenheit ledic aller crêatûren. Dar umbe sprach unser herre ze Marthâ: 'unum est necessarium', daz ist als vil gesprochen: Marthâ, wer unbetrüebet und lûter welle sîn, der muoz haben einez, daz ist abegescheidenheit.

### IAST

### Pratibha Translation
I have read many writings — of the pagan masters and of the prophets, of the old covenant and the new — and I have sought with earnestness and whole diligence which is the highest and best virtue by which a human being can join most nearly to God, and by which one may become by grace what God is by nature, and stand most like the image one was in God when there was no distinction between oneself and God before creatures were made. And as far as reason can search and know, I find nothing other than this: pure detachment [*lûteriu abegescheidenheit*] stands above all things. For every virtue still looks somehow toward creatures, while detachment stands free of all creatures. That is why our Lord said to Martha: *unum est necessarium* — which means: Martha, whoever would be untroubled and pure must have one thing, and that is detachment.

### Pratibha Commentary
Detachment is not one virtue among others but the condition that empties the field so that God alone can fill it. Eckhart's contested move is to rank *abegescheidenheit* above the whole catalogue of Christian excellences — not because love, humility, or mercy are false, but because each still orients toward creatures (the neighbor to help, the lowliness to perform, the suffering to endure). Detachment alone is *ledic aller crêatûren*: free of creatures as such. The citation of Luke 10:42 relocates Martha's "one necessary thing" from busy service to a single ontological posture. The goal named at the opening is radical: to become by grace what God is by nature, and to stand again in the pre-creational image where no *underscheit* (distinction) obtained between the soul and God. For the practitioner this means the spiritual life is not primarily the accumulation of worthy acts but the clearing of the looking-toward that keeps the heart busy with what is not God.

### Key Terms
**abegescheidenheit (MHG)** — *abe* ("off, away") + *scheiden* ("to separate, cut") + abstract noun. Not mere emotional coolness or Stoic indifference; a standing free of creatures so that nothing created occupies the receptive place. Default "detachment" underplays how near this word sits to *niht* (nothingness).

**lûter (MHG)** — "pure, clear, unmixed." Qualifies detachment as undiluted; impure detachment would still smuggle a creaturely looking-toward.

**underscheit (MHG)** — "distinction, difference." The pre-creational image is defined by the *absence* of distinction between the soul and God — the metaphysical target of the treatise.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Aṣṭāvakra Gītā 1.4 / 15.1 (detachment as condition of recognition):** Both treat release from object-clinging as the condition for standing as what one already is, not as a new acquisition.
*Divergence:* Aṣṭāvakra's release opens onto the Self as awareness; Eckhart's opens onto union with the God who alone can occupy a heart emptied of creatures.

**Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam sūtra 5 (contraction into the small self):** Both map a fall from undivided ground into creaturely looking; Eckhart's cure is emptying that looking.
*Divergence:* Kṣemarāja frames contraction as Consciousness's free play reclaimable by recognition; Eckhart frames creaturely looking as what must be vacated for grace.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Today, notice three moments when attention leans toward a creature — a person, outcome, or status — as if that lean were necessary. For each, pause one breath and ask: what remains if I do not look toward it? Rest there without forcing an answer.


## Detachment Compels God to Love Me
**Source:** Meister Eckhart, *Von Abegescheidenheit* (§2 — above love)

Die lêrære lobent die minne grœzlîche, als sant Paulus tuot, der sprichet: 'in waz üebunge ich mac gestân, enhân ich niht minne, sô enbin ich nihtes niht'. Sô lobe ich abegescheidenheit vür alle minne. Von êrste dar umbe, wan daz beste, daz an der minne ist, daz ist, daz si mich twinget, daz ich got minne, sô twinget abegescheidenheit got, daz er mich minne. Nû ist vil edellîcher daz ich twinge got ze mir, dan daz ich mich twinge ze gote. Und ist daz dâ von, wan got kan sich învüeclîcher vüegen ze mir und baz vereinigen mit mir, dan ich mich künde vereinigen mit got. Daz abegescheidenheit twinge got ze mir, daz bewære ich dâ mite: wan ein ieglich dinc ist gerne an sîner natiurlîchen eigen stat. Nû ist gotes natiurlîchiu eigen stat einicheit und lûterkeit, daz kumet von abegescheidenheit. Dâ von muoz got von nôt sich selber geben einem abegescheidenen herzen. Ze dem andern mâle lobe ich abegescheidenheit vür minne, wan minne twinget mich dar zuo, daz ich alliu dinc lîde durch got, sô bringet mich abgescheidenheit dar zuo, daz ich nihtes enpfenclich bin wan gotes. Nû ist vil edeler nihtes niht enpfenclich sîn wan gotes, dan alliu dinc lîden durch got, wan in dem lîdenne hât der mensche etwaz ûfsehennes ûf die crêatûre, von der der mensche daz lîden hât, sô stât abegescheidenheit genzlîche ledic aller crêatûre.

### Pratibha Translation
The teachers praise love greatly, as Saint Paul does when he says that whatever practice I undertake, if I have no love, I am nothing. Yet I praise detachment above all love. First, because the best thing in love is that it compels me to love God, while detachment compels God to love me. Now it is far nobler that I compel God to me than that I compel myself to God — for God can join himself to me more inwardly and unite with me better than I can unite myself with God. That detachment compels God to me I prove thus: every thing gladly rests in its own natural place. God's natural place is unity and purity, and these come from detachment. Therefore God must of necessity give himself to a detached heart. Second, I praise detachment above love because love compels me to suffer all things for God, while detachment brings me to be receptive of nothing except God. Now it is nobler to be receptive of nothing but God than to suffer all things for God — for in suffering a person still looks toward the creature from which the suffering comes, while detachment stands wholly free of creatures.

### Pratibha Commentary
Eckhart reverses the usual hierarchy of charity. Love's excellence is that it moves *me* toward God; detachment's excellence is that it moves *God* toward me — because God's "natural place" is unity and purity, and a detached heart is that place. The contested claim is almost scandalous in a tradition of striving: the highest act is not heroic loving effort but becoming the site where God must give himself. The second argument cuts deeper: even redemptive suffering still keeps a looking-toward (*ûfsehen*) at the creature that wounds. Detachment receives only God because it has drawn so near to nothingness that nothing subtle enough to lodge there remains — except God, who alone is simple enough to abide in such emptiness. Existentially: stop measuring spiritual progress by how hard you love or how much you endure; ask whether the heart still has a looking-toward that blocks receptivity.

### Key Terms
**minne (MHG)** — "love," including caritas. Eckhart does not deny Paul's ranking; he ranks a different excellence *above* love's best feature.

**enpfenclich (MHG)** — "receptive, capable of receiving." Detachment's perfection is receptivity narrowed to God alone — not numbness, but selective openness.

**natiurlîchiu eigen stat (MHG)** — "natural proper place." Cosmological image: as fire rises and stone falls, God rests in unity/purity; detachment *is* that place in the heart.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Plotinus, Enneads V.1 / VI.9 (the One as source that the soul returns to by stripping):** Both treat approach to the Absolute as a clearing rather than an addition.
*Divergence:* Plotinus's stripping is intellectual ascent toward the One; Eckhart's is a heart-posture that *compels* God's self-gift by ontological fit.

**Vijñāna Bhairava (yuktis of emptying / resting as the gap):** Both prize a state free of object-grip so the Absolute can disclose itself.
*Divergence:* VBT's techniques are often momentary doorways in perception; Eckhart's detachment is a standing virtue meant to become the soul's abiding form.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
For ten minutes, drop every effort to 'love God better.' Instead, become available: sit as an empty place. Each time a looking-toward arises (outcome, image, self-story), release it and return to receptivity alone.


## Remaining Within Is Nobler Than Going Out
**Source:** Meister Eckhart, *Von Abegescheidenheit* (§3 — above humility)

Die meister lobent ouch dêmüeticheit vür vil ander tugende. Aber ich lobe abegescheidenheit vür alle dêmüeticheit, und ist daz dar umbe, wan dêmüeticheit mac gestân âne abegescheidenheit, sô enmac volkomeniu abegescheidenheit niht gestân âne volkomene dêmüeticheit, wan volkomeniu dêmüeticheit gât ûf ein vernihten sîn selbes. Nû rüeret abegescheidenheit alsô nâhe dem nihte, daz zwischen volkomener abegescheidenheit und dem nihte kein dinc gesîn enmac. Dâ von enmac volkomeniu abegescheidenheit niht gesîn âne dêmüeticheit. Nû ist alle zît zwô tugende bezzer dan einiu. Diu ander sache ist, war umbe ich lobe abegescheidenheit vür dêmüeticheit, wan volkomeniu dêmüeticheit ist sich selber neigende under alle crêatûre, und in dúr neigunge sô gât der mensche ûz im selber ûf die crêatûre, sô blîbet abegescheidenheit in ir selber. Nû enmac kein ûzganc niemer sô edel werden, daz inneblîben ensî vil edeler in im selber. Dâ von sprach der wîssage Dâvît: 'omnis gloria eius filiae regis ab intus', daz ist gesprochen: 'des küniges tohter hât alle ir êre von ir inwendicheit'. Volkomeniu abegescheidenheit enhât kein ûfsehen ûf keine neigunge under keine crêatûre noch über keine crêatûre; si enwil weder under noch obe sîn, si wil alsô stân von ir selber, niemanne ze liebe noch ze leide, und enwil weder glîcheit noch unglîcheit mit keiner crêatûre haben noch diz noch daz: si enwil niht anders wan sîn. Daz si aber welle diz oder daz sîn, des enwil si niht. Wan swer wil diz oder daz sîn, der wil etwaz sîn, sô enwil abegescheidenheit nihtes niht sîn. Dâ von stânt alliu dinc von ir unbeswæret.

### Pratibha Translation
The masters also praise humility above many other virtues. But I praise detachment above all humility — because humility can stand without detachment, while perfect detachment cannot stand without perfect humility, since perfect humility aims at an annihilation of oneself. Now detachment touches so near to nothingness that nothing can stand between perfect detachment and nothingness. Therefore perfect detachment cannot be without humility. And two virtues are always better than one. The second reason I praise detachment above humility: perfect humility bows itself under all creatures, and in that bowing a person goes out of oneself toward creatures; but detachment remains within itself. Now no going-out can ever become so noble that remaining-within is not far nobler in itself. So the prophet David said: all the glory of the king's daughter is from within. Perfect detachment has no looking up or down under or over any creature; it will be neither under nor over; it will stand from itself, for no one's joy or sorrow, and will have neither equality nor inequality with any creature, neither this nor that: it wills nothing other than to be. That it should be this or that — that it does not will. Whoever wills to be this or that wills to be something; but detachment wills to be nothing at all. Therefore all things stand unburdened by it.

### Pratibha Commentary
Humility's classical Christian form still *moves toward creatures* — bowing under them. Eckhart grants humility's necessity (detachment cannot be perfect without the self-annihilation humility names) yet insists the nobler act is *inneblîben*: remaining within. The contested metaphysics is here: spiritual excellence is not relational positioning (higher/lower than others) but a refusal of all such coordinates. Detachment wills neither this nor that; it wills only *to be* — which, paradoxically, means willing to be nothing (*nihtes niht*). To want a named spiritual identity ("the humble one," "the detached one") already exits the state. Existentially: watch how quickly "being spiritual" becomes a this-or-that you are performing for yourself or others; return to the unburdened standing that claims no rank.

### Key Terms
**dêmüeticheit (MHG)** — "humility, meekness." Valued, yet structurally a going-out; incomplete without the remaining-within of detachment.

**inneblîben / ûzganc (MHG)** — "remaining-within" / "going-out." The treatise's central geometry: every virtue that exits toward creatures is less than the posture that stays.

**nihtes niht (MHG)** — "nothing at all." Detachment's will is not nihilism as despair but the refusal to be a something among somethings.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Zhuangzi, free-and-easy wandering (useless tree / no ranking):** Both prize a stance that does not compete for place among things.
*Divergence:* Zhuangzi's ease often dissolves into natural spontaneity; Eckhart's remaining-within is ordered to God's occupancy of the emptied heart.

**Aṣṭāvakra Gītā 18 (the one who has nothing to do / no status):** Both describe liberation as freedom from being somebody.
*Divergence:* Aṣṭāvakra's somebody is the doer-enjoyer in awareness; Eckhart's somebody is the creaturely self that still claims rank before God and world.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Once today, catch yourself arranging an identity — humble, wise, wounded, advanced. Silently drop the label. Stand for one minute without being this or that. Notice whether the world feels less heavy when you claim no rank in it.


## Do Not Name the Detachment — Let God Speak Inward
**Source:** Meister Eckhart, *Von Abegescheidenheit* (§4 — Mary and silence)

Nû möhte ein mensche sprechen: nû wâren doch alle tugende volkomenlîche in unser vrouwen, und alsô muoste ouch volkomeniu abegescheidenheit in ir sîn. Ist nû abegescheidenheit hœher dan dêmüeticheit, war umbe ruomte sich danne unser vrouwe ir dêmüeticheit und niht ir abegescheidenheit, dô si sprach: 'quia respexit dominus humilitatem ancillae suae', daz ist: 'er sach ane die dêmüeticheit sîner diernen', - war umbe ensprach si niht: er sach ane die abegescheidenheit sîner diernen? Des antwürte ich alsô und spriche, daz in gote ist abegescheidenheit und dêmüeticheit, als verre wir tugende von gote gesprechen mügen. Nû solt dû wizzen, daz diu minnebære dêmüeticheit got dâ zuo brâhte, daz er sich neigete in menschlîche natûre, und stuont abegescheidenheit unbewegelich in ir selber, dô er mensche wart, als si tete, dô er himelrîche und ertrîche beschuof, als ich dir her nâch sagen wil. Und wan unser herre, dô er mensche werden wolte, unbewegelich stuont an sîner abegescheidenheit, dô weste unser vrouwe wol, daz er des selben ouch von ir begerte und daz er in der sache anesach ir dêmüeticheit und niht ir abegescheidenheit. Dâ von stuont si unbewegelich in ir abegescheidenheit und ruomte sich ir dêmüeticheit und niht ir abegescheidenheit. Und hæte si niuwan gedâht mit einem worte ir abegescheidenheit, daz si gesprochen hæte: er sach ane mîne abegescheidenheit, dâ mite wære diu abegescheidenheit betrüebet worden und wære niht ganz noch volkomen gewesen, wan dâ wære ein ûzganc geschehen. Sô enmac kein ûzganc sô kleine gesîn, in dem diu abegescheidenheit müge âne mâsen blîben. Und alsô hâst dû die sache, war umbe sich unser vrouwe ruomte ir dêmüeticheit und niht ir abegescheidenheit. Dâ von sprach der wîssage: 'audiam, quid loquatur in me dominus deus', daz ist gesprochen: 'ich wil' swîgen und wil 'hœren, waz mîn got und mîn herre in mich rede', als ob er spræche: wil got ze mir reden, sô kome her in mich, ich enwil niht hin ûz.

### Pratibha Translation
Someone might say: all virtues were perfect in Our Lady, so perfect detachment must have been in her. If detachment is higher than humility, why did she praise her humility and not her detachment when she said, 'He has regarded the humility of his handmaid'? Why not: he has regarded the detachment of his handmaid? I answer: detachment and humility are in God, so far as we may speak of virtues in God. Know this: loving humility brought God to incline into human nature, while detachment stood immovable in itself when he became human — as it stood when he created heaven and earth. And because our Lord, wishing to become human, stood immovable in his detachment, Our Lady knew well that he desired the same of her, and that in this matter he regarded her humility, not her detachment. So she stood immovable in her detachment and praised her humility, not her detachment. Had she so much as thought with one word of her detachment — 'he regarded my detachment' — that detachment would have been troubled and would no longer have been whole and perfect, for a going-out would have occurred. No going-out can be so small that detachment remains unblemished in it. So you have the reason she praised humility and not detachment. Therefore the prophet said: I will be silent and hear what my God and Lord may speak *in* me — as if to say: if God would speak to me, let him come in here; I will not go out.

### Pratibha Commentary
The Marian puzzle teaches a discipline of silence about the highest state. Humility can be *named* because naming it is congruent with its outward bow; detachment cannot be named without performing the very going-out that ruins it. Mary's wisdom is double: she keeps detachment immovable while offering humility as the speakable surface God regards for the Incarnation's work. The prophet's line seals the practice: do not go out to fetch God; be silent so God can speak *in*. The contested pastoral implication is sharp: talking about one's detachment, advertising one's emptiness, or making a spiritual brand of non-attachment is already the fall. Existentially: the deepest work may be the one you refuse to narrate.

### Key Terms
**unbewegelich (MHG)** — "immovable." God's and Mary's detachment share this quality; motion toward creatures (even verbal) is the threat.

**ûzganc (MHG)** — "going-out." Even a word of self-reference about detachment counts as exit.

### Pratibha Translation
Now you may ask what detachment is, since it is so noble in itself. Know this: true detachment is nothing other than for the spirit to stand as immovable against whatever may befall it of joy and sorrow, honor, shame, and disgrace, as a mountain of lead stands immovable before a little wind. This immovable detachment brings a person into the greatest equality with God. For that God is God, he has from his immovable detachment; and from detachment he has his purity, his simplicity, and his unchangeability. Therefore if a human being is to become equal with God — so far as a creature can have equality with God — it must happen through detachment. Detachment then draws one into purity, and from purity into simplicity, and from simplicity into unchangeability, and these produce an equality between God and the human being; and that equality must come about in grace, for grace draws one from all temporal things and purifies from all transient things. ### Pratibha Commentary
Here Eckhart defines the virtue by a physics of immovability. The lead mountain does not refuse the wind by fighting it; it is simply too dense to be swayed. Equality with God is not moral imitation but participation in God's own detachment — the purity, simplicity, and unchangeability by which God *is* God. The ladder (purity → simplicity → unchangeability) is a phenomenology of emptying, and Eckhart insists the equality is *in grace*, not self-manufacture. The final maxim is the treatise in one line: creature-full equals God-empty; creature-empty equals God-full. The contested pastoral reading of Stoic calm is refused: this is not indifference as armor but vacancy as hospitality. Existentially: inventory what currently "fills" the heart; each occupancy is a displacement of God.

### Key Terms
**blîgîn berc (MHG)** — "lead mountain." Image of density against trivial force; detachment's strength is mass of emptiness, not willpower clenched.

**einvalticheit (MHG)** — "simplicity, single-foldedness." Not naivety; undividedness — the opposite of a heart split among creatures.

**lære sîn / vol sîn (MHG)** — "to be empty" / "to be full." Zero-sum occupancy: creatures and God do not cohabit as equals in the same receptive place.

**Īśāvāsya Upaniṣad 1 (the Lord's filling / renunciation):** Both couple fullness of the Absolute with a releasing of grasping.
*Divergence:* The Īśā holds enjoyment-with-renunciation in the world; Eckhart's maxim is starker about mutual exclusion in the heart's occupancy.

**Plotinus VI.9 (union by stripping to simplicity):** Both ascend by simplification toward the unchangeable.
*Divergence:* Plotinus's One is beyond being and intellect; Eckhart's God remains the personal giver who occupies the emptied heart by grace.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Evening review: list what occupied your heart today (people, fears, ambitions, screens). For each, say quietly: this filled me; therefore God was crowded out. Then sit two minutes empty — not pushing contents away violently, just not feeding them — and notice the different density.

### Pratibha Translation
Know that God has stood in this immovable detachment from everlasting and still stands. When God created heaven and earth and all creatures, that affected his immovable detachment as little as if no creature had ever been made. Further: all the prayer and good works a person can perform in time move God's detachment as little as if no prayer or good work ever happened in time — and God is never the gentler or more inclined toward someone than if that prayer or work had never been done. Further still: when the Son in the Godhead wished to become human and became human and suffered, that affected God's immovable detachment as little as if he had never become human. You might say: then all prayer and good works are wasted, since God is not moved by them — and yet people say God wants to be asked for everything. Understand, if you can: in his first eternal glance God saw all things as they would happen — when and how he would create, when the Son would become human and suffer, the least prayer and work anyone would do, and which devotion he would hear. He saw that tomorrow you will call on him earnestly; he will not hear that prayer *tomorrow*, for he has heard it in his eternity before you ever became human. If your prayer lacks earnestness, he does not refuse you *now*; he has refused you in his eternity. So God works nothing newly; everything is already wrought beforehand. Thus God always stands in immovable detachment — and yet prayer and good works are not lost: whoever does well is well rewarded, whoever does ill is rewarded accordingly. As Augustine says: God forbid anyone should say God loves someone in time, for with him nothing is past or future; he loved all the saints before the world was made. When in time he shows what he has regarded in eternity, people think God has turned a new love toward them; yet when he seems angry or gracious, *we* are changed and *he* remains unchangeable — as sunlight hurts sick eyes and helps healthy ones, while the sunlight itself remains unchangeable.

### Pratibha Commentary
Eckhart protects God's immutability without voiding prayer. The contested synthesis: temporally, nothing you do *begins* a new divine attitude; eternally, your earnest prayer is already included in the first glance that sees all. Prayer is not lever-pulling on a changeable God; it is alignment with what eternity has already regarded. The Augustine sun image relocates all drama of divine mood into the creature's changing receptivity. Existentially: pray earnestly without the superstition that you are updating God; let the practice change *you* into the one who can bear the light.

**êwiger anblik (MHG)** — "eternal glance." God's single regard in which all temporal events are seen at once.

**unwandelbære (MHG)** — "unchangeable." Applied to God; change is predicated of us.

**vorgewürket (MHG)** — "already wrought beforehand." Denies novelty in God without denying the moral seriousness of acts.

**Bhagavad Gītā 9–11 (the cosmic form / acts within an already-complete divine field):** Both place human action inside a totality that is not surprised by time.
*Divergence:* The Gītā keeps a personal Lord who reciprocates devotion within līlā; Eckhart stresses that reciprocity never implies temporal change in God.

### Pratibha Translation
Someone might ask: did Christ also have immovable detachment when he said, 'My soul is sorrowful unto death,' and Mary, standing under the cross — of whom so much lament is told? How can this stand with immovable detachment? Know that the masters say there are two persons in every human being: the outer person, which is sensibility, served by the five senses, yet working by the soul's power; and the inner person, which is one's inwardness. A spiritual person who loves God uses the soul's powers in the outer person no further than the five senses necessarily require; and inwardness turns to the senses only as their guide and guardian, lest they use their objects bestially. Whatever powers the soul has beyond what it gives the five senses, it gives entirely to the inner person. Now know: the outer person may be in activity while the inner person stands wholly free and immovable. Christ also had an outer and an inner person, and so did Our Lady; whatever they said of outer matters, they said according to the outer person, while the inner stood in immovable detachment. So Christ spoke when he said his soul was sorrowful unto death; and whatever Our Lady lamented, her inwardness still stood in immovable detachment. Take a likeness: a door opens and closes on a hinge. I liken the outer board of the door to the outer person, and the hinge to the inner person. When the door opens and closes, the outer board moves back and forth, yet the hinge remains in one place, immovable, and is never altered. Just so is it here, if you can take it rightly.

### Pratibha Commentary
Eckhart refuses the false choice between feeling and detachment. Christ's agony and Mary's lament are real in the outer person; they do not require the inner hinge to swing. The contested pastoral error is equating holiness with numbness, or equating emotion with spiritual failure. The door-hinge image is precise engineering: motion at the periphery is compatible with stillness at the axis. Existentially: let the outer life feel, speak, work, and even weep — while asking whether the inner axis is still trying to become a swinging board.

### Key Terms
**ûzer mensche / inner mensche (MHG)** — "outer person / inner person." Anthropological dual register, not two selves to war against each other but two functions.

**angel (MHG)** — "hinge." The still pivot; detachment's seat.

**sinnelicheit (MHG)** — "sensibility." Legitimate within need; dangerous when it commandeers all soul-power.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Bhagavad Gītā 2 (the wise one acts while established in the Self):** Both allow full outer engagement without inner displacement.
*Divergence:* The Gītā's steadfastness is often framed as buddhi-yoga in action; Eckhart's hinge is explicitly the seat of *abegescheidenheit* amid pathos.

**Spandakārikā (vibration without loss of the still ground):** Both hold movement and ground together.
*Divergence:* Spanda names the Absolute *as* vibratory; Eckhart keeps a sharper outer/inner split for pastoral clarity.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
In the next strong emotion (joy, anger, grief), do not suppress the outer movement. Place attention for thirty seconds on the 'hinge': the awareness that is not identical with the swinging feeling. Whisper: the door moves; the hinge need not.

### Pratibha Translation
I ask here: what is the object of pure detachment? I answer: neither this nor that is the object of pure detachment. It stands upon a bare nothing [*blôzez niht*], and I will tell you why. Pure detachment stands upon the highest. Now that one stands upon the highest in whom God can work according to his whole will. And though God is almighty, he can work only as he finds readiness or makes it. I say 'or makes it' because of Saint Paul: there God found no readiness, yet prepared him by pouring in grace. So God works according to the readiness he finds. His working differs in a human being and in a stone. Likeness from nature: heat a baking oven and put in doughs of oats, barley, rye, and wheat — one heat, yet unequal loaves, and the fault is not the heat's but the matter's. Likewise God does not work equally in all hearts; he works as he finds readiness and receptivity. Wherever a heart holds this or that, something in that this-or-that can keep God from working at the highest. Therefore if the heart is to be ready for the highest, it must stand upon a bare nothing — and therein lies the greatest possibility there can be. Will I write on a wax tablet, nothing already written there — however noble — fails to hinder me; if I would write well, I must erase everything, and the tablet never suits writing so well as when nothing at all stands on it. Just so: if God is to write in my heart at the highest, everything that can be called this and that must leave the heart. So stands the detached heart. Therefore God can work there at the highest and according to his uppermost will. Therefore the object of the detached heart is neither this nor that.

### Pratibha Commentary
The treatise culminates in a metaphysics of readiness. Omnipotence does not cancel the need for a fitting receptacle; God works as readiness allows — or as grace creates readiness (Paul). The oven and wax tablet images make the same point: unequal results are often the matter's density, not the heat's failure. *Blôzez niht* is not despairing void but maximal capacity — the greatest *mügelicheit* (possibility). The contested spiritual consumerism of filling the heart with holy this-and-that (images, techniques, identities) is inverted: the highest object is no object. Existentially: erase one noble inscription you keep polishing — a spiritual self-image, a preferred consolation — and leave the tablet blank for an hour.

### Key Terms
**blôzez niht (MHG)** — "bare / naked nothing." The objectless ground of detachment; not absence as lack but absence as capacity.

**bereitschaft / enpfenclicheit (MHG)** — "readiness / receptivity." Conditions of divine working in the heart.

**diz noch daz (MHG)** — "neither this nor that." Refusal of every determinate content as the heart's occupying object.

### Cross-Tradition Resonances
**Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad / Gauḍapāda (turīya beyond waking-dream-sleep contents):** Both point beyond all determinate states to a ground that is not another content.
*Divergence:* Turīya is articulated within an Indic fourfold; Eckhart's bare nothing is the Christian heart cleared for God's writing.

**Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam (Citi resting as herself):** Both end in a state where the Absolute is not an object among objects.
*Divergence:* Recognition reclaims authorship of appearance; Eckhart's blank tablet awaits God's inscription by grace.

### Practice (Abhyasa)
Choose one cherished mental inscription (a plan, grievance, spiritual identity). For fifteen minutes, each time it appears, mentally wipe the tablet clean. Do not replace it with a better inscription. End by sitting one minute with nothing written.
